i.The problem

A community needed a home of its own.

Sistahz In Systems, a community for women in AI and technology, needed a private home where members could post, learn and connect under the community’s own name.

  • ✕Posting and discussion
  • ✕Learning and courses
  • ✕Events and recordings
  • ✕Members finding each other
  • ✕All under the community’s own name

Needed: one private homeBuilt: one private home

ii.Process first

Don’t reinvent the wheel. Make it hers.

I had already built a community platform for an earlier project. Rather than reinvent the wheel, I started from that proven foundation and made it Sistahz’s: its own brand, its own hosting and domain, and permission rules locked down in the database before real members joined.

  1. DiscoverySet the spec: what to keep, the brand, and what launch needed
  2. BuildRebranded every screen from the crest’s colors: navy, violet, teal and gold
  3. TestingLocally, on staging and on the live domain, including live attempts to break the rules
  4. LaunchOctober 17, 2026, with new features the same day

iii.Then the tool

Then the tool: everything members need, in one place.

I adapted my platform for Sistahz from foundation to launch: rebranded screen by screen, moved onto its own hosting and domain, and secured so the rules that protect members are enforced in the database, not the browser.

iv.Where it runs

Tested on the live site before anyone joined.

  • The sign-in page is public at app.sistahzinsystems.com. The community behind it is members-only.
  • Every member workflow was tried on the live domain before members were invited. 35 of 35 browser workflows and 26 of 26 database permission tests passed.
  • Every attempt to change protected data was blocked, 12 out of 12.
  • All test accounts and test content were deleted before launch.
  • On launch day, at the team’s request: post types, topic categories, polls, questions, an emoji picker and new admin tools.
