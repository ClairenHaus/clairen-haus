Common questions

Answers from the firm.

Drawn from the firm's current site.

What is probate?

From the Latin term meaning “to prove,” probate is the process established by state law to prove the validity of a will and carry out its provisions for transferring assets after death. A will is administered by a personal representative appointed by the court.

What are non-probate assets?

Assets that transfer at death separately from a will, such as property owned jointly with rights of survivorship, accounts with “payable on death” designations, and assets with a designated beneficiary like life insurance, retirement accounts and pension plans.

What happens if someone in Indiana dies without a will?

When an individual dies without a valid will, trust or beneficiary designation, assets pass to heirs under the laws of intestate succession. Those laws direct specific shares to spouses and children and may differ from what the person intended; without a will or trust, assets could be distributed to a child at age 18.

What is a durable power of attorney?

A power of attorney names a person you trust to act for you in financial matters. A “durable” power of attorney is not affected by later incapacity, and it can be effective immediately or only if you become incapacitated.
