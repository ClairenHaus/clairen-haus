Probate & Trust Administration

Administering probate estates and trusts, large and small, and handling probate and trust litigation when disputes arise.

How the firm handles it

Charles Lillie has administered both large and small probate estates and trusts and has handled probate and trust litigation.

Probate administration

When someone dies owning property in their own name, a probate estate may need to be opened in the probate court. The personal representative gathers and values the assets, gives notice to creditors, pays valid debts, expenses and taxes, and distributes what remains to the people entitled to it. Each step has its own requirements and deadlines, and a personal representative is responsible for getting them right.

Trust administration

A trust avoids probate for the assets it holds, but it does not run itself. When a trust becomes irrevocable, the successor trustee takes on duties to the beneficiaries: managing the assets, keeping them informed, accounting for what was done, handling tax matters, and distributing the trust as its terms require.

Probate and trust litigation

Sometimes an estate or trust ends up in dispute: over whether a document is valid, how it should be read, or whether a personal representative or trustee is meeting their duties. Mr. Lillie’s practice includes probate and trust litigation as well as administration.

This page explains the area in general terms. It is not legal advice; every situation is different, and the attorneys can tell you how it applies to yours.
