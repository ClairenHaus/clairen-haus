Alternative dispute resolution

Resolve the dispute without handing control to someone else.

Some disputes must be litigated. Many, however, can be resolved through an alternative dispute resolution process designed to minimize fallout and offer benefits litigation cannot.

The better you understand your dispute resolution options, the better you can manage your circumstances. We explain the available options, assess whether alternative dispute resolution is appropriate for your case, and help you make a well-informed decision.

What ADR can offer

  • A clear definition of the genesis of the dispute
  • A thorough assessment of the parties’ underlying interests
  • A creative analysis of the short- and long-term benefits you can obtain and confer
  • Potentially, a negotiated resolution of all open issues that is flexible, durable and, if you elect, completely confidential

Rather than relinquishing control to a judge, well-informed parties take control of the process and manage their conflict with the help of a trained, skilled neutral, such as a mediator. Both Mark Carta and Darcy McAlister are trained, certified mediators.

Commercial mediation

As a commercial trial attorney, Mark Carta is experienced in a broad range of disputes. He has served as Co-Chair of the Fairfield County Bar Association’s ADR Section, is on the ADR Center’s panel of approved mediators and arbitrators, and has mediated disputes as a Special Master in Connecticut federal and state courts since 1990. He has mediated many fiduciary disputes between family members and disputes between attorneys.

Family mediation

Darcy McAlister has assisted the matrimonial judges of Connecticut’s Judiciary as a Special Master, serving as an objective guide and sounding board for matrimonial litigants and their lawyers. See Divorce & Family Law Alternatives.
