Executive compensation

Negotiate your compensation on terms that reflect your true value.

Our lawyers have many years’ experience reviewing executive compensation agreements of all types, and have negotiated hundreds of executive contracts.

Executive employment agreements

The best time to negotiate the most favorable employment terms is when you are being hired. We review your agreement and help you negotiate base salary and benefits, sign-on bonus, performance-based bonuses, equity awards such as RSUs, and other incentives. We think through often-overlooked issues with you: your severance rights, what happens if there is a change in control, and your right to resign for “good reason.” If you are being awarded equity, you will want a clear understanding of how your interests vest and your anti-dilution rights. We also counsel on post-employment issues such as covenants not to compete, non-solicitation prohibitions and non-disparagement clauses.

Executive severance packages

If you are facing the possibility of losing your position, you will want a knowledgeable advocate on your side. We carefully vet your employer’s proposed severance agreement, alert you to its pitfalls, and propose practical revisions that bring balance to these often one-sided agreements.

Equity compensation and stock options

If you are founding a new business or joining a start-up, your equity share is likely to be the most significant part of your compensation: stock grants, stock options, stock appreciation rights and restricted stock units. Understanding the vesting period, exercise price, dilution rights and other key provisions shows what you can expect as your “real” compensation. We can also help you negotiate favorable option terms, such as a shorter vesting period or a lower exercise price.

Cash incentive bonuses

Long- and short-term cash incentive plans reward employees for meeting performance objectives. Experience has shown that these plans work best when the objectives are reasonable, detailed and mutual. We help you define and negotiate a plan with clearly defined metrics and stepped incentives.

Deferred compensation, phantom stock and change in control

  • Deferred compensation. Agreements must be carefully drafted to comply with Section 409A of the Internal Revenue Code; we prepare or review them and recommend the changes needed to protect your interests.
  • Phantom stock rights. Many of the benefits of stock ownership without a stock grant; we can suggest when they are appropriate.
  • Change in control agreements. A single- or double-trigger provision can activate your severance, accelerate vesting and reduce the reach of post-employment covenants.
