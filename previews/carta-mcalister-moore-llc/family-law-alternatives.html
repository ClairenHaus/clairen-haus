Alternatives to a litigated divorce

Litigation, mediation, collaborative divorce, or limited help: choose the right process.

Mediation and collaborative divorce are designed to let the parties work cooperatively toward an amicable resolution, without the need for judicial intervention.

Traditional, litigated divorce

Like other civil litigation, the traditional process is adversarial and may conclude in judicial orders. Its hallmark is ongoing oversight and intervention by the court: both parties have access to interim orders, can seek penalties if either acts against those orders, and work within clear structure, concrete steps and specific time frames. When the parties cannot reach an accord, we work diligently to seek a judgment in your favor by advancing your interests, arguing the legal issues and advocating your position before the court.

Alternative dispute resolution

For ADR to succeed, both parties need to be committed to the process and to resolving their differences outside the courtroom. Where the parties cannot agree on a particular issue, they may choose to jointly retain an independent expert rather than engage in a battle of the experts. No guarantee can be made that an alternative process will cost less than litigation, but it is our belief that a cooperative approach yields better long-term results, with agreements that are more comprehensive, personalized and enduring.

Mediation

In mediation, the parties work with one neutral professional. As mediator, we act as facilitator and professional resource: in three-way sessions we explore the topics to be addressed, identify areas of agreement, narrow the issues and suggest potential resolutions, with referrals to financial, valuation or mental health professionals when needed. Once an agreement is reached, we draft the documents, review them with you, and help you adopt the agreement as a court order in an uncontested divorce. Mediation styles range from evaluative to purely facilitative, so consider a mediator’s approach carefully before engaging one.

Collaborative divorce

Each party retains independent, collaboratively trained counsel to resolve family matters outside the court system. Unlike mediation, each party has personal counsel throughout; unlike litigation, the attorneys commit to working cooperatively, using problem-solving techniques in four-way meetings. A central element is the written agreement that if either side goes to court, both attorneys withdraw and both parties must retain new counsel. Darcy McAlister has served as co-chair of the Collaborative Attorneys of Fairfield County.

Limited representation

  • Review counsel. An independent, objective assessment of proposed settlement terms for parties in mediation: the viability of the structure, provisions that may prove problematic, ambiguous terms, alternatives to consider, and whether the terms accurately reflect your intentions.
  • Unbundled services. An à la carte menu for parties negotiating their own divorce: consultation on reasonable settlement parameters, negotiating particular provisions, drafting or reviewing the paperwork, and reviewing the terms so you understand and consent to them.
  • Document preparation. Help preparing the necessary paperwork in an acceptable format, and guidance on what to expect in the formal proceedings.
