Frequently asked questions

Answers to common questions.

Straight answers about the firm, our practice areas, and how to get started. For advice about your own situation, please schedule a consultation.

Where is the firm’s office?

Our office is at 777 Post Road, 2nd Floor, Darien, CT 06820. You can reach us at (203) 202-3100 or info@cmm-law.com.

Which towns do you serve?

We serve clients in Darien, Fairfield, Greenwich, New Canaan, Norwalk, Rowayton, Stamford, Weston, Westport and Wilton, Connecticut.

What areas of law does the firm handle?

Employment disputes, executive compensation, business and corporate law, litigation, commercial dispute resolution, residential real estate, wills, trusts and estates, and divorce and family law, including mediation and collaborative divorce.

Will I work directly with a partner?

Yes. At CMM you work directly with a partner who has focused on a discrete area of law. Rather than trying to be all things to all clients, each of us focuses on one specific area of practice.

How do I get started?

It all starts with a consultation. Call (203) 202-3100 or email info@cmm-law.com to schedule one. Together we will identify the issues, define your goals and develop a strategic plan.

Do you represent employees or employers?

Both. Mark Carta continues to serve as employment counsel for several corporations and non-profit organizations, but now primarily represents individual employees, predominantly C-suite executives and employees in the financial sector.

Can you review a severance agreement or a new employment contract?

Yes. Severance agreements and executive employment agreements are a central part of our employment practice. The best time to negotiate favorable terms is when you are being hired, and we also review and negotiate severance packages when a position is ending.

Does a divorce have to go to court?

Not necessarily. We are prepared to litigate when circumstances demand, but the vast majority of family cases can, and should, be resolved outside the courtroom through mediation or collaborative divorce.

What is the difference between mediation and collaborative divorce?

In mediation, both parties work with one neutral professional. In collaborative divorce, each party has their own collaboratively trained attorney, and everyone works cooperatively in four-way meetings, with an agreement that both attorneys withdraw if either side goes to court.

Can you help if we have already worked out our own divorce terms?

Yes. We act as review counsel for parties in mediation, offer unbundled services for parties negotiating their own divorce, and help prepare the necessary paperwork for parties who have settled matters themselves.

Do you handle residential real estate closings?

Yes. Under Glen Moore’s leadership the firm handles residential closings from simple to complex purchases and sales, mortgage loans for purchases, refinances and home equity lines, title insurance, and property disputes.

Do I need more than a will?

Often only a will is needed, but some estates require more. An estate plan should also include documents for the appointment of health care agents, powers of attorney and conservators, and we can advise on how to title assets and name beneficiaries.

Do you help executors settle an estate?

Yes. We represent executors and administrators throughout the estate settlement process in the local probate courts, and represent trustees, conservators and guardians.

Are there mediators at the firm?

Yes. Both Mark Carta and Darcy McAlister are trained, certified mediators.
