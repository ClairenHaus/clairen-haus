Managing Partner

Mark R. Carta, Esq.

Employment law, executive compensation, business litigation and commercial mediation. Mark’s clients are primarily C-suite executives and financial sector employees, starting a new position or negotiating severance.

Writing is lawyering

Mark spent two years in a New England boarding school that drilled writing skills into its students. Those skills took him to an Ivy League university, made him an Associate Editor of the Law Review in law school, and let him go toe-to-toe in lawsuits against the best law firms in the country.

Problem solving and strategic thinking

A problem solver and strategic thinker by nature, Mark was elected by his peers to head the Litigation Department in the Greenwich office of Whitman Breed Abbott & Morgan, where he spent the first half of his career. Before opening Carta, McAlister & Moore, he served as Head of the Litigation Department and Managing Partner at Rucci Burnham.

Truly effective advocacy

Mark began his legal career clerking for a federal judge who had been a master trial attorney before taking the bench. There he saw the difference between truly effective advocacy and overly zealous representation. He has been trying civil jury and court-side cases ever since. Today his clients are primarily C-suite executives and financial sector employees seeking counsel when starting a new position or in severance negotiations.

He is the author of “Negotiating Your Severance Agreement” (PDF).

Trust is everything

Deciding to embark on a lawsuit has significant financial and emotional consequences. Mark advocates taking that step only as a last resort, and only in the appropriate instances.

Mediation

Mark is a trained, certified mediator. He has served as Co-Chair of the Fairfield County Bar Association’s ADR Section, is on the ADR Center’s panel of approved mediators and arbitrators, and has mediated disputes as a Special Master in Connecticut federal and state courts since 1990.

Part of the community

Mark serves on the Board of the Silvermine Guild of Artists. He was one of the original five founders of A Better Chance of Wilton, a non-profit that provides an educational opportunity to minority high school scholars, served as its President for two years, and has been its legal counsel and a member of its Executive Committee for many years. He has also served Wilton Presbyterian Church in many capacities.

Personally speaking

Mark was raised in Fairfield and has at some point lived in virtually every town in lower Fairfield County. He loves to travel, enjoys cooking and good wine, and is a disciplined, if not particularly fast, swimmer.
