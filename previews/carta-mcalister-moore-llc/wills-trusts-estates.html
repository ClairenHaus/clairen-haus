Wills, trusts & estates

Estate plans that do what you intend, and help when a family needs it most.

This practice covers four areas: drafting wills, estate planning, representing the fiduciaries of decedents’ estates, and representing trustees and other fiduciaries such as conservators and guardians.

Wills and estate planning

We perform a comprehensive analysis of your estate needs:

  • Writing documents intended to take effect at death, which identify the fiduciary you have selected and how your assets will be allocated
  • Preparing documents for use during life, including living trusts, powers of attorney, living wills and healthcare proxies
  • Determining the best way to title assets and name beneficiaries
  • Determining whether lifetime gifting will help you achieve your objectives

Often only a will is needed, but some estates require more. From small estates to large, we prepare carefully drafted documents, and an estate plan should be more than simply a will: we prepare the documents for the appointment of health care agents, powers of attorney and conservators as part of your plan.

Decedents’ trusts and estates

During a time of grief and confusion, you and your family will be treated with the respect and empathy you deserve. The firm’s attorneys have decades of hearing experience before the probate judges of lower Fairfield County, and our lawyers and paralegals know how to move the process along. Timothy Fray represents executors and administrators throughout the estate settlement process in the local probate courts.

Post-mortem planning is another area of particular focus: helping your family save on taxes and ease the ultimate distribution of estate assets, including using trusts funded at one family member’s death to achieve the long-term goals of the survivors.

Conservatorships and guardianships

Tim Fray also frequently serves as court-appointed counsel for individuals in sensitive probate proceedings such as conservatorships, guardianships and involuntary commitments.

Contested matters

Challenges against fiduciaries can arise in the local probate court or in the state court system. Mark Carta handles these situations, from complex cases to routine disagreements. See Litigation.
