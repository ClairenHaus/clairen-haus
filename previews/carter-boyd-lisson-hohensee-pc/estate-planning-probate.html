Estate Planning & Probate

Planning ahead, settling an estate, and resolving disputes when they arise.

At this firm: estate planning and probate is the first practice area on the list, and five of the six attorneys work in it. James A. Carter has been certified in Estate Planning & Probate Law by the Texas Board of Legal Specialization since 1977.

Estate planning

An estate plan sets out who receives what you own, who manages it, and who makes decisions for you if you can’t. It usually starts with a will, and may include one or more trusts, along with the beneficiary designations and titles that pass property outside a will. Because a plan touches taxes as well as family, it helps when the people drafting it understand both.

Trusts

A trust holds property for the benefit of others under terms you set. Trusts can be used to manage property over time, to provide for a family member, or to plan for taxes. The firm frequently represents clients regarding trusts.

Probate

Probate is the court process that proves a will, appoints the person who will settle the estate, and oversees paying debts and passing property to the people entitled to it. In Texas, probate matters are heard in the county where the person lived.

Estate litigation

When a will is challenged, or heirs, beneficiaries and executors disagree, the dispute may end up in court. Jeffrey S. Lisson’s practice includes probate litigation, and the firm frequently represents clients in estate litigation.

Planning that involves a business or significant taxes also touches Taxation and Business Formation & Representation.
