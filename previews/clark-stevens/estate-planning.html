Estate Planning in Hilton Head Island

Wills, trusts, powers of attorney and living wills under South Carolina law.

Clark & Stevens, P.A. helps Hilton Head Island families put a plan in place: wills and trusts, durable powers of attorney, and living wills, along with guidance for personal representatives working through probate.

Planning ahead

A will directs who receives your property, names who administers your estate (so that person need not post a surety bond), and, for parents of minor children, names a guardian and someone to manage the children’s inheritance. A durable power of attorney and a living will round out a plan by naming who acts for you and what care you want if you cannot speak for yourself.

Probate

Whether an estate needs a formal or informal probate, personal representatives often work with an attorney to minimize their liability and speed up distribution to survivors.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I need a will?

Even without substantial assets, a will matters. Without one, the state distributes your property under its intestacy statutes, which may not match your wishes and cannot direct specific items of personal property to specific people. Dying without a will can also make settling the estate more difficult and expensive, and if you have minor children a will is critical for naming who should have custody and manage their inheritance.

What is probate?

Probate is the legal process that establishes ownership of property after a person dies. Some property does not require probate (for example, real estate owned in joint tenancy), but probate is required to confirm a will’s validity and to distribute property not covered by a will. Many personal representatives hire an attorney to help with their duties, limit their liability and speed distribution to survivors.

I already have an estate plan. Does it need updating?

Estate plans should be reviewed periodically as your situation changes. Marriage, divorce, the death of a beneficiary, or the birth or adoption of a child call for further planning, as do changes in tax law, your finances or your wishes.

What is a living will?

South Carolina’s Death With Dignity Act lets adults state their wishes for end-of-life care. By signing a Declaration of Desire for Natural Death (a “living will”), you specify which life-sustaining procedures you want provided or withheld if you become terminally ill or permanently unconscious, and you may appoint a trusted agent with authority to revoke or enforce the declaration.
