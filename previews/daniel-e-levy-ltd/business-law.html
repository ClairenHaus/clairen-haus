Incorporations

The firm discusses each client’s business goals and often recommends forming a corporation to limit personal liability. It routinely incorporates business entities, preparing the Articles of Incorporation, the application for a Federal Employer Identification Number, shareholder and director resolutions, bylaws, stock certificates and the S Corporation election.

Limited liability companies

Often a limited liability company is the better fit. The firm prepares the Articles of Organization and the Operating Agreement for this form of entity.

Contracts

Business clients often ask the firm to prepare contracts that protect their interests. The firm routinely prepares buy/sell agreements, employment contracts, restrictive covenants and confidentiality agreements.

Business planning

Before forming a business, the firm discusses the forms of ownership with the client: partnership, corporation, limited liability company and sole proprietorship. It then recommends a meeting with the client’s accountant to discuss the tax consequences and benefits of each. Once the client understands the pros and cons and is fully informed of the choices, the firm prepares the documents that best serve the client’s needs and goals.

In plain terms

Some general background

General information about how these matters usually work in Illinois, not legal advice about your situation.

Corporation or LLC?

Both can shield owners from personal liability for the business’s debts. They differ in formalities, ownership structure and how they can be taxed, which is why the tax conversation with your accountant comes before the filing.

What gets filed

In Illinois, a corporation is formed by filing Articles of Incorporation with the Secretary of State, and an LLC by filing Articles of Organization. The bylaws or operating agreement, resolutions and ownership records are the internal documents that make the company run.

Why a buy/sell agreement

A buy/sell agreement decides in advance what happens to an owner’s share if an owner leaves, retires, becomes disabled or dies, so the business and the remaining owners are not left guessing.

Questions about business law

Should my business be a corporation or an LLC?

Before forming anything, the firm discusses the forms of ownership (partnership, corporation, limited liability company, sole proprietorship) and recommends a meeting with your accountant on the tax side. Once you understand the pros and cons, the firm prepares the documents that fit your goals.

What business contracts does the firm prepare?

Buy/sell agreements, employment contracts, restrictive covenants and confidentiality agreements, prepared to protect the business’s interests.
