Wills

As part of its estate planning practice, the firm routinely prepares simple wills. A simple will names an executor of the estate and a guardian for minor children, and states the testator’s wishes for how assets are distributed. When a review of a client’s financial plan shows a living trust is recommended, the firm prepares a pour-over will that designates the client’s living trust as the beneficiary of the estate.

Trusts

A revocable living trust is often recommended to avoid both estate tax and the probate of assets. The firm prepares revocable living trusts and counsels clients on how to fund the trust once it is created.

Probate of a decedent’s estate

When a client or relative passes away, the firm counsels the family on the ownership of the decedent’s assets, how to transfer those assets to the beneficiaries, and how and when to pay the decedent’s final bills. Depending on many factors, a probate estate may need to be opened. The firm has probated estates in Cook County, Lake County and DuPage County, and is here to help in a time of grief.

In plain terms

Some general background

General information about how these matters usually work in Illinois, not legal advice about your situation.

Will or trust?

A will takes effect at death and usually passes through probate. A revocable living trust holds assets during your lifetime and can pass them to your beneficiaries without probate, as long as the assets were actually transferred into the trust.

What “funding” a trust means

Funding is retitling assets, such as a home or accounts, into the name of the trust. An unfunded trust is just paper; the pour-over will is the safety net that sends anything left outside the trust into it.

What probate is

Probate is the court-supervised process of gathering a decedent’s assets, paying final bills and distributing what remains. Whether an estate needs to be opened depends on how the assets were owned and other factors, which is the first thing to sort out with an attorney.

Questions about estate planning & probate

What is the difference between a simple will and a pour-over will?

A simple will names an executor of the estate and a guardian for minor children, and states how the testator wants assets distributed. When a living trust is recommended, the firm prepares a pour-over will that names the client’s living trust as the beneficiary of the estate.

Do you prepare revocable living trusts?

Yes. A revocable living trust is often recommended to avoid both estate tax and the probate of assets. The firm prepares the trust and counsels clients on how to fund it once it is created.

A family member has passed away. Can you help with probate?

Yes. The firm counsels the family on who owns the decedent’s assets, how to transfer them to the beneficiaries, and how and when to pay final bills. Depending on many factors, a probate estate may need to be opened; the firm has probated estates in Cook, Lake and DuPage counties.
