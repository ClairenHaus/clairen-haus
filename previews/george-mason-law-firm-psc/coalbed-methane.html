Coalbed Methane

Counsel on coalbed methane (CBM) contracts, development and sale, from a lawyer involved in CBM operations since the first commercial well in Dickenson County, Virginia.

Involved since the first commercial well

George Mason has been involved in coalbed methane operations since 1988, when EQT Production Co. drilled the first commercial CBM well in Dickenson County, Virginia. His knowledge of oil, gas and coalbed methane legislation, the model for which he helped author, makes him a valuable resource in the field.

The firm advises companies and individuals on contracts and strategies for the acquisition, development and sale of coalbed methane.

What coalbed methane is

Many coal seams contain natural gas, either within the seam itself or in the surrounding rock. Historically this methane was treated as a nuisance and a safety hazard in coal mines, and it was vented into the atmosphere. Today it is produced as an unconventional source of natural gas: it can be injected into pipelines for resale, used as an industrial feedstock, or used for heating and electricity generation.

Why CBM raises legal questions

Because the gas sits in or around the coal, coalbed methane brings together coal rights and oil and gas rights, which are often owned by different people. Leases, deeds and legislation written before CBM was commercially produced may not address it directly, which is why contracts and title work deserve careful attention.
