Civil & Administrative Appeals

Appeals are won on the record and in the brief. The firm takes appeals to the Connecticut Appellate and Supreme Courts, and appeals to the Superior Court from zoning, wetlands and state agency decisions.

How appeals generally work in Connecticut

An appeal from a Superior Court judgment generally goes to the Connecticut Appellate Court; the Connecticut Supreme Court hears some appeals directly and reviews others after the Appellate Court decides. The appeal is decided on the record of what happened in the trial court and on the parties’ written briefs and oral argument. New evidence is generally not allowed, which is why issues have to be preserved at trial.

Deadlines are short

In most civil cases, an appeal must be filed within 20 days of notice of the judgment, and appeals from local land use decisions are generally due within 15 days of the published notice of the decision. If you are considering an appeal, call right away.

Administrative appeals

Decisions of state agencies are generally appealed to the Superior Court under Connecticut’s Uniform Administrative Procedure Act. The court reviews the agency’s record and decision rather than hearing the case anew, so the record made before the agency matters a great deal.
