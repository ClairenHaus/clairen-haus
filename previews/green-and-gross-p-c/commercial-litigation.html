How a commercial case generally moves in Connecticut

Most business disputes in Connecticut are heard in the Superior Court, in the judicial district where the case belongs; some, such as cases between parties from different states or cases under federal law, are heard in the United States District Court for the District of Connecticut. A case typically moves through pleadings, discovery (the exchange of documents and testimony), motions, and then settlement talks or trial.

Many civil cases never reach a jury. The Superior Court refers some cases to an attorney fact finder or arbitrator, and parties often agree to mediation or private arbitration. Knowing how each of those paths works, and when to push for trial, is part of the strategy from the first meeting.

Disputes between co-owners

Disagreements among the members of an LLC or the shareholders of a small corporation are often governed first by the company’s own operating agreement or bylaws. Bringing those documents to your first meeting helps the attorney see your options quickly.
