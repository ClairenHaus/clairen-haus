Eric M. Gross

A founding member of the firm and its president since 2003, Eric Gross has represented individuals and businesses in every phase of civil litigation for more than forty-five years.

Phone (203) 335-5141Email egross@gglaw.netFax (203) 367-9964

Practice

Mr. Gross is an experienced civil litigator. He represents individuals and business organizations in all phases of civil litigation, including business litigation, disputes among members of closely held entities, complex real estate litigation, personal injury cases and probate litigation.

He has been selected, both privately and by court appointment, to serve as an arbitrator in commercial and real estate disputes, and he is an attorney state trial referee and a fact finder/arbitrator for the Judicial District of Fairfield, a role he has held since 1993.

Representative matters

Among his cases, Mr. Gross obtained a judgment, including interest, in excess of $1,000,000 in federal court in Hudson Pak Establishment v. Shelter for the Homeless, Inc., et al. (No. 91CV00468 (AWT)), which was affirmed by the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit (No. 05-22112).

Service to the profession and community

Mr. Gross is a sustaining member of the Bridgeport Bar Association, a member of the Connecticut Bar Association's Commercial Law and Bankruptcy Section, and a member of the Connecticut Trial Lawyers Association. He served on the Bar Examining Committee for the State of Connecticut for more than a decade.

He was vice-chairman of the Trumbull Ethics Commission from 2002 to 2016, is a past member of the Trumbull Town Council, and served as a member of the United States Commission on Civil Rights. He is a past president of the Jewish Center for Community Services and of the Probus Club of Greater Bridgeport, and has served on the Board of Trustees of Temple B’nai Israel in Bridgeport.

Background

Mr. Gross was born in New York City and raised in Fairfield, Connecticut, and lives with his wife in Trumbull. He earned a B.A. in history from Middlebury College in 1969 and his J.D. from the University of Connecticut School of Law in 1972.
