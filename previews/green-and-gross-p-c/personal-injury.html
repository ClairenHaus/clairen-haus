Personal Injury Litigation

If you were hurt because someone else was careless, the firm’s personal injury attorneys work to obtain compensation for you, in cases such as automobile accidents and unsafe property.

How we help

Our personal injury attorneys represent people injured by the negligence of others, for example in automobile accidents and premises liability cases (injuries caused by unsafe conditions on someone else’s property). Eric M. Gross has handled numerous personal injury cases for plaintiffs over a civil litigation career of more than forty-five years.

  • Automobile accidents
  • Premises liability: falls and injuries on unsafe property
  • Other injuries caused by negligence

How a claim generally works in Connecticut

A negligence claim asks whether someone failed to use reasonable care and whether that failure caused your injury. Compensation can include medical bills, lost wages, and pain and suffering. Many claims settle with an insurance company; others are filed in the Superior Court.

Connecticut follows a comparative negligence rule: if you were partly at fault, your recovery is generally reduced by your share of the fault, and you generally cannot recover if your share is greater than the combined share of the people you are suing.

Time limits

Most Connecticut negligence claims must generally be filed within two years of the injury, and some claims involving a town or the state require written notice much sooner. Talk with an attorney early, before evidence is lost.

What to bring to your first meeting

  • Any accident or police report
  • Photos of the scene and your injuries
  • Names and contact details of witnesses
  • Medical records and bills
  • Letters from insurance companies
