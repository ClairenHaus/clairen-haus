Probate & Estate Settlement

When a family member dies, someone has to gather what they owned, pay what they owed and pass on the rest. The firm guides executors and families through the Connecticut Probate Courts.

How probate generally works in Connecticut

Connecticut estates are handled by the Probate Court for the district where the person lived. If there is a will, the court admits it and appoints the executor named in it; if not, the court appoints an administrator and Connecticut law decides who inherits. The fiduciary then gathers and values the assets, gives notice to creditors, pays valid debts and taxes, and accounts to the court and the beneficiaries before distributing what remains.

Some smaller estates can use a simpler procedure, and some assets, such as jointly owned property or accounts with a named beneficiary, pass outside probate altogether. An early meeting helps sort out which steps your family actually needs.

Serving as executor?

An executor is personally responsible for handling the estate properly. Bring the will, a list of known assets and debts, and any recent tax returns to your first meeting.
