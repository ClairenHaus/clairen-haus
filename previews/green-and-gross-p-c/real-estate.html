Commercial & Residential Real Estate

What a closing generally involves

In Connecticut, attorneys commonly take part in real estate closings. Before the closing, the purchase agreement sets the price, deadlines and conditions such as financing and inspections. A title search checks for liens, easements and other issues, and title insurance protects the buyer and lender. At closing, the deed and loan documents are signed, funds change hands, and the deed and mortgage are recorded on the town land records.

Commercial property

Commercial transactions add their own questions: zoning and permitted uses, environmental conditions, existing leases and tenants, and the terms of commercial financing. The firm’s real estate and land use attorneys work side by side, which helps when a property’s value depends on what the zoning allows.
