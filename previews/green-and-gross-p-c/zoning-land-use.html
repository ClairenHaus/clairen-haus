Zoning & Land Use Law

Whether you are seeking an approval or opposing a project next door, the firm’s land use attorneys have deep experience before Connecticut’s municipal land use agencies and in the courts.

How we help

Our land use attorneys appear before planning and zoning commissions, zoning boards of appeals, inland wetlands commissions, historic district commissions and harbor management commissions throughout Fairfield and New Haven Counties. We frequently represent individuals and neighborhood groups in opposing development applications, and we also help property owners win approval of their own.

  • Special permits and site plan approvals
  • Zone changes and zoning text amendments
  • Subdivisions

  • Affordable housing applications
  • Appeals of land use decisions to the Superior Court and beyond

How the local process generally works

In Connecticut, each town’s land use agencies apply that town’s regulations. Many applications, such as special permits, zone changes and variances, require a public hearing, where the applicant presents the proposal and neighbors and the public may speak. The commission then votes and publishes notice of its decision.

What is said and submitted at the hearing becomes the record. If the decision is appealed, the court generally reviews that record rather than hearing new evidence, so expert reports, testimony and objections have to be in the record before the hearing closes.

Appeals move fast

An appeal from a planning and zoning, zoning board of appeals or inland wetlands decision generally must be filed in the Superior Court within 15 days of the published notice of the decision, and the person appealing must show they are aggrieved by it. If a decision affects your property, call promptly.
