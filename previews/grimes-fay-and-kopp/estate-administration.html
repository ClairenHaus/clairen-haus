Estate Administration

Probate, trust administration, guardianship and conservatorship: we help clients through Missouri’s estate administration system with care and experience.

Most people think of an estate only in terms of what happens when someone passes away. Death is the most common reason administration is required, but it can also arise during a person’s lifetime. A minor may need an estate opened after losing a parent, or after receiving funds or assets above a certain amount, and an estate may also be necessary for an adult who can no longer make decisions about their care or finances.

Grimes, Fay & Kopp provides a full range of services to help clients with the many responsibilities of administering an estate. Through decades of experience, our attorneys know Missouri’s estate administration and probate system, and our team helps clients with both modest estates and those with substantial net worth.

Types of administration

Probate estate

Court administration of a deceased person’s assets and debts, and distribution to the rightful beneficiaries under a will or under Missouri’s intestacy laws.

Trust administration

Administering a revocable or irrevocable trust, including the required notices, publications, collections, debt payments and distributions.

Guardianship

A guardian appointed by the court to provide for the care, custody, food, shelter and welfare of an incapacitated or disabled adult or a minor child.

Conservatorship

A conservator appointed by the court to take charge of property and manage the financial affairs of an incapacitated or disabled person.

What to expect

In general, administration involves gathering assets, notifying the people the law requires, paying valid debts, and distributing what remains to the right beneficiaries, with the court’s involvement when the estate is in probate. Your attorney will explain which steps apply to your situation and guide you through each one.
