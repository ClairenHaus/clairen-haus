The people behind the practice

Biographies drawn from the firm’s current attorney pages.

F. Edwin Hallman, Jr.

Attorney

Has practiced environmental law for more than 35 years, representing companies as “potentially responsible parties” at state and federal Superfund sites, on underground storage tanks, and in related litigation.

Before private practice he was an Assistant Attorney General for Georgia and Regional Counsel and Chief Enforcement Attorney for the U.S. Department of Energy, Region 4.

Richard Wingate

Focuses on Georgia environmental regulation (HSRA, the Underground Storage Tank Act, the Solid Waste Management Act) and federal law including RCRA, CERCLA and the Clean Water Act.

About the firm

A boutique built around environmental law.

166 Anderson Street, Suite 210 · Marietta, Georgia 30060

Hallman & Wingate, LLC arose from the former partnership of Decker, Hallman, Barber & Briggs. After working together since 2002, Ed Hallman and Richard Wingate partnered in July 2009, with Richard Decker as of counsel, to establish a boutique firm concentrating on environmental law.

The firm’s attorneys have argued before the Eleventh Circuit, the U.S. Court of Claims, the U.S. Supreme Court, the Georgia Court of Appeals and the Supreme Court of Georgia. The firm works with the U.S. EPA, the Georgia Department of Natural Resources and other agencies, and represents clients across the country and internationally.

Selected cases from the firm’s site
  • Boyce v. Augusta-Richmond County, Superior Court of Richmond County, GeorgiaDescribed by the firm as the first jury verdict in the United States for damages caused by contamination from sewage sludge.
  • McElmurray v. U.S. Dept. of Agriculture, S.D. GeorgiaOverturned the USDA’s denial of prevented-planting credits for thousands of acres of impacted farmland.
  • Hoffman v. Atlanta Gas Light Co., 206 Ga. App. 727 (1992)Clarified the statute of limitations for environmental contamination from petroleum pipelines.
  • Anderson, et al. v. Federal Energy Regulatory Commission, D.C. CircuitRepresented over 40 individual landowners in condemnation proceedings.
