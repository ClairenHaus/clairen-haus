How divorce works in Wisconsin

Wisconsin is a no-fault state: the court does not need to find that either spouse did something wrong, only that the marriage is irretrievably broken. A divorce resolves several connected questions at once: legal custody and physical placement of any children, child support, maintenance (Wisconsin’s term for alimony), and the division of property and debts.

Wisconsin law sets a waiting period before a divorce can be finalized, and many cases settle along the way. When issues cannot be resolved, they are decided by the court at trial.

Legal separation and annulment

A legal separation addresses many of the same issues as a divorce, including custody, support and property, without ending the marriage. An annulment declares that a valid marriage never existed and is available only in limited circumstances. An attorney can explain which option fits your situation.

Choosing your battles

Linda Ivanovic often tells clients that “we must choose our battles.” Some issues, such as who gets the frying pans, are not worth fighting over. Others, such as your children and your financial future, are worth the fight. The firm will advise you through each decision along the way and, if things cannot be resolved, prepare your case for trial.
