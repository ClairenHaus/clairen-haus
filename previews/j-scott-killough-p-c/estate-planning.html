Estate Planning

Wills, trusts, durable powers of attorney and medical powers of attorney, prepared by an attorney who is Board Certified in Estate Planning and Probate Law and a Certified Public Accountant.

What the firm prepares

Estate planning has been the center of Scott Killough’s practice since he opened it in 1990. The firm’s estate planning work includes the use and preparation of:

  • Wills, which say who receives your property and who will handle your estate.
  • Trusts, which hold property for the people you name and set out how a trustee manages it for them.
  • Durable powers of attorney, which name someone you trust to handle financial and legal matters for you, and which continue to work if you later become unable to act for yourself.
  • Medical powers of attorney, which name someone to make health care decisions for you if you cannot make them yourself.

Why it helps to plan

A plan puts your own decisions on paper: who inherits, who is in charge, and who can step in for you during a serious illness. Without one, those questions are answered by state law and, often, by a court. A clear plan also tends to make the work your family faces later simpler and less uncertain.

A tax background behind the plan

Before law school, Scott spent his early career in the Tax Department of Arthur Andersen & Co. in Dallas, where his work included estate and gift tax, corporate and individual income tax, non-profit organizations, real estate, and oil and gas. He is a Certified Public Accountant as well as an attorney, and the firm describes that combination as giving clients a more complete approach to their legal concerns.

He has been Board Certified in Estate Planning and Probate Law by the Texas Board of Legal Specialization since 1993.
