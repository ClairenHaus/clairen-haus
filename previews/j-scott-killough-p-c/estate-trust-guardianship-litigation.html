Estate, Trust & Guardianship Litigation

When a family disagreement over an estate, a trust or a guardianship has to be resolved, in court or at the mediation table.

The kinds of disputes involved

Litigation in this area can arise in many ways: a challenge to a will, a disagreement about how an estate is being administered, questions about a trustee’s decisions or accounting, or a dispute over whether a guardianship is needed and who should serve. These cases are often as personal as they are legal.

Litigation experience

Litigation involving estates, trusts and guardianships is part of Scott Killough’s practice. Earlier in his career, as an associate with Carr, Evans, Fouts & Hunt in Lubbock, he gained experience in probate and tax matters as well as a wide variety of commercial, construction and family law litigation, and he later supported the partners of Pye, Dobbs, Johnson & Berry in Tyler in these same areas.

Mediation as an option

Since 2009, Scott has also been a credentialed mediator for these types of disputes. Many disputes of this kind are resolved through mediation, and understanding both sides of that process is useful to clients. Read more about mediation.

He has been Board Certified in Estate Planning and Probate Law by the Texas Board of Legal Specialization since 1993, and is admitted to practice before the U.S. District Courts for the Northern, Eastern and Western Districts of Texas, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, and the U.S. Tax Court.
