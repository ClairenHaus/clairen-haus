Guardianships

Guardianship administration, including contested guardianships, for families caring for a loved one who can no longer manage on their own.

What a guardianship is

A guardianship is a court-supervised arrangement in which a judge appoints someone to care for a person who cannot fully care for themselves or manage their own affairs, such as an adult with a serious incapacity or a minor. A guardian may be responsible for the person’s care, for their property and finances, or both, and generally reports to the court.

Administering a guardianship

Once appointed, a guardian has ongoing duties to the person in their care and to the court. Knowing what the court expects, and keeping the guardianship in order over time, is a large part of doing the job well.

When a guardianship is contested

Families do not always agree on whether a guardianship is needed or who should serve. The firm’s practice includes contested guardianships, and Scott Killough also serves as a credentialed mediator for these types of disputes.
