What changes

What this redesign fixes.

Two honest observations from the current site, and what this concept does about them.

  • Two pages that say “Information coming soon.”The current Areas of Practice and FAQ pages both carry only that line. The concept fills both from the firm's own About page: nine practice areas with descriptions and a question-and-answer section.
  • Nothing machine-readable about the firmThe current site has no structured data and no sitemap. The concept adds LegalService, Person and FAQ markup with the full name, address, phone and the nine counties served so search engines and AI assistants can read who the firm is.
  • A thin home pageThe current home page is about 250 words and one paragraph. The concept leads with the board certification, the CPA background and the practice list, all on one page.
  • Images that describe themselvesSeven of nine images on the current site lack alt text. The real logo, the board-certification badge and Mr. Killough's photo are all described here.

About the practice

The current site describes a Tyler-based firm concentrating on estate planning, probate and trust administration, business transactions, guardianships, estate and trust litigation, tax planning and real estate. Mr. Killough has over 38 years of experience as a practicing attorney and, before that, several years as a Certified Public Accountant.

He began in 1980 in the tax department of Arthur Andersen & Co. in Dallas, earned his law degree at Texas Tech in 1985, practiced with Carr, Evans, Fouts & Hunt in Lubbock and then Pye, Dobbs, Johnson & Berry in Tyler, and launched his own practice in 1990, becoming Board Certified in Estate Planning and Probate Law in 1993. In 2009 he became a credentialed mediator for these types of disputes.

The firm serves clients in Anderson, Cherokee, Gregg, Henderson, Rusk, Smith, Upshur, Van Zandt and Wood Counties.
