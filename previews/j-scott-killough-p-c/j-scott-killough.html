The attorney

J. Scott Killough

Attorney at Law · Board Certified in Estate Planning and Probate Law · Certified Public Accountant · Credentialed Mediator

J. Scott Killough began practicing law in 1985 and has practiced in Tyler since 1989. He is Board Certified in Estate Planning and Probate Law by the Texas Board of Legal Specialization, is a Credentialed Mediator and is a Certified Public Accountant.

Call (903) 561-8280

From accounting to law

Scott entered the workforce in 1980, joining the Tax Department of Arthur Andersen & Co. in its Dallas office. During his years in accounting he gained experience in a broad range of tax areas, including corporate and individual income tax, estate and gift tax, non-profit organizations, real estate, and oil and gas.

Deciding to pursue law, he moved to Lubbock to attend law school at Texas Tech University, where he received his Doctor of Jurisprudence degree in 1985. During that time he also completed the requirements to practice accounting as a Certified Public Accountant.

Lubbock, then Tyler

Settling in Lubbock, Scott began his legal career that year as an associate with Carr, Evans, Fouts & Hunt, a well-known, medium-sized local law firm. There he gained experience in probate and tax matters, as well as a wide variety of commercial, construction and family law litigation.

Seeking an opportunity to move to East Texas, he moved to Tyler in 1989 and took a position with Pye, Dobbs, Johnson & Berry, where he supported each of the partners in these same areas of law.

His own practice

Launching his solo practice in 1990, Scott began specializing in estate planning and probate, and became Board Certified in Estate Planning and Probate Law in 1993. He continues to practice in estate planning, including wills, trusts, durable powers of attorney and medical powers of attorney; probate and administration of decedents’ estates; trust creation and administration; business transactions, including organization, business purchases, sales and a broad range of contracts; guardianship administration, including contested guardianships; litigation involving estates, trusts and guardianships; tax planning; and real estate transactions.

In 2009, he augmented his practice by becoming a credentialed mediator for these types of disputes.
