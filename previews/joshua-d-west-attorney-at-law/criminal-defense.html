Criminal Defense

Joshua D. West represents people charged with crimes in Michigan, both felonies and misdemeanors.

Being charged with a crime starts a process with deadlines, court dates and decisions that can follow you for years. Knowing how that process works is the first step.

Felonies and misdemeanors

Michigan criminal charges fall into two broad groups. Misdemeanors are less serious offenses and are generally handled from start to finish in district court. Felonies are more serious; they begin in district court and, if the case continues past the preliminary examination, move up to circuit court.

The firm handles both.

How a case usually moves

  1. Arraignment. The charge is read, rights are explained, and bond is set.
  2. Pretrial stage. The defense reviews the evidence, talks with the prosecutor and decides how to respond.
  3. Preliminary examination (felonies). A district court judge decides whether there is enough evidence for the case to go forward.
  4. Motions, plea or trial. Many cases resolve before trial; others are tried to a judge or jury.
  5. Sentencing, if there is a conviction.

Every case is different. This is a general outline, not legal advice about your situation.

Why the court perspective matters

Before private practice, Joshua D. West served as judicial law clerk for Chief Judge William E. Bolle of the 52nd District Court. During his tenure he mediated cases and consulted with and advised the judge. He has handled cases throughout the State of Michigan.

If you have been arrested or charged

  • Keep every paper you were given: the ticket, bond paperwork, any notice to appear.
  • Write down dates, names and what happened while it is fresh.
  • Avoid discussing the facts of your case with anyone but your attorney.
  • Call. The firm keeps a 24-hour support line at (248) 259-5562.
