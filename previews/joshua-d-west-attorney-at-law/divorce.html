Divorce

Divorce, and the family law questions that come with it: property, support, and the children.

A divorce reorganizes a family’s finances and, often, its children’s lives. Good decisions start with understanding how the process works in Michigan.

Divorce in Michigan

Michigan is a no-fault divorce state: a court can grant a divorce when the marriage has broken down and there is no reasonable likelihood it can be preserved. Neither spouse has to prove the other did something wrong.

  • Residency. Generally, one spouse must have lived in Michigan for 180 days, and in the county where the case is filed for 10 days, before filing.
  • Waiting period. A divorce cannot be finalized until at least 60 days after filing, or six months when there are minor children (a court can shorten the six months in some circumstances).

What a divorce decides

  • Division of property and debts
  • Spousal support
  • Child custody
  • Parenting time
  • Child support
  • The home and other major assets

Many divorces resolve by agreement; others need the court to decide some or all of these issues.
