Forming, structuring and buying businesses.

Planning and formation of domestic and foreign businesses, and the agreements that go with buying and selling them.

Choosing the right structure

The way a business is organized affects who owns it, who is liable for its debts, how it is taxed and how ownership can change hands later. Common choices include corporations, limited liability companies, partnerships, sole proprietorships and business trusts.

How the office helps

  • Planning and formation of domestic and foreign businesses.
  • Incorporations and limited liability companies.
  • Stock issuance and transfer.
  • Corporations, sole proprietorships and business trusts.
  • General and limited partnerships.
  • Analyzing, preparing and negotiating asset purchase agreements and related documentation.

Buying or selling a business’s assets

In an asset purchase, the buyer acquires specific assets of a business, such as equipment, inventory, contracts or real estate, rather than the owner’s shares. The asset purchase agreement sets out exactly what is being sold, the price and the promises each side makes.
