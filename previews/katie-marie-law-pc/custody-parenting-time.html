Custody & Parenting Time

Custody and parenting time cases, from first orders to changes after a judgment.

From the firm: Attorney Baluha and her staff are experienced trial lawyers with many years of extensive training. KatieMarieLaw should be your first choice for any type of Custody cases.

Legal custody and physical custody

Michigan law separates legal custody, which is the right to make important decisions about a child such as schooling, health care and religion, from physical custody, which is where the child lives. Either can be shared by both parents (joint) or held by one parent (sole).

Parenting time

Parenting time is the schedule a child spends with each parent. Michigan presumes that it is in a child’s best interests to have a strong relationship with both parents, and a parenting time order can be general (“reasonable parenting time”) or a specific schedule covering weekdays, weekends, holidays and summers.

The best interests of the child

Michigan courts decide custody using the “best interests of the child” factors in the Child Custody Act, which look at things like each parent’s relationship with the child, the stability of the child’s home, and each parent’s willingness to support the child’s relationship with the other parent.

Changing an order

Custody and parenting time orders can be changed after a judgment, but the parent asking must first show a proper cause or a change of circumstances. Where a child has an established custodial environment, the court requires stronger proof before changing it.
