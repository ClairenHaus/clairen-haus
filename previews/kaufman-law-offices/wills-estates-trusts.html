Wills, Estates, Power of Attorney & Trusts

Wills, trusts, powers of attorney and settling estates.

Putting your plans in writing

An estate plan decides who receives your property, who handles your affairs, and who makes decisions for you if you cannot. Kaufman Law Offices prepares wills, trusts and powers of attorney, and helps families with estates.

The documents

  • Will: says who receives your property and names an executor to carry out your wishes. A will can also name a guardian for minor children.
  • Trust: holds property for your beneficiaries under rules you set. Some trusts can help an estate avoid probate or manage money for someone over time.
  • Financial power of attorney: lets a person you choose handle your money and property if you are unable to.
  • Health care power of attorney and living will: name someone to make medical decisions for you and record your wishes about end-of-life care.

Estates

When someone dies, their estate usually goes through the probate court in the county where they lived. The executor or administrator gathers the property, pays debts and taxes, and distributes what remains. Help with the paperwork and deadlines can take a great deal of stress off a family.

Keep it current

Marriage, divorce, a new child or grandchild, a move or a death in the family are all good reasons to review an existing plan.

This page is general information about Ohio law, not legal advice. Every situation is different, and deadlines can have exceptions.
