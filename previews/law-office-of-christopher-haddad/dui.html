DUI defense in Palm Beach County

A DUI charge can be challenged at every step: the stop, the encounter, the testing and the report.

Every DUI case has questions worth asking

Under Florida law, a DUI is usually a misdemeanor, but repeat offenders can face felony charges. To challenge a DUI effectively, many parts of the case have to be examined. Police often know nothing about a driver beforehand, so ordinary traits can be mistaken for signs of impairment.

The stop

  • What reason did police give for pulling you over?
  • Were you seen leaving a bar or restaurant, and were you singled out because of it?
  • How were you driving? Were you keeping your lane or speeding? Did another driver report you? Was there an accident?

These questions go to whether the officer acted reasonably in stopping you, and can be the basis of a pre-trial motion challenging the State’s case.

The encounter and the evidence

  • What did the officer ask, and were you alert, responsive and able to explain yourself?
  • What signs of impairment does the officer claim, and are they in the report?
  • Was a breath or blood test taken? If a breath machine was used, was it properly calibrated and used correctly, and can police produce the paperwork to prove it?
  • What happened at the scene that shows you may not have been impaired, and did the report include it?
  • Could something else explain apparently erratic behavior, such as exposure to other substances or medication?

Bias in a police investigation can sometimes be exposed to the accused person’s advantage. Mr. Haddad has defended more than 100 DUI charges since 1990 and has obtained acquittals using many different defenses.

Representative DUI cases

Paint fumes, not alcohol

The client’s car spun onto a neighbor’s lawn. A toxicologist established that unvented paint fumes from his work as a painter caused him to lose control.

Not guilty

Medical care, not alcohol

The client was rear-ended and charged with DUI and leaving the scene; the breath result was .18. The defense established he was under medical care at the time.

Not guilty

Stress and anxiety

The client, who had recently been diagnosed with breast cancer, was charged after moving into a new home; the breath result was .14. The defense showed stress and anxiety caused the erratic driving.

Not guilty

Parked, not driving

The client was found asleep in her vehicle in a fast-food drive-through lane. The defense established she was parked.

Arrest procedures

The defense established that the officer violated policies and procedures in making the arrest, including how sobriety tests were given.
