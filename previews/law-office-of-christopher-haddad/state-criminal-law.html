Florida state criminal defense

Defense of Florida state charges, from investigation through trial, by a former Palm Beach County assistant public defender.

How a Florida criminal case comes together

In a Florida state criminal case, the party bringing the case is the State of Florida, and the prosecutor is called the State Attorney. Assistant state attorneys handle cases day in and day out. On special occasions, the Attorney General’s Office of Statewide Prosecution handles a case, most often when it involves several counties or jurisdictions.

Investigations are often started by the Florida Department of Law Enforcement (FDLE), a county sheriff or a local police department. They range from simple matters to cases built over months or even years. When an investigation is complete, the agency usually sends a packet to the prosecutor, who weighs questions such as:

  • Is there probable cause that a crime was committed, and by the person charged?
  • Are there witnesses, and are they reliable?
  • Does forensic evidence exist? Was there a confession?

Making sure your voice is heard

While this happens, a savvy defendant makes sure his or her own voice is heard. Police and prosecutors can be so focused on making a case that they lose sight of other important issues: the person’s background, the circumstances of the case, education and work history, and any medical or mental health issues.

Mr. Haddad believes the best defense draws on investigation, forensics, psychology and legal precedent together.

Representative state cases

Second-degree murder with a firearm: Stand Your Ground

A 19-year-old was charged after an armed intruder was shot in the doorway of his family’s home while his mother and siblings hid inside. The firm moved for immunity and dismissal under Florida’s Stand Your Ground law; the State conceded the motion on the record and the court dismissed the homicide charge. The State later filed new charges on a different theory, and the judge found the evidence not strong enough to hold the client without bond.

Homicide charge dismissed

First-degree murder, drive-by shooting

Three teenagers were charged as adults in 2019. For one of them, Mr. Haddad brought in a firearms expert and a ballistics and crime-scene reconstruction expert, whose analysis showed the bullet his client was accused of firing was more likely fired by someone else. The State had offered a 25-year plea.

All charges dropped (nolle prosequi)

Attempted murder of a law enforcement officer

The client was accused of trying to run over an officer with an all-terrain vehicle. After an intensive investigation, a detailed analysis of the eyewitnesses, and expert testimony on crime scene analysis and mechanical engineering, the jury acquitted on all charges.

Acquitted on all charges

Manslaughter

Mr. Haddad defended the stepfather of a young boy who died in a go-cart accident. The defense showed a bottle in the road caused the go-cart to lose control, and expert testimony showed there was not enough time to stop.

Acquitted

Battery on a school board employee

A mother was accused of slapping her son’s principal at an Honor Society event. The defense established that any contact was unintentional and reflexive.

Acquitted of all charges

Aggravated battery

One of the longest criminal trials in Palm Beach County history, and an early prosecution of alleged gang activity. The defense showed the witnesses’ testimony was inconsistent and biased; after a hung jury the case resolved with a plea to a misdemeanor.

Resolved as a misdemeanor

Attempted first-degree murder, aggravated battery with a firearm

Mr. Haddad defended a 13-year-old accused in a middle school shooting. The defense established that the boy was incompetent to stand trial and legally insane at the time.

All charges dismissed
