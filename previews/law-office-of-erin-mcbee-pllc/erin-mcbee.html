Erin McBee

Attorney at Law, licensed in Massachusetts and Texas. Estate planning, elder law and disAbility law.

Erin McBee received her Bachelor of Arts in Anthropology and in Government from the University of Texas at Austin. During college and after graduation, Erin worked in the Dean of Students office at UT in the Services for Students with Disabilities division. In that role she helped make UT more accessible to students with various abilities, coordinating the recording of textbooks, arranging test proctors, repairing wheelchairs and converting documents into Braille.

Erin earned her Juris Doctor cum laude from New England School of Law in Boston, which awarded her a full-tuition scholarship for all three years. She received the New England School of Law Service Award, the New England Scholar Award and the CALI Award for Remedies, and was on the Dean’s List. In law school she was President and Auction Co-chair of the Public Interest Law Association, served as Executive Literary Editor of the New England Journal on Criminal & Civil Confinement, and wrote a memo for the Prosecutor of the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda.

Erin is licensed to practice in both Texas and Massachusetts. After working as Legal Director at Volunteer Legal Services in Austin, Texas, she returned to Massachusetts, where she worked in the Public Records Division of the Secretary of the Commonwealth. She then served Of Counsel to the Law Office of Nicole K. Talbot in Littleton, focusing on estate planning, family law, business and real estate.

Erin opened her own practice in North Andover and later moved it to Harvard. Since opening the office she has expanded into elder and disAbility law, including long-term care planning and Medicaid planning, working with the Boston elder law firm of Cohen & Oalican, LLP to gain experience with Medicaid applications and long-term care planning.

Erin keeps up with developments in her fields through continuing legal education seminars, trade journals such as Trusts & Estates, the NAELA journal and the Texas Bar Journal, and professional listservs.

“The A in disAbility is capitalized on purpose to reflect a focus on abilities.”
