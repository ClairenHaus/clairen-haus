FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers about working with the office. For anything about your own situation, call or email Erin.

Does the office work with clients in Texas?

Yes. Erin is licensed to practice in both Massachusetts and Texas. She has a virtual office in Texas and is able to meet clients in person in Richardson, Plano, Austin and surrounding cities and towns.

Can I do my estate plan without coming to the office?

Yes. Erin offers virtual, online estate planning for Texas clients, and for Massachusetts clients who prefer it. Meetings can be held by online video, and document signings can be scheduled with a mobile notary.

Will Erin travel to meet me?

Erin can, and frequently does, travel throughout Massachusetts to serve her clients.

What documents are part of an estate plan?

Erin’s estate planning work includes wills, durable powers of attorney, health care powers of attorney, living wills and advance directives, and trusts, including revocable living trusts, irrevocable trusts and irrevocable life insurance trusts.

Can Erin work with my financial advisor and accountant?

Yes. Erin works together with you and your financial and tax advisors to balance tax issues in your estate plan, particularly federal and state estate tax and capital gains.

My loved one died without a will. Can the office help?

Yes. The office can help with the paperwork, organization and distribution of the assets of your loved one, whether they had a will, a trust or no estate plan at all.

Why is the “A” in disAbility capitalized?

On purpose, to reflect a focus on abilities.

Can Erin give a seminar for our group?

Yes. Contact the office if you would like to schedule a seminar presentation for your friends or organization.

Does sending a message through this website make me a client?

No. Contacting the office through this website does not create an attorney-client relationship, and information sent through the website is not protected by attorney-client privilege.
