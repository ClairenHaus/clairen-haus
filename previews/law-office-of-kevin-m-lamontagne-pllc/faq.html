Frequently asked questions

Questions clients ask

Short answers drawn from Kevin’s own guidance. Every situation is different, so call the office to talk through yours.

Estate planning, trusts & wills

Does a living trust automatically avoid probate?

No. A living, revocable trust is one of several tools for avoiding probate, but it has to be carefully planned, drafted and funded. If a living trust is not properly structured and funded, probate may still be required. Post-signing planning, guidance and legal advice matter as much as the signing itself.

Why does owning real estate in another state matter for my estate plan?

Even if all of your Florida property is held in a trust, a Florida probate proceeding is likely to be required if you own real estate in another state. That is one reason a living trust can be especially important for people who own property in more than one state.

What is a ladybird deed?

A ladybird deed, also known as an enhanced life estate deed, is sometimes used to avoid probate for real estate. Care is needed when adding another person to title, because creditors of the added person might make a claim against the property or try to attach it.

Can a designation of health care surrogate take effect right away?

Yes. Florida’s statutes were revised effective October 2015 to allow health care surrogate designations that are effective immediately.

Why should I discuss durable powers of attorney with an estate planning attorney?

Durable powers of attorney, sometimes with “super” powers, can work together with an estate plan, particularly where someone might need to apply for Medicaid at a later date.

Real estate, leases & contracts

Do I need a lawyer to buy or sell real estate in Florida?

Florida law does not require a lawyer at the closing table, but Kevin’s view is that it is always prudent to have legal representation in a matter as important as a sale or purchase, whether the deal uses a standard FAR/BAR contract or a tailor-made one.

Isn’t title insurance enough?

Title insurance is only as good as the worst title exception contained in the policy. Having title insurance is not a reason to go without a real estate attorney.

Can the office help landlords who own rental property?

Yes. Kevin forms LLCs to protect clients who own and hold rental properties, and he reviews residential and commercial leases and handles evictions.

What should a commercial lease cover?

Depending on the deal, provisions to include or consider include base rent, percentage rent and rent escalation; common area maintenance (CAM); a personal guaranty; the term; assignment and subletting; permitted uses; signs; the tenant’s options if permits can’t be obtained within a reasonable time; events of default and remedies; a subordination, non-disturbance and attornment agreement (SNDA); and a “good guy” clause.

Bankruptcy

Does the office handle bankruptcy cases?

Kevin has represented clients in Chapter 7 bankruptcy proceedings and is experienced in bankruptcy litigation. At present, he is limiting his bankruptcy practice to litigated matters.

Who can use Florida’s bankruptcy exemptions?

A Chapter 7 debtor may use Florida’s exemptions if the filer has been a resident of Florida for at least two years before filing.

Does Florida’s unlimited homestead exemption apply in every bankruptcy?

No. How far Florida’s homestead exemption can be used depends on how long the homestead has been owned and other legal considerations. Each case has to be evaluated on its own facts.
