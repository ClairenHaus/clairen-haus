The importance of a Florida homestead

A homestead is a powerful tool for protecting your wealth in estate planning, with some limits worth understanding.

Florida’s homestead laws are very favorable and let people exempt their home from most debt collection. Here are the most important parts to understand.

The full value of your home

Florida lets you declare the house and property you live on as your homestead. There is no upper limit on value: your home can be worth millions and still be covered.

Up to a certain size

While the value is unlimited, the size is not. An urban home can be protected up to half an acre; a rural home up to 160 acres.

Protected from creditors

Once your home is a homestead, it is generally protected from debt collection efforts. If you file bankruptcy, you can still live there and will not have to sell it to pay your debts. Because resolving debts is part of probate, a home that is not subject to those debts is a valuable asset in an estate.

Limitations

There are limits. If the house is used as collateral, or is subject to mechanic’s liens or federal taxes, those may force a sale. Homestead status also will not get you out of debts that predate the homestead filing.

This page explains the general rules. How a homestead fits your own plan depends on your situation; ask in a free consultation.
