Probate attorney in Delray Beach

Probate concerns everyone should know, and how the plan you make with your lawyer shapes the process for your family.

When a person dies, their estate goes through some form of the Florida probate process. How long it takes, and how it affects your family, will largely be decided by the plan you develop with your lawyer. The firm prepares you for the most important parts of probate by working through these questions with you.

Do you have a will?

A valid will guides the probate process. It is the clearest and most widely used way to direct your estate. Without a will, your estate is divided according to Florida law, without considering your wishes.

Who is your personal representative?

In Florida, the person responsible for filing forms and carrying out the wishes in a will is the personal representative. Choosing that person, and making sure they understand their responsibilities, lets you have faith in what happens next.

What type of probate administration fits?

Florida probate administration comes in two main forms: formal administration and summary administration. Which one applies depends on your situation, and for smaller estates it may be possible to settle the estate without probate.

What assets will not go through probate?

Common assets that avoid probate include:

  • Property held in a trust
  • Real estate jointly owned with survivorship rights
  • Life insurance policies
  • Financial accounts with a designated beneficiary

Knowing what will, and what you would like to, avoid probate decides the strategy. It all depends on what works best for you.
