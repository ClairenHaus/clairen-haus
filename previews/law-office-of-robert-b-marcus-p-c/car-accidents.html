Car accidents

When a crash changes your life

The firm represents people injured in motor vehicle accidents. Two of the results on its site involve car crashes: a rear-end collision with minimal impact and no visible damage to the car, and an intersection collision where both drivers claimed the right of way.

Earlier injuries don’t end a case

Both of those clients had prior injuries. In the rear-end case, a biomechanical expert showed that even a minimal impact was enough to cause the client’s injuries and surgery. In the intersection case, the client already had a lawsuit pending over an earlier crash, and the new collision made her neck and back injuries worse.

In plain terms

General information about New York law, not legal advice for your situation.

  • No-fault insurance. In New York, your own auto insurance generally pays basic medical bills and lost earnings after a crash, up to policy limits, no matter who was at fault.
  • The “serious injury” threshold. To sue the other driver for pain and suffering, New York law generally requires a “serious injury” as defined in the Insurance Law, such as a fracture or a significant limitation of use of a body part.
  • Shared fault. New York uses comparative fault: if you are found partly responsible, your recovery is reduced by your share, but not barred.
  • Time limits. Most personal injury lawsuits in New York must be filed within three years. Claims against a city, county or other public body usually require a notice of claim within 90 days.
