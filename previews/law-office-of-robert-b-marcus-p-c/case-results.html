Case results

A few of our million-dollar results.

Twelve results the firm has published, most of them for union and non-union workers hurt on the job.

$7.0 Million

Union electrician

A union electrician in his 40s received an electrical shock while installing an exterior electrical sign at a bank and fell from his ladder, fracturing his spine. Several defendants’ lawyers argued his injuries pre-existed the incident or that he alone caused them.

$6.0 Million

Two union construction laborers

A verdict at trial for two construction laborers hit by a vehicle. One suffered a right leg injury that led to an above-the-knee amputation; the other suffered crush injuries to his right foot. Using expert witnesses and video animation, the firm proved at trial that the driver, who claimed a green light, failed to keep a proper lookout.

$3.1 Million

Union window washer

A 29-year-old window washer fell when his ladder broke, suffering multiple fractures and head injuries.

$3.0 Million

Union carpenter

A union carpenter was injured in a fall that caused knee and back injuries.

$2.2 Million

Account executive

A 39-year-old account executive was rear-ended, aggravating an earlier back condition; he needed surgery and could no longer do his job. The impact was minimal and his car showed no visible damage, but a biomechanical expert showed the crash was enough to cause his injuries and surgery.

$1.975 Million

Union carpenter

A settlement for a 43-year-old union carpenter who fell from a Baker’s scaffold while sheetrocking and fractured his elbow, which needed multiple surgeries. The case settled before a trial date was set, giving his family relief sooner.

$1.9 Million

HVAC worker

A 46-year-old HVAC worker fell from a defective ladder, aggravating an earlier knee injury. He needed arthroscopic surgery, a future knee replacement was recommended, and he could not return to work.

$1.7 Million

Non-union cable technician

A 42-year-old cable technician fell when the ladder he was working on shifted, injuring his neck and hand and requiring multiple surgeries. The defense argued his injuries weren’t caused by the fall, that his work wasn’t a protected activity, and that the building owner was exempt under the Public Service Law.

$1.5 Million

Car accident

A 45-year-old woman was hurt in an intersection collision in which both drivers claimed the right of way. She already had a lawsuit pending over an earlier crash that injured her neck and back; this collision made those injuries worse and required surgery.

$1.36 Million

Union carpenter

A 41-year-old union carpenter suffered a serious foot fracture with complications in healing. The verdict was reduced by 35% for his share of fault, and the case then settled while an appeal was pending.

$1.2 Million

Union electrician

An electrician in his 50s fell at a construction site, aggravating an earlier injury and causing other injuries that needed surgery. He could not return to work, despite the defendants’ claims otherwise.

$1.0 Million

Truck accident

A New York City supervisor was rear-ended in Massachusetts by a tractor-trailer. He had been treated for neck and back injuries a month earlier; after this crash he needed surgery. The case, pending in federal court in Boston, was resolved before trial.
