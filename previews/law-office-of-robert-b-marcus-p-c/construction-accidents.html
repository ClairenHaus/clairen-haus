Construction accidents

Falls from ladders, scaffolds, roofs and cranes, being struck by falling objects, and every other kind of accident on a construction site.

Workers hurt on the job

Construction cases are the heart of this practice. Most of the million-dollar results on the firm’s site are for people hurt while working: union electricians, carpenters and laborers, a window washer, an HVAC worker and a non-union cable technician.

The office handles all kinds of construction-site accidents, including but not limited to:

  • Falls from ladders, including ladders that broke, shifted or were defective
  • Falls from scaffolds, including Baker’s scaffolds
  • Falls from roofs and cranes
  • Being struck by falling objects
  • Electrical shock and other accidents on the job
  • Workers hit by vehicles at a work site

What the other side often argues

In the firm’s own cases, defendants have argued that a worker’s injuries existed before the accident, that the worker alone caused the fall, that the work he was doing wasn’t a “protected activity,” or that a building owner was exempt from liability. The results below include cases where those arguments were made.

In plain terms

General information about New York law, not legal advice for your situation.

New York’s Labor Law gives construction workers some of the strongest protections in the country:

  • Section 240(1), often called the Scaffold Law, makes property owners and general contractors responsible when a worker is hurt in a fall from a height, or by a falling object, because proper safety devices such as ladders, scaffolds or harnesses weren’t provided or didn’t work.
  • Section 241(6) covers injuries caused by violations of specific safety rules for construction, demolition and excavation work.
  • Section 200 sets a general duty to keep a work site reasonably safe.

Workers’ compensation usually covers claims against your own employer. A separate lawsuit may be possible against property owners, general contractors and others responsible for the site.
