Estate planning, in plain terms

An estate plan is a set of documents that says who receives your property, who manages your affairs if you cannot, and who makes decisions on your behalf. A will is the starting point for most people. A trust can hold property for the people you choose and, depending on how it is set up, can be changed during your lifetime (revocable) or not (irrevocable). Powers of attorney name someone you trust to act for you on financial or other matters.

Planning with later life in mind

For many families, planning is as much about the years ahead as about what happens after a death: making sure the right person can step in to pay bills, manage property or make decisions if a parent or spouse can no longer do so. Putting those documents in place early is usually simpler and less stressful than doing it in a hurry.

Probate and estate administration

When someone passes away, their estate generally has to be settled: the will is presented to the probate court, a personal representative is appointed, debts and taxes are handled, and property is passed to the people entitled to it. Tom assists clients with probate and estate administration, so a family has an experienced attorney guiding each step.
