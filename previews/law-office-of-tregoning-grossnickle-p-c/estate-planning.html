Wills, Trusts & Estate Planning

An estate plan built around your family, your property and your wishes, with the right tax planning in view.

What the firm does

The attorneys at the firm have a broad range of experience in assisting clients in developing an estate plan to meet each client’s needs, while taking advantage of the appropriate estate and gift tax planning. Estate planning services include the preparation of wills and trusts, along with the related documents described on the other practice pages.

What an estate plan usually covers

In general terms, an estate plan is a set of documents that says who receives your property, who is in charge of carrying out your wishes, and who can act for you if you become unable to act for yourself. For most people it answers questions like these:

  • Who should receive your property, and when?
  • Who will serve as personal representative of your estate, or as trustee of a trust?
  • Who would care for minor children, and how will money be managed for them?
  • How can taxes and the cost and delay of administration be kept down?

Wills

A will directs how property in your name alone passes at death and names the personal representative who will handle the estate. A will generally takes effect only at death and passes through probate.

Trusts

A trust is an arrangement in which a trustee holds and manages property for the benefit of others under written terms. Trusts can be created during life or under a will, and are often used to manage property for children or other beneficiaries over time, to plan for taxes, or to keep certain property out of probate.

Keeping a plan current

Marriage, divorce, the birth of a child or grandchild, a move, the sale of a business or a change in the law are all good reasons to review an existing plan with an attorney.
