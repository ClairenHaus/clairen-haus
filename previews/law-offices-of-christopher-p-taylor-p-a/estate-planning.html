Estate Planning

Simple and more complex estate plans for individuals and families in South Florida, drafted around your own facts, circumstances and goals.

Why plan now

Estate planning is, in its simplest terms, a set of instructions about what should happen to your minor children and to your things, from money and investments to a home or family heirlooms, both before and after you die. Without the necessary paperwork in place, the State decides how your belongings are distributed and who cares for your minor children.

It isn’t only about death. If an illness or accident leaves you incapacitated, your plan says who makes medical decisions for you, pays your bills and looks after your children. Whatever the reason a plan gets put off, the people left to gather assets, pay debts and deal with the courts are usually your loved ones, and how hard their job is depends on how much was prepared beforehand.

Planning that holds up

Estate planning comes in many forms, and there is no one-size-fits-all approach. After years in estate and trust litigation, Mr. Taylor has seen how poorly drafted wills, trusts, healthcare surrogate designations and powers of attorney can become as costly and burdensome for a family as having no plan at all.

Documents without the proper legal wording, as is often the case with do-it-yourself forms found online, or that were not properly executed, can be disregarded by a Florida court, which recognizes only documents prepared and executed with the formalities Florida law requires. The best way to make sure your wishes are carried out is to make them known and to document them in a clear, lawful form.

Documents and services

  • Last Will and Testament
  • Will codicils
  • Revocable and irrevocable trusts
  • Amendment and restatement of trust
  • Durable powers of attorney
  • Living will and medical power of attorney
  • Designation of healthcare surrogate
  • Designation of pre-need guardian or guardian for a minor child
  • Beneficiary designations and titling of assets to avoid probate (real property, life insurance, bank accounts, pension and retirement plans)
