Frequently asked questions

Straight answers, drawn from the firm’s own materials. For your specific situation, the free consultation is the place to start.

Who does Mr. Taylor represent?

Beneficiaries, heirs and fiduciaries (personal representatives and trustees) in contested and uncontested Florida estate, probate, trust and guardianship matters, as well as individuals and families planning their estates.

Does every Florida estate have to go through probate?

No. Not all Florida estates require probate, and not all property a person owns at death is subject to it. Generally, if a person dies owning property in his or her name alone, probate is required. How property is titled, and the estate planning done in advance, can avoid unnecessary probate costs.

Does a personal representative need a lawyer in Florida?

In Florida, every personal representative must be represented by an attorney admitted to practice in Florida, unless the personal representative is the sole interested person or is a licensed Florida attorney.

Is there a deadline to contest a Florida Will?

Yes. There are crucial and fairly short filing deadlines, and missing them could forever bar a challenge. The deadline depends on the type of notice you received, or should have received, and on whether you are an interested person. Contact the firm promptly if you have questions about a deadline.

Does an inheritance dispute have to go to court?

Not always. Litigation is sometimes necessary to protect and preserve an inheritance, but exploring pre-suit settlement, through informal negotiation or formal mediation, can greatly benefit the parties and save thousands of dollars in fees and costs.

Can a guardianship be avoided?

Often, yes. Before appointing a guardian, a Florida court must consider less restrictive alternatives such as durable powers of attorney, healthcare surrogate designations and living trusts. Planning ahead, including designating a pre-need guardian, also lets you have a say in who would make decisions for you.

Does contacting the firm create an attorney-client relationship?

No. An email, a phone inquiry or any preliminary communication with Mr. Taylor does not create an attorney-client relationship. Confidential or time-urgent matters are best discussed by phone or at a scheduled appointment.
