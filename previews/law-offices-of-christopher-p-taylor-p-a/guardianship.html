Guardianship

Guardianships for minors and incapacitated adults, incapacity proceedings, and planning that can avoid formal guardianship altogether.

What a guardian does

A guardian is a person, or in some cases an entity, appointed by the court to act for a minor child, or for an adult whose mental or physical impairment prevents them from making reasonable decisions or managing daily living without help. Once the court adjudicates incapacity, the guardian makes the decisions the court finds the ward cannot make, always in the ward’s best interests.

Minors and adults

Florida law allows a guardian to be appointed for a minor child when, for example, both parents have died, the parents are unable to raise the child, or the child is entitled to an inheritance or settlement larger than the State allows parents to receive on the child’s behalf.

For adults, guardianship follows a court finding that the person is incapacitated. The court may then appoint someone to make personal, health and financial decisions for the ward.

Less restrictive alternatives come first

Before appointing a guardian, the court must determine that there are no less restrictive alternatives, such as durable powers of attorney, healthcare surrogate designations, living trusts or other advance directives. The goal is the least restrictive way to protect the person, so they keep their dignity and avoid court intrusion into their lives.

Guardianships can often be avoided through estate planning. And if a guardian is ever needed, having designated a pre-need guardian in an advance directive gives you a say in who makes decisions about your health and finances.

When guardianship is necessary

Guardianships carry real ethical and legal duties and ongoing court oversight through annual reports and accountings, so they can be costly and demanding for the family member, friend or professional who serves. They are also an important last line of defense for people who can no longer protect themselves, including from exploitation by those around them.

  • Appointment of emergency temporary, limited and plenary guardians of person and property
  • Determination of incapacity proceedings
  • Planning to avoid formal guardianship through less restrictive alternatives
