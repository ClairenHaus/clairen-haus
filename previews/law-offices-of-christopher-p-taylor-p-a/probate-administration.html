Probate Administration

Guiding personal representatives, beneficiaries and heirs through the administration of Florida probate estates, with or without a Will.

What probate is

Probate is the legal process in which the court oversees the appointment of a personal representative, who gathers the deceased person’s assets, pays creditors and administration expenses, and then makes final distribution to the beneficiaries or heirs. An estate may have a Will (a testate estate) or no Will (an intestate estate).

Personal representatives need counsel

In Florida, every personal representative must be represented by an attorney admitted to practice in Florida, unless the personal representative is the sole interested person or is a licensed Florida attorney.

A great deal can arise during an administration: court-ordered and statutory deadlines, statutory duties and other legal responsibilities that a personal representative must meet, or risk liability to heirs, beneficiaries or creditors. An experienced probate attorney can guide you through the process and provide peace of mind along the way.

If you have been named personal representative or executor in a Will, or are seeking appointment in an estate without a Will, it is wise to speak with a probate attorney right away, so the administration begins within the required deadlines and your exposure to liability is reduced.
