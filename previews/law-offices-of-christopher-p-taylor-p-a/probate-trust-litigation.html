Probate & Trust Litigation

Experienced counsel for beneficiaries, heirs, surviving spouses and fiduciaries in Florida inheritance, probate and trust disputes.

When an estate or trust ends up in dispute

Many estate and trust administrations run efficiently as long as the beneficiaries, heirs and fiduciaries keep a cooperative relationship, and minor disagreements are often best resolved quickly and informally. Sometimes, though, litigation becomes the only way to protect a beneficial interest in an estate or trust, and to preserve the decedent’s estate plans and wishes.

Mr. Taylor has represented individuals and entities in nearly every common type of probate and trust litigation, in cases in nearly every major city in Florida. While many of the laws are similar, the facts of every case are unique, as are the rules and expectations of probate judges across the State.

Settlement first, where it makes sense. Probate and trust litigation, especially between family members, is often highly emotional and can last for years. Exploring pre-suit settlement, through informal negotiation or a formal mediation, can greatly benefit the parties and save thousands of dollars in needless fees and costs.

Common disputes

Florida Will contests

A Will can be challenged by filing a formal complaint or objection in the probate division of the circuit court. Grounds include lack of testamentary capacity, undue influence, mistake, improper execution and fraud. The filing deadlines are fairly short, and missing them can forever bar a challenge. Read Mr. Taylor’s guide.

Trust reformation and termination

When an otherwise unambiguous trust does not achieve what its maker intended, whether through unforeseen changes in life, health or the economy, or through drafting errors and mistakes of fact or law, the Florida Trust Code provides relief for beneficiaries and fiduciaries. Read the guide.

Breach of fiduciary duty, removal and surcharge

Actions against personal representatives, trustees and their attorneys who fail in their duties, including requests to remove a fiduciary and to surcharge them for losses.

Elective share and surviving spouse rights

Florida lets a surviving spouse elect to take 30% of the “elective estate” instead of what the Will or intestacy statutes provide, which can lead to disputes over valuation and entitlement. Read the guide.

Exploitation of an elderly or vulnerable adult

Claims against people who financially exploit an elderly or vulnerable adult, most often someone the person trusted, which can frustrate that person’s own estate plans. Read the guide.

Compensation and fee disputes

Disputes over what personal representatives, trustees and their attorneys are paid. Courts can review compensation, adjust it and order unreasonable amounts returned. Read the guide.
