Workers’ Compensation

If you were injured at work in Alabama, you may be entitled to workers’ compensation benefits from your employer to help with lost income and medical bills.

When benefits are delayed or stopped

Workers’ compensation benefits help with lost income and medical bills after an on-the-job injury. Some employers and insurance companies try to keep employees from receiving the benefits they are entitled to, or stop them early. An attorney can help you through the claim process.

Common workplace injuries

  • Slip and fall, trip and fall
  • Vehicle accidents
  • Broken or fractured bones
  • Herniated or bulging discs
  • Carpal tunnel and repetitive-motion injuries
  • Shoulder, neck, back and joint injuries
  • Severe burns
  • Brain injuries
  • Hernias
  • Occupational illnesses and chemical exposure

Alabama workers’ compensation: questions and answers

Am I required to see the doctor my company selected?

Yes. The employer is only liable to pay medical benefits for an injured employee who has been treated by a doctor the employer has chosen or approved.

Will I receive weekly benefits while I can’t work?

If the employer’s selected or approved physician says you cannot work at all because of your injury, you are entitled to Temporary Total Disability (TTD) benefits. If the physician says you can work part-time, or in another position that pays less, you are entitled to Temporary Partial Disability (TPD) benefits.

Can my employer refuse to file a claim because no one saw the accident?

No. An accident that was not witnessed still happened, and the employer must file a first report of injury.

How long do I have to act?

In Alabama, the statute of limitations for a workplace injury is two years from the date of injury, or two years from the date of the last compensation payment.

Questions clients ask

What is the statute of limitations in Alabama for a workplace injury?

Two years from the date of injury or two years from the date of last compensation payment.

Can an employer refuse to file a workers compensation claim because there was not a witness to the accident?

No. Just because an accident was not witnessed, it does not mean it did not happen. A first report of injury must be filed by the employer.
