Estate Planning

Estate planning is a strong part of the practice, and it is a relatively simple three-step process: an initial meeting, drafting and review, and the signing.

Call 770-751-0900

1. The initial meeting

About an hour and a half. You meet your attorney and ask questions; your attorney learns about your assets and liabilities, how you want your estate distributed, and your choices of executor, trustee and guardian, along with alternates.

2. Drafting and review

Your lawyer drafts and you review. Drafts typically include a will, an affidavit, and a living will or health care power of attorney; you may also want a general durable power of attorney or a trust. You request changes until everyone is happy with the documents.

3. Signing

The signing, which lawyers call execution, takes place at the office, typically takes less than half an hour, and is straightforward.

What to bring

  • Closing files, deeds and notes for any real estate you own
  • Recent tax bills
  • A list of valuable personal property, with any appraisals
  • Recent bank and brokerage statements
  • Stock or bond certificates
  • Life insurance policies and statements

FAQ

Common questions

How long does the estate planning process take?

It is three steps: an initial meeting of about an hour and a half, drafting and review of the documents, and a signing at the office that typically takes less than half an hour.

What documents does an estate plan usually include?

Typically a will, an affidavit, and a living will or health care power of attorney. A general durable power of attorney or a trust may be added.
