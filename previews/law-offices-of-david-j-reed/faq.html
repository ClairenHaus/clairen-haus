Answers

Questions clients ask

Who does the firm represent?

Individuals and small businesses in metro Atlanta. The Law Offices of David J. Reed is the solo practice of David J. Reed, Esq.

What kinds of cases does the firm handle?

Civil litigation (with a focus on real property, plus personal injury, business disputes, business divorce and commercial cases), real estate, business law and incorporation, contract preparation and review, and estate planning.

Does the firm handle real estate closings?

No. The firm does not do closings, but it handles nearly everything else related to real estate: entities and trusts, contract review, and real property litigation.

Does the firm handle criminal cases?

The firm’s litigation practice is civil litigation: real property, personal injury, business disputes, business divorce and commercial cases.

Which courts is David Reed admitted to?

The Supreme Court of Georgia, the Georgia Court of Appeals, all other Georgia state courts, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, and the U.S. District Courts for the Northern and Middle Districts of Georgia.

What are the stages of a lawsuit?

There are at least six major phases in nearly all litigation: preparation, filing, answer, discovery, motions, and trial.

How long does a defendant have to answer a lawsuit?

Typically 30 days after the Summons and Complaint are served. If no Answer is filed, the court can enter a default judgment.

What is the dollar limit in Georgia Magistrate Court?

$15,000. If your claim is worth more, filing in Magistrate Court can limit you to that amount for the rest of the case, so analyze the full value of the claim first.

Should I give a statement to the other driver’s insurance company?

Not without your attorney’s approval. The insurer may record the conversation and use unfavorable statements against you.

What should I bring to a first meeting about an injury case?

Medical records and bills, the police report, photographs of the vehicles and your injuries, your own written account, your insurance policies, and any letters from an adjuster.

How long does the estate planning process take?

It is three steps: an initial meeting of about an hour and a half, drafting and review of the documents, and a signing at the office that typically takes less than half an hour.

What should I bring to an estate planning meeting?

Closing files and deeds for any real estate, recent tax bills, a list of valuable personal property with any appraisals, recent bank and brokerage statements, stock or bond certificates, and life insurance policies.

Why have a contract reviewed before signing?

Review is an efficient way to avoid problems and litigation later. The firm looks closely at restrictive covenants, jurisdiction clauses, unusual terms, and exactly who is being bound.
