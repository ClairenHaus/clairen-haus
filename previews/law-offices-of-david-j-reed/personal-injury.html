The first call

A case typically begins with a first call to the office, often from someone calling a lawyer for the first time in their life, and frequently referred by an existing client. The call focuses on three questions: liability (who is at fault), damages (what a jury would likely award) and coverage (whether there is insurance or another resource to pay a judgment or settlement).

What to bring to the first meeting

  • Medical records and bills
  • The police report
  • Photographs of your car and the other driver’s car, if applicable
  • Photographs of your injuries
  • Your written summary or diary of what happened
  • Your auto and health insurance policies
  • Any correspondence from an adjuster
  • Anything else relevant to the case

Before you talk to anyone

Do not talk to anyone about your case, including insurance companies, until your attorney’s office approves it. An insurance carrier may record its conversation with you and use unfavorable statements against you. Get the police or incident report and the names and phone numbers of any witnesses to the office as soon as possible, and keep a diary of events and complaints starting on the date of the incident.

FAQ

Common questions

Should I give a statement to the other driver’s insurance company?

Not without your attorney’s approval. The insurer may record the conversation and use unfavorable statements against you.

What should I bring to a first meeting about an injury case?

Medical records and bills, the police report, photographs of the vehicles and your injuries, your own written account, your insurance policies, and any letters from an adjuster.
