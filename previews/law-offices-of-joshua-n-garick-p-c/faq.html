Questions & answers

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about the office, how the firm works and how to get started.

Where is the office?

The office is at 34 Salem Street, Suite 202, Reading, MA 01867, on Salem Street (Route 129) in downtown Reading. Off-street parking is available in front of the building.

What areas of Massachusetts does the firm serve?

The firm represents clients throughout Massachusetts. Attorney Garick’s cases bring him to courts in and around Boston and in Suffolk, Middlesex, Essex, Norfolk, Worcester, Nantucket, Plymouth, Bristol, Berkshire, Hampden, Hampshire, Dukes, Franklin and Barnstable Counties.

How do I set up a consultation?

Call (617) 600-7520 or email joshua@garicklaw.com to arrange a no-obligation consultation.

Does the firm represent employees or employers?

Both. The firm has represented employees and employers in discrimination claims and other workplace disputes, and appears for both at unemployment hearings.

Does the firm represent tenants or landlords?

Both. The firm represents tenants and landlords in residential and commercial lease disputes, and advises residential landlords before a tenancy begins.

Can you handle an appeal if you weren’t the trial lawyer?

Yes. The firm handles appeals in cases where it served as trial counsel and appeals where it is retained only for the appeal. It also files amicus curiae briefs. Appellate deadlines are strict, so contact the office right away if you think you have an appealable issue.

Does the firm bring class actions?

Yes. The firm has filed wage and hour claims as class actions, and has brought class actions against residential landlords whose leases include unlawful terms. Massachusetts consumer protection law also allows class actions.

Which courts is Attorney Garick admitted to?

He is licensed in the Commonwealth of Massachusetts, the U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts and the U.S. Court of Appeals for the First Circuit, and has practiced temporarily in other jurisdictions, including Florida, Minnesota, New Hampshire, New York and Pennsylvania.

How does the firm handle the cost of a case?

Cost is treated as a key part of a successful result. Attorney Garick pays close attention to it and keeps clients informed of costs throughout the representation.

Does the firm take pro bono cases?

Yes. The firm keeps at least one pro bono case on its active roster at all times. To qualify you must meet income guidelines and there can be no conflict with other clients. If the firm can’t help, it will try to find you an attorney through legal aid offices across the state.

Is it safe to send details of my case by email or through the website?

Please don’t include confidential or sensitive information. Email and website messages are not secure, and sending them does not create an attorney-client relationship.
