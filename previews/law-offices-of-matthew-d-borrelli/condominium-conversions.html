Condominium Conversions

Turning a multi-family home or investment property into condominiums.

“We can provide legal representation in the conversion of your multi-family home or investment property into a condominium.”

A conversion creates separate units that can be sold on their own, plus an association to manage what the owners share. The work includes four main steps.

1. Drafting the Master Deed

The Master Deed specifies what is common area and outlines the boundaries of each unit. It also sets out the rights and obligations of the unit owners, including exclusive-use areas for each unit, which usually include basements, porches, storage areas and parking spaces. When it is recorded at the Registry of Deeds, the Master Deed legally creates the condominium.

2. Drafting the Declaration of Trust and Bylaws

These documents create the condominium association, the entity that manages the condominium. The association has its own bank account for each unit’s monthly condominium fees, and uses those funds to pay the common expenses: usually maintenance, repair, upkeep, insurance premiums, the common water bill and landscaping.

3. Coordinating the architect and engineer

A conversion also needs an architect and an engineer or land surveyor. The architect measures the interior of each unit, including basements, entry hallways, stairways and other shared areas, and shows any exclusive-use areas such as storage. The engineer or surveyor prepares a plan of the lot showing where the house sits, including any driveway and garage.

4. Recording the documents

The firm records the Master Deed, the Declaration of Trust and Bylaws, the floor plans and the site plan with the appropriate Registry of Deeds.
