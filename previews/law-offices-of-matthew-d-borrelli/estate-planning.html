Estate Planning & Probate

Wills, trusts and the documents that protect your family.

“We offer estate planning services tailored to meet the special needs and objectives of each client. We take particular pride in drawing basic estate plans for young families and couples to protect and provide for their children.”

The most common estate planning tools the firm prepares include:

  • Wills
  • Trusts
  • Durable power of attorney
  • Advance health care directive
  • Homestead protection

The firm also works closely with financial planners who can offer further protection through life insurance, or help with general financial growth.

What estate planning covers

Estate planning is the process of making sure the assets you build during your lifetime are protected and go to the people you love. It is more than a will or a trust. A complete plan addresses:

  • who manages your assets if you can’t;
  • who makes medical and personal care decisions for you and your minor children if you can’t;
  • how your assets pass to your beneficiaries after your death; and
  • keeping the administrative and tax costs of that transfer down.

Wills

A will directs how your assets are distributed and names the executor who will manage your estate. Most importantly for parents, it can name a guardian to care for children who are still minors.

A will generally controls only assets titled in your name alone. Retirement accounts, jointly owned property, “payable on death” accounts and life insurance usually pass by their own beneficiary designations or ownership terms, so they need to be reviewed as part of the plan.

In Massachusetts a will must be in writing, signed, and witnessed by two people. A will written out by hand and signed but not witnessed is generally not valid here.

Living trusts

A living trust holds your assets for your benefit during your lifetime and directs their distribution after your death. You usually serve as your own trustee and name successor trustees to step in if you become unable to serve. A trustee must follow the trust’s terms and use its property only for you and your beneficiaries.

A properly funded living trust can allow your assets to be managed during incapacity without a court-appointed conservator, can let your estate be settled without probate court supervision, and keeps your finances private. A trust does not, by itself, save estate taxes or change your income tax; tax-saving provisions can be written into either a trust or a will.

Probate

Assets held in your own name at death generally go through the probate court, which supervises paying creditors, inventorying assets and distributing them to beneficiaries. Good planning decides which assets go through probate and which pass directly.
