Incapacity Planning

Estate planning is not just for the elderly. Anyone can become incapacitated at any age, so you choose now who makes decisions for you, and when.

Financial Power of Attorney

A Financial Power of Attorney authorizes a trusted person to make financial decisions on your behalf. It can take effect immediately or spring into effect upon disability, and it can be limited to one purpose or be very broad. Georgia adopted a standard form in 2017; the firm takes the time to talk through its many options with you.

Georgia Advance Directive for Health Care

Released in 2007, the Georgia Advance Directive for Health Care combines the living will and the health care power of attorney. It lets you choose in advance what medical treatment you should receive if you cannot express your wishes, and gives your health care agent a roadmap to follow.

Guardianships and conservatorships

If a loved one can no longer make decisions about their health and safety or their finances, they may need a guardianship or a conservatorship. A special needs child who is not able at age 18 to live independently also requires a guardian, and perhaps a conservator.

The firm represents clients in guardianship and conservatorship proceedings before the probate courts in most metro Atlanta counties, and works to make an often uncomfortable process as smooth as possible so that someone with your loved one’s best interests in mind is appointed.

Questions about incapacity planning

Is estate planning only for older people?

No. Anyone can become incapacitated at any age. Financial powers of attorney and a Georgia Advance Directive for Health Care let you choose who makes decisions for you and when that transition takes place.

Where does the firm handle guardianship cases?

Before the probate courts in most metro Atlanta counties.
