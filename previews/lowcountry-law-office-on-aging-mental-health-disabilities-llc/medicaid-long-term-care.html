Practice area

Medicaid to pay for care, at home or in a facility.

We help clients obtain Medicaid to pay for care, whether in a nursing facility or at home through South Carolina’s Home and Community-Based Waiver services.

What Medicaid is

Medicaid is a program in which the federal government and the State of South Carolina share the cost of medical care for people with limited income. Because the cost is shared, each state has some flexibility, so the rules vary from state to state.

Nursing home care, or care at home

South Carolina Medicaid pays for long-term care for people living in licensed and certified nursing facilities. It also pays for services through Home and Community-Based Waiver programs, which help people who need a nursing-home level of care remain at home instead. The same eligibility requirements apply to both.

Community Long Term Care (CLTC) is a set of services designed to help people avoid unnecessary or premature nursing home placement. Depending on need, it can include:

  • Personal care aides and attendant care
  • Home management and environmental modifications
  • Home-delivered meals
  • Adult day health care
  • Respite care
  • A personal emergency response system

Who decides eligibility

The South Carolina Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) decides eligibility for Medicaid programs, except Supplemental Security Income (SSI). People found eligible for SSI by the Social Security Administration are generally eligible for Medicaid. Applicants must meet both financial and non-financial standards.

Before you apply for a loved one: in South Carolina, an adult child or spouse cannot sign a Medicaid application on someone else’s behalf without being appointed under that person’s power of attorney. If no power of attorney exists and your loved one can no longer sign one, a conservatorship may be needed.

How we help

  • Explaining the options for paying for care in a facility or at home
  • Preparing and signing the right powers of attorney while your loved one is able to
  • Guiding families through the application

Common questions

Can my spouse or adult child sign my Medicaid application?

Only if they have been appointed under your power of attorney. In South Carolina, adult children and even spouses cannot sign without that appointment.

Does Medicaid only pay for nursing homes?

No. South Carolina Medicaid also pays for Home and Community-Based Waiver services, which help people who need a nursing-home level of care stay at home.
