Practice area

Advocating for people with mental illness.

We believe in protecting, advancing and advocating for the rights of adults and children with mental disabilities, and that every person is entitled to choice and dignity.

Choice and dignity

We envision a community where people with mental illness or developmental disabilities make their own life choices and have the resources to participate fully. For many, that starts with something basic: a decent place to live, supportive services and equal opportunity.

Where mental health and the law meet

“Competence” is a legal conclusion that a person can enter a binding contract, transfer assets or take part in a legal proceeding. Questions of capacity come up in estate planning, in benefits applications, and in court.

Commitment proceedings in South Carolina

In South Carolina, a person may become subject to the mental commitment process if local law enforcement considers them a danger to themselves or others. The person may then be hospitalized against their will, and the county becomes involved. Depending on the case, proceedings may be handled by the Department of Social Services (Adult or Child Protective Services) and the county Family Court, by the county Probate Court, or by both.

A commitment can mean a short hospital stay (typically fewer than 30 days) and court-ordered outpatient treatment or counseling for up to two years. Commitments can involve minors or adults living with a mental illness, a drug or alcohol addiction, or the effects of aging.

The commitment process is not designed to manage the long-term needs of someone who has lost capacity and is unlikely to regain it. For that, families may need a guardianship and conservatorship.

  • Guardianship and conservatorship when long-term decision-making is needed
