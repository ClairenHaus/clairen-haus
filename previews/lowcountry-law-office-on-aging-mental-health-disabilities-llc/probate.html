Practice area

Probate and estate administration.

Probate makes sure a person’s debts are addressed and their property passes properly to loved ones. It can be complex and time-consuming, especially during a time of grief, so we handle the legal side while you focus on your family.

Why an estate has to be probated

Estates must be probated whether or not there is a will. Probate is how ownership of property, especially real estate, is legally transferred, so the deed is in the name of someone living and the property can be sold. The process usually begins with naming a personal representative to administer the estate.

What to bring to your first appointment

  • A certified copy of the death certificate (with the raised seal)
  • A copy of the funeral program
  • A list of the names and addresses of all living blood relatives

How long it takes

An estate typically takes about one year to go through probate. When an estate is opened, a notice to creditors is published in a local newspaper, and creditors have eight months to make a claim. The personal representative can pay a valid claim or deny one they believe is not valid; if needed, a probate judge decides after a hearing.

What a “simple” estate still requires

  1. Filing a petition, the original will (if there is one) and a certified death certificate with the right county Probate Court
  2. Paying filing fees and creditor’s notice fees
  3. Preparing and filing the Information to Heirs and Devisees and the Inventory and Appraisement
  4. Handling claims
  5. Re-titling and distributing assets
  6. Preparing and filing the closing documents, including the Notice of Right to Demand Hearing, receipts and releases, deeds of distribution, accountings, and the petitions for settlement and discharge, all on deadline

Common questions

Does an estate have to go through probate if there is a will?

Yes. Estates must be probated whether or not there is a will, so ownership of property can legally pass to someone living.

How long does probate take?

About one year. Creditors have eight months from the published notice to make a claim against the estate.

What do I need to bring to start probate?

A certified copy of the death certificate with a raised seal, a copy of the funeral program, and the names and addresses of all living blood relatives.
