Elder Law & Medicaid Planning

Elder law and Medicaid planning

Planning for the legal, financial and care decisions that come with aging, including how to pay for long-term care.

What elder law covers

Elder law brings together the issues older adults and their families face: planning for incapacity, health care decisions, long-term care, protecting a spouse, and passing on property. It draws on estate planning, guardianship and public benefits law at the same time.

Medicaid planning

Nursing-home care is expensive, and Medicaid can help pay for it for people who meet its medical, income and asset rules. The rules are technical. Transfers of assets made within a five-year look-back period can delay eligibility, and Florida has specific requirements for people whose income is above the limit, such as the use of a qualified income trust. Planning ahead, and understanding the rules before acting, can make a significant difference.

Protecting decisions as well as assets

A durable power of attorney, health care surrogate designation and living will let you choose who decides for you, and how, if you cannot. Without them, a family may need a court-ordered guardianship.
