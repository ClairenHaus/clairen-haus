Estate Planning

Estate planning for you, your family and your legacy

A plan that says who receives what you have built, who makes decisions if you cannot, and how to spare your family avoidable cost and uncertainty.

What an estate plan does

An estate plan is a set of documents that carries out your wishes during your life and after it. Done well, it names the people you trust, provides for the people who depend on you, and reduces the delay, expense and conflict your family might otherwise face.

An initial meeting is a good time to talk through your situation, your concerns and your estate planning goals.

Documents a plan often includes

  • A will, naming who inherits your property and who will serve as your personal representative.
  • A revocable trust, which can hold assets during your life and pass them to your beneficiaries without a court-supervised probate.
  • A durable power of attorney, so someone you choose can handle financial matters if you cannot.
  • Health care documents, such as a designation of health care surrogate and a living will, which state who decides and what you want.
  • Beneficiary designations on accounts and policies, coordinated with the rest of the plan.

Why tax and business experience matters here

Many estates include a business interest, real estate or assets with tax consequences. Tance E. Roberts is Florida Bar Board Certified in Tax Law, holds an LL.M. in Taxation and is an Accredited Estate Planner®, so the tax and business sides of a plan are part of the same conversation.

Keeping a plan current

Marriage, divorce, a birth, a death, a move, a new business or a change in the law can all make an older plan out of date. Reviewing your documents after major life events keeps them working the way you intend.
