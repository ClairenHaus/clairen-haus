Wills, Trusts & Estates

Wills, trusts and estates

Choosing the right tools for your plan, and carrying out estates and trusts with care when the time comes.

Wills

A will states who receives your property at death and names a personal representative to carry out your wishes. Property that passes under a will generally goes through probate, the court process that confirms the will, pays valid debts and distributes what remains.

Trusts

A trust is an arrangement in which a trustee holds property for the benefit of one or more beneficiaries under written instructions. A revocable living trust lets you keep control during your life, name a successor trustee to step in if you become unable to manage your affairs, and pass trust assets without probate. Other kinds of trusts can be used for specific goals, such as providing for a beneficiary over time.

Will or trust?

The right choice depends on what you own, where it is, who your beneficiaries are and what you want to accomplish. Many plans use both: a trust to hold most assets and a will to catch anything left outside it.

Estate and trust administration

When someone passes away, the people they named have real duties. A personal representative or successor trustee must gather and protect assets, give required notices, deal with debts and taxes, keep records and distribute property as the documents direct. The firm’s practice includes estate and trust administration, guiding fiduciaries through each of these steps.
