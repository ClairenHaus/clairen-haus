Estate and Trust Administration

The death of a loved one is a time of great difficulty. Meyer Law Firm can advise you on administering the estate yourself, or handle the entire process for you.

Administering an estate can involve probate of a will, administration of a revocable trust, or guidance on collecting insurance proceeds and transferring title to bank accounts and real estate. There are also tax issues in distributing assets held in pension and profit-sharing plans, 401(k) plans and IRAs. The firm strives to make the process as smooth as possible.

How the firm helps trustees

Notice to beneficiaries

When a living trust becomes irrevocable at its creator’s death, the trustee must give the heirs and all trust beneficiaries certain required notices. The firm counsels newly appointed trustees on the statutory notice requirements and deadlines, and prepares the notices so the trustee complies with the law.

Assessment of trust inventory

The firm helps the trustee identify and collect all trust assets and, when necessary, obtains valuations and appraisals to determine an accurate value for each.

Creating, funding and administering sub-trusts

It may be necessary to split the trust into sub-trusts, such as a bypass or credit shelter trust (often called a “family trust” or “AB trust”). Failing to create and fund them properly and on time can have significant estate tax consequences.

Trust accounting

After remaining debts are paid, sub-trusts are established and the relevant state and federal estate tax returns are filed, the firm assists trustees with accounting documents and with the statutory annual accounting requirements.
