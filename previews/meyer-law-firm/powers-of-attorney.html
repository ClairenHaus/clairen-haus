Powers of Attorney

Thinking about what would happen if you became ill or seriously hurt is never easy. These documents make sure someone you trust can speak for you, and spare your loved ones a difficult process.

If you cannot speak for yourself, your loved ones and health care providers can be unclear about the treatments you would want and about who should make those decisions. Meyer Law Firm recommends that every individual have these five emergency documents.

Five critical emergency documents

1. Durable power of attorney

A durable power of attorney protects you if you become incapacitated. It lets you appoint someone you trust to manage your financial and personal affairs if you become temporarily or permanently unable to, so financial decisions are made with your best interests at heart.

2. Designation of health care surrogate

Also called a health care power of attorney, it appoints a trusted person to make health care decisions on your behalf if you are unable to make them yourself.

3. HIPAA release authority

The federal HIPAA law requires health care providers to get written authorization before sharing medical records, even with caregivers or close relatives. A HIPAA release lets your records be provided to a person you specify, so they have the information they need to make sound decisions based on your wishes.

4. Living will declaration

Also known as a health care directive, a living will states your wishes about life-sustaining procedures you do or do not want. It is used only if you cannot make your own decisions, and it spares your loved ones from guessing.

5. Declaration of preneed guardian

This lets you name a preferred guardian in case a court ever finds you incapacitated. If you do not name one, the court may appoint a stranger. See Guardianships.

With these documents in place, you and your family have the peace of mind of knowing there is a plan for emergency financial and medical situations.
