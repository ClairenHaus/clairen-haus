How these cases generally work

What the False Claims Act does

The federal False Claims Act lets the government recover money from people or companies that knowingly submit false claims for payment to federal programs, such as health care, defense or other government contracts. Damages can be up to three times the government’s loss, plus civil penalties for each false claim.

What “qui tam” means

A private person with inside knowledge of the fraud, often called a relator or whistleblower, can file a qui tam lawsuit on the government’s behalf. The case is filed under seal while the government investigates and decides whether to join it. A successful relator may receive a share of the recovery, and the Act protects employees from retaliation.

Strict procedures

Qui tam cases have strict filing and disclosure requirements, and some claims can be barred if the information was already public or if deadlines pass. Talking to a lawyer early matters.
