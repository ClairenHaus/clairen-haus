FAQ

Questions people ask

Straight answers, taken from Andy’s own practice information.

Where is Andy Tindel’s office?

The office is at 112 East Line Street, Suite 304, Tyler, Texas 75702. Call 903-596-0900 or toll-free 800-392-4031.

What kinds of cases does Andy handle?

Complex commercial litigation, patent infringement trials, appeals, securities fraud actions, False Claims Act and qui tam cases, insurance law, and personal injury and wrongful death claims. He also mediates civil disputes.

Is Andy Tindel board certified?

Yes. He is certified by the Texas Board of Legal Specialization in Civil Trial Law, Civil Appellate Law and Personal Injury Trial Law.

Which courts is Andy admitted to?

The Supreme Court of the United States, the U.S. Courts of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit and the Federal Circuit, the U.S. District Courts for the Eastern, Southern, Western and Northern Districts of Texas and the Eastern District of Oklahoma, and the Texas Supreme Court.

Does Andy serve as local counsel in the Eastern District of Texas?

Yes. He serves as advisor and local counsel for companies, inventors and national law firms with cases in any division of the Eastern District of Texas, from discovery and motion practice through trial and appeal.

Can Andy help with an appeal in a case another lawyer tried?

Yes. He acts as co-counsel on appeal, helping with briefs and argument strategy, or as lead appellate counsel at the request of the client or the trial lawyers. He is also retained early in cases to advise on motions, the jury charge and post-trial motions.

Does Andy mediate cases?

Yes. He has been a certified mediator since March 1999 and has served on the Twelfth District Court of Appeals mediator panel since 2001. He mediates selected civil cases in state and federal court in Northeast Texas and will travel at the parties’ request.

How do I schedule a mediation?

Contact Sheri Peterson at 903-596-0900 or speterson@andytindel.com.

How are mediation fees set?

Half-day and full-day mediation fees are competitively priced. Fees for multi-party or unusually complex cases are set case by case after consulting with the parties.

Is it safe to email confidential information?

Please don’t send confidential information until you have spoken with Andy. Email may not be confidential, and sending a message does not create an attorney-client relationship. The firm must first check for conflicts with existing or former clients.

Where did Andy go to school?

Baylor University (B.B.A. in Accounting, 1979) and Baylor University School of Law (J.D., 1982). He then clerked for U.S. District Judge William M. Steger from 1982 to 1985.

Talk with Andy about your case

Call the Tyler office, or send a short note about your matter. Andy works with clients directly and with the lawyers and law firms who retain him.
