Practice Areas

Patent Infringement Trials

Intellectual property and technology trials in one of the busiest patent venues in the country, the United States District Court for the Eastern District of Texas.

Andy’s experience

Andy’s curriculum vitae lists intellectual property and technology matters, patent infringement suits, and copyright and trademark disputes among the cases he tries and appeals. He is admitted to the United States Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit, the court that hears patent appeals, and he frequently serves as advisor, attorney and local counsel for domestic and overseas companies and national law firms with cases in the Eastern District.

How these cases generally work

What a patent case involves

A patent owner who believes another company is making, using or selling its patented invention without permission can sue for infringement in federal court. The accused company usually answers that it does not infringe, that the patent is invalid, or both.

The usual stages

Patent cases typically involve early infringement and invalidity contentions, a claim construction (Markman) hearing where the court decides what disputed patent terms mean, expert discovery on technology and damages, and a jury trial. Appeals go to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit.

Why local rules and local counsel matter

The Eastern District of Texas has its own patent rules, standing orders and courtroom customs. Out-of-state firms often work with a lawyer who practices there every day for court protocol, local rules and trial strategy.

  • Trial team member in patent infringement trials
  • Guidance on Eastern District local rules and court protocol
