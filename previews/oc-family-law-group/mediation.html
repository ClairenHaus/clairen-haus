What is Mediation?

Mediation is a voluntary process where a neutral third party, known as a mediator, assists the disputing parties in negotiating a settlement. The mediator helps facilitate communication, clarify issues, and explore potential solutions, but does not impose a decision. This process allows parties to maintain control over the outcome and create customized solutions that meet their specific needs.

Benefits of Mediation

Cost-Effective

  • Lower Costs. Mediation is generally less expensive than litigation, saving both time and money.
  • Efficient Process. Disputes are often resolved faster through mediation than through the court system.

Confidential

  • Private Proceedings. Mediation sessions are confidential and not part of the public record, allowing for more open and honest communication.
  • Safe Environment. The confidential nature of mediation encourages a safe space for discussing sensitive issues without fear of public exposure.

Control and Flexibility

  • Customized Solutions. Parties have more control over the outcome and can tailor solutions to their specific needs and circumstances.
  • Flexible Scheduling. Mediation sessions can be scheduled at times that are convenient for all parties involved, offering greater flexibility.

Preserves Relationships

  • Collaborative Approach. Mediation focuses on cooperation rather than confrontation, which can help preserve relationships, especially important in family matters.
  • Reduces Hostility. By fostering a collaborative environment, mediation can help reduce hostility and encourage amicable resolutions.

The Mediation Process

Initiating Mediation

  • Agreement to Mediate. Both parties must agree to participate in the mediation process.
  • Selecting a Mediator. Choosing a qualified mediator who is neutral and experienced in family law is essential for a successful mediation.

Mediation Sessions

  • Initial Meeting. The mediator explains the process, establishes ground rules, and identifies the issues to be resolved.
  • Discussion and Negotiation. Parties discuss their concerns, with the mediator facilitating dialogue and helping to explore options for resolution.
  • Reaching an Agreement. The goal of mediation is to reach a mutually acceptable agreement on all disputed issues.

Finalizing the Agreement

  • Drafting the Settlement. The mediator helps draft a written agreement outlining the terms of the settlement.
  • Legal Review. Parties may choose to have their attorneys review the agreement before signing to ensure all legal rights are protected.
  • Court Approval. In some cases, the agreement may need to be submitted to the court for approval and incorporation into a court order.
