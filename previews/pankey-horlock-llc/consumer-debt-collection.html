Consumer Debt Collection

Collecting association assessments and other debts in Georgia since 1991, before and after judgment.

Our experience

Since 1991, our attorneys have been collecting all kinds of debts from debtors located in Georgia. We collect community association assessments, fines, charges and attorneys’ fees, as well as judgments, invoices, open accounts, secured debts, commercial and consumer debts, deficient automobile leases and mortgages, and generally any other type of debt.

The firm has significant experience in pre-judgment and post-judgment collection, including garnishments, asset location and foreclosure.

Pre-judgment collection

Community associations have a number of collection strategies available without litigation or a judgment. Depending on the type of association and what the declaration authorizes, an association may be able to:

  • Suspend use of common elements, such as the swimming pool, tennis courts or parking privileges
  • In certain circumstances, suspend services or utilities paid for by the association as a common expense, such as trash pick-up, water or power
  • Suspend the delinquent owner’s right to vote on association matters
  • Require a tenant leasing from a delinquent owner to pay rent directly to the association

If your association is not already using these strategies, we can help identify your rights and put the proper procedures in place so these pre-litigation steps work.

Post-judgment collection

Often an association must file a lawsuit to collect what is owed and to use the power of the courts to enforce payment. Once the court awards judgment, the association may take further steps to enforce it, such as:

  • Garnishing the debtor’s wages and bank accounts
  • Placing judgment liens on the debtor’s real estate and personal property, such as cars and motorcycles
  • Having the sheriff seize and sell real estate or personal property at a public sale to satisfy the debt

With a judgment above a certain amount, associations subject to the Georgia Condominium Act may suspend utilities paid for by the association as a common expense. With a judgment, associations subject to the Georgia Condominium Act or the Georgia Property Owners’ Association Act usually have the right to foreclose on the delinquent property, a very powerful collection tool.

Working with collection attorneys

Court action can often be avoided altogether. Debtors are more likely to respond to a collection letter from a lawyer, and the prospect of legal action often leads to voluntary payment of outstanding debts.

The firm provides a full range of resources for collecting association debts: locating debtors and their assets, identifying the debtor’s employer and bank accounts, litigating the claims, and enforcing the court’s judgment.
