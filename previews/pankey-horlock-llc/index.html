About the practice

Aggressive, diligent and persistent.

Serving associations across Metropolitan Atlanta and Northern Georgia

Pankey & Horlock represents community associations in all legal issues necessary for the successful operation of their associations. Its attorneys have represented and advised many condominium and homeowners’ associations of all sizes throughout northern Georgia.

The firm has handled scores of issues for associations: interpreting and enforcing covenants, advising boards, drafting and revising governing documents, coordinating meetings and votes for amendments, collecting assessments and perfecting liens. It also litigates covenant disputes and disputes with vendors, and its attorneys represent clients before administrative agencies, in mediation and arbitration, and in state and federal trial and appellate courts.

Common questions

Answers from the firm’s own site.

Each answer below is drawn from facts the firm already publishes, marked up so search engines and AI assistants can cite it. General information only, not legal advice.

What does a community association lawyer do for an HOA or condo board?

Pankey & Horlock handles the full spectrum of legal issues for associations: interpreting and enforcing covenants, advising boards on their rights and obligations under the governing documents, drafting and amending governing documents, coordinating amendment votes, collecting assessments, perfecting liens, negotiating vendor contracts and litigating disputes.

Can an association collect unpaid assessments without filing a lawsuit?

Often, depending on the type of association and what its declaration authorizes. Many associations can suspend use of common elements such as pools, tennis courts or parking, may suspend voting rights, and in some cases can require a tenant of a delinquent owner to pay rent directly to the association.

What can an association do after it gets a judgment?

It can garnish the debtor’s wages and bank accounts, place judgment liens on real estate and personal property, and have the sheriff seize and sell property. Associations subject to the Georgia Condominium Act or the Georgia Property Owners Act usually have the right to foreclose on the delinquent property.

Which areas does the firm serve?

All of Metropolitan Atlanta and Northern Georgia, including Bartow, Carroll, Cherokee, Clayton, Cobb, Coweta, Dawson, DeKalb, Douglas, Fayette, Forsyth, Fulton, Gwinnett, Hall, Henry, Lumpkin, Paulding and Rockdale counties.
