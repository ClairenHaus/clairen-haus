Social Security Disability & SSI

Helping disabled individuals obtain the Social Security disability and Supplemental Security Income benefits to which they are entitled.

SSD and SSI: two different programs

If an injury or illness has kept you from working for 12 months or more, or is expected to, you may be eligible for Social Security disability (SSD) or Supplemental Security Income (SSI) benefits.

  • SSD is for people who worked and paid Social Security taxes before they became disabled. Eligibility depends on your work history.
  • SSI is a needs-based program for people with limited income and assets. It does not depend on work history.

Some people qualify for both. In either program, the Social Security Administration generally asks whether a medically determinable impairment prevents you from doing substantial work, and whether it has lasted or is expected to last at least 12 months.

The process, in general

  • Application. The claim is filed with the Social Security Administration, with medical and work information.
  • Reconsideration. If the claim is denied, you can generally ask for reconsideration, usually within 60 days.
  • Hearing. After a reconsideration denial, you can request a hearing before an administrative law judge, where evidence and testimony are presented.
  • Further appeals. Decisions can be appealed to the Appeals Council and then to federal court.

This is general information, not legal advice. Benefit amounts and income and asset limits change over time; the Social Security Administration publishes the current figures.
