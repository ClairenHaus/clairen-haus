Asset Protection

Structuring what you own so it is protected during your lifetime and passes to your family afterward.

Asset protection planning restructures what you own to reduce the chance of loss in a future lawsuit, while keeping you in control. Peck Ritchey's attorneys help families and business owners set goals that discourage litigation, encourage favorable settlements and keep assets beyond the reach of creditors, all coordinated with the estate plan.

The firm cautions against do-it-yourself asset protection kits, which rarely hold up because they miss the legal detail that makes a plan valid.

What we handle

  • Placing assets beyond the reach of creditors
  • Maintaining control of business interests
  • Providing income and support for family under all circumstances
  • Avoiding fraudulent conveyances
  • Avoiding probate and minimizing taxes
  • Coordinating asset protection with the estate plan

Common questions

What is asset protection planning?

It is the process of arranging your assets so they are less exposed to future lawsuits and creditor claims, while you keep control of them and they still pass to your family as you intend. It works alongside, not instead of, an estate plan.

Is it too late to protect assets once I have been sued?

Transfers made to avoid a known creditor can be reversed as fraudulent conveyances, so planning works best before a claim exists. The firm's planning specifically aims to avoid fraudulent conveyance problems.

Do business owners need separate asset protection?

Owners often do, because maintaining control of business interests and providing for family income are two of the goals the firm's asset protection planning addresses.
