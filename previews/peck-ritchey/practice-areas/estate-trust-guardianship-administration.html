Administration of Estates, Trusts & Guardianships

Guiding executors, trustees and guardians through probate, accountings, tax returns and distributions.

Serving as an executor, trustee or guardian is a legal responsibility, and the paperwork is unfamiliar to most people the first time. Peck Ritchey advises fiduciaries through the whole process, appearing in court as required, transferring assets, resolving claims and implementing the plan the decedent left.

The firm administers decedents' estates as well as guardianship estates for minors and disabled adults, prepares the required state and federal estate and inheritance tax returns, and works alongside the estate's accountants on fiduciary income tax matters.

What we handle

  • Probate of decedents' estates
  • Estate accountings and disclosures to beneficiaries
  • Executor and trustee representation
  • State and federal estate and inheritance tax returns
  • Post-mortem planning
  • Trust administration
  • Guardianship estates for minors and disabled adults

Common questions

What does an executor actually have to do?

In general terms, an executor gathers the estate's assets, files the required court documents and accountings, pays valid claims and taxes, and distributes what remains according to the will. Peck Ritchey guides executors through each of those steps and appears in court on their behalf as required.

Does the firm handle estate tax returns?

Yes. The firm routinely prepares the required state and federal estate and inheritance tax returns and coordinates with the estate's accountants on fiduciary income tax matters.

Can you help if I have been appointed guardian of a parent's estate?

Yes. The firm administers guardianship estates for disabled adults and minors, including the court filings and accountings a guardian is responsible for.
