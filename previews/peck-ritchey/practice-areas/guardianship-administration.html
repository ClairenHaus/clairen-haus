Guardianship Administration

Becoming and serving as guardian for a minor or an adult who cannot manage their own affairs.

A guardianship protects the welfare and rights of someone who cannot handle their own affairs, whether a minor or an adult with diminished capacity. Deciding to become a guardian is a serious step with real duties. Peck Ritchey helps families understand those duties, obtain the appointment and administer the guardianship afterward.

The firm's attorneys are frequently appointed by the court as guardian ad litem, trustee, executor and administrator, so they know the process from every side.

What we handle

  • Petitioning for guardianship of a minor or disabled adult
  • Guardianship of the person and of the estate
  • Court filings, reports and accountings
  • Guidance on a guardian's ongoing duties
  • Guardianship estates for minors and disabled adults

Common questions

How do I become someone's legal guardian in Illinois?

Guardianship is granted by a court. In general terms, a petition is filed, the court reviews evidence about the person's capacity, and a hearing is held before a guardian is appointed. The firm handles the petition, the hearing and the reporting that follows.

What is the difference between guardian of the person and guardian of the estate?

A guardian of the person makes decisions about care, housing and medical treatment. A guardian of the estate manages money and property. A court can appoint one person for both roles or different people for each.

Does a guardian have to report to the court?

Yes. Guardians have ongoing duties, including reports and accountings to the court. The firm advises guardians on those duties throughout the guardianship.
