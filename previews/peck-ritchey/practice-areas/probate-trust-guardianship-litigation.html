Probate, Trust & Guardianship Litigation

Contesting or defending a will, trust or guardianship, and recovering assets taken from an estate.

When a will, trust or guardianship is challenged, the outcome shapes a family for years. Peck Ritchey represents every side of these disputes: beneficiaries contesting a document, executors and trustees defending one, and families seeking to recover assets that were taken.

The firm's attorneys are recognized leaders in estate, trust and guardianship litigation. Managing partner Kerry Peck was asked by the Illinois Institute of Continuing Legal Education to author its chapters on will contests and guardianship.

  • Trust contests and trust construction, interpretation and reformation
  • Decedent's estate litigation

  • Citations to discover and recover assets
  • Lack of mental capacity
  • Removal of executors, trustees and agents under power of attorney
  • Theft, improper withdrawals and mismanagement
  • Disputed accountings, claims and heirship disputes
  • Total return trusts

Common questions

Can a will or trust be contested in Illinois?

Yes. A will or trust can be challenged in court, for example on the ground that the person lacked mental capacity or was unduly influenced when it was signed. Peck Ritchey represents both the people bringing a contest and the executors, trustees and beneficiaries defending one.

What is a citation to discover and recover assets?

It is a court proceeding used in Illinois probate and guardianship cases to find out what happened to property belonging to an estate or a ward, and to get it back when someone has concealed, embezzled or wrongly taken it. It is one of the tools the firm uses in estate and guardianship litigation.

Does the firm represent executors and trustees, or only people suing them?

Both. The firm represents all parties to estate, trust and guardianship litigation, including fiduciaries who need to defend their administration and beneficiaries who believe a fiduciary has breached a duty.
