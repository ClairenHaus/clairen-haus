Alimony Lawyer in Bergen County, NJ

Alimony in New Jersey comes in five distinct types, with the amount and length set by a dozen or so factors. Philip Puglisi has handled alimony cases throughout his career in Bergen County.

The five types of alimony

The original purpose of New Jersey’s alimony statutes included keeping a spouse in need from becoming a public charge. Over the years, through statute and case law, alimony has developed into five distinct types.

Open durational alimony

For marriages longer than 20 years, measured from the date of marriage to the filing of the divorce complaint. This long-term alimony was previously called permanent alimony.

Limited duration alimony

For marriages shorter than 20 years. Its length will be less than the length of the marriage; after a 10-year marriage, for example, it might run between five and eight years, depending on the circumstances.

Rehabilitative alimony

Helps a spouse regain the skills and training needed to become self-sufficient, such as paying for courses to return to a career. It may be in addition to regular alimony.

Reimbursement alimony

Repays a spouse who supported the other’s career, for example by helping pay for graduate school in the expectation of sharing the benefits of that education later in the marriage.

Pendente lite alimony

Temporary support while the divorce is pending, for example when the earning spouse refuses to contribute to the support of the other spouse and children after the complaint is filed. It maintains the status quo until a final award.

When alimony ends

Alimony terminates when either party dies or the supported spouse remarries, and depending on the circumstances, when the supported spouse cohabits with another person as New Jersey law defines it. Alimony can also be modified after a significant change in circumstances.

When one spouse is underemployed

An issue that comes up fairly often, during and after divorce, is a claim that one spouse is “underemployed”, earning less than he or she is able to. It matters because it bears on the amount of both alimony and child support.
