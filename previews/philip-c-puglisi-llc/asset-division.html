Asset Division & Equitable Distribution in New Jersey

When a New Jersey couple divorces, what was acquired during the marriage is divided equitably. Equitable means fairly, though most of the time it ends up equal.

What counts as a marital asset

Simply stated, anything acquired during the marriage, including:

  • The marital home and other real estate
  • Bank and brokerage accounts, stocks and bonds
  • Pensions, 401(k)s, IRAs and other retirement benefits
  • Furniture, furnishings and appliances
  • Cars and recreational vehicles
  • Jewelry, art, collectibles and electronics

The exceptions

Property acquired before the marriage, gifts from third parties and inheritances are excluded, but they may not stay excluded if they are commingled with marital assets. For example, if one spouse inherits money and deposits it in the couple’s joint account, it might be treated as a gift to the marriage and become subject to equitable distribution.

Pensions and retirement benefits

Retirement benefits earned during the marriage, including monthly payouts after retirement, are part of what gets divided. These assets often need careful valuation and the right paperwork to divide them properly.

How a court decides what is fair

If the case is litigated, New Jersey’s statute lists factors the court uses to determine a fair distribution, including the length of the marriage, the ages and health of the parties, the standard of living during the marriage, each party’s contribution to acquiring the assets, the parties’ debts and liabilities, and each party’s general economic circumstances.
