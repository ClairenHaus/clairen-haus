Child Support Lawyer in Bergen County, NJ

Every child has the right to share in the income of both parents. Philip Puglisi explains how New Jersey calculates support, when it ends, and how it is enforced.

How support is calculated

New Jersey uses the New Jersey Child Support Guidelines to set the support a non-custodial parent pays the custodial parent. Both parents must contribute to their children’s food, clothing, shelter, transportation, health insurance, unreimbursed health care and reasonable entertainment. Support is owed whether or not the parents were ever married.

The Guidelines produce a figure from information that includes:

  • each parent’s income, including alimony paid from one spouse to the other
  • the number of overnights each parent has with the children
  • income or benefits paid on a child’s behalf, such as Social Security
  • whether the paying parent has children from another relationship

Higher-income families

When the parents’ combined net income is above the top of the Guidelines, the court starts from the Guidelines figure and adds an amount for the excess, looking at the child’s needs, the standard of living the children were used to, both parents’ assets, income and earning ability, and the child’s education, including higher education.

When support ends

Child support ends when the child is emancipated. A New Jersey law that took effect in January 2016 ends support automatically when a child turns 19, with exceptions: for example, when the child is still in high school, enrolled full time in college or vocational school, or when an agreement sets a different date.

Enforcing a support order

When a parent stops paying, the other parent can apply to the court. Remedies include holding the delinquent parent in contempt, sanctions, an arrest warrant, seizing tax refunds or other benefits, and suspending a driver’s license after a hearing. If circumstances change after the divorce, support can also be modified.
