Domestic Violence & Restraining Orders in New Jersey

New Jersey takes domestic violence very seriously. Whether you need protection or have been accused, the hearing on a final restraining order comes quickly, and you have every right to be represented.

What counts as domestic violence

An act must have occurred, and it must have caused the plaintiff to fear for his or her safety and well-being. New Jersey courts use the criminal statutes to define the acts, which include assault, harassment, kidnapping, stalking, criminal restraint, terroristic threats and criminal trespass. The hard part for a court is often deciding whether an act is truly domestic violence or ordinary marital conflict, which the courts call “domestic contretemps”, particularly with harassment claims between spouses.

You do not have to be married to be a victim of domestic violence or to seek a restraining order. The person committing the act can be a current or former household member, or someone you are dating or have dated.

Temporary restraining orders

While the Superior Court is open, a person can go to the courthouse domestic violence unit, where a case worker helps complete the complaint and brings it before a judge, who decides whether to enter a Temporary Restraining Order (TRO). When the courts are closed, the local police respond, take the information and contact a Municipal Court judge, who can issue a TRO by telephone.

The final hearing

Either way, the case is listed for a hearing within ten days. While the TRO is in place, the accused party is barred from the marital residence, the victim’s workplace and other locations the victim requests. At the hearing, the judge takes testimony and decides whether to enter a Final Restraining Order (FRO). Both parties have every right to be represented by counsel, and should be.
