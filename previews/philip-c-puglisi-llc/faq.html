Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to the questions people ask most about divorce and family law in New Jersey, drawn from Philip Puglisi’s own guidance.

What are the ways to get divorced in New Jersey?

There are three avenues: a traditional litigated divorce filed in the Superior Court of New Jersey, Chancery Division, Family Part; divorce mediation; and the collaborative divorce process. Philip can handle your divorce whichever you choose.

Does New Jersey have legal separation?

No. New Jersey does not offer a legal separation, and the court will not sign an order declaring spouses separated. Spouses are free to separate physically for as long as they like, ideally under a written agreement covering finances, custody and parenting time.

How long does a divorce take in New Jersey?

It depends on how complex the issues are. If most issues are resolved or can be resolved quickly, a divorce can be completed in a matter of months. Some counties, including Bergen County, require a case to be completed, by settlement or trial, within one year of filing the complaint.

Will filing on fault grounds get me more in the divorce?

In most circumstances, no. A common misconception is that the court awards the non-faulting spouse more in assets or alimony. Fault filings are usually more expensive, because the spouse claiming fault has the burden of proof.

What should I bring to my first meeting?

Be ready to give an overview of your situation: current living arrangements, your overall financial picture, the children, a list of assets and who owns them, debts, employment history, any domestic violence or substance abuse issues, and your immediate and future goals. A written list of questions helps too.

What is a Case Information Statement?

The Case Information Statement, or CIS, is a required court filing that covers the marital history, the children, the lifestyle and monthly expenses during the marriage, and the marital assets and liabilities. It is needed whenever financial relief is sought from the court, and gathering the information early helps your attorney.

How much will my divorce cost?

You have every right to ask. It is hard to give a number without the whole picture: the lawyer is paid for time and advice, so a contested matter that needs more time costs more than a straightforward, uncontested one. At the first meeting, Philip explains the retainer the case will require.

Can my spouse be ordered to pay my legal fees?

Possibly. The court decides counsel fees case by case. Two major factors, besides the reasonableness of the fees, are whether one spouse is far better able to pay than the other, and whether the spouse asked to pay acted in good faith throughout the case.

Does a mediator give legal advice?

No. A mediator is neutral and is not permitted to advise either spouse on what to do or what the law is. You are free to have your own attorney advise you during mediation, and it is the better way to go.

Can I move out of New Jersey with my children?

Not without the other parent’s written consent or a court order. The court decides based on the children’s best interest, and a move is harder to obtain when the parents share an equal custodial arrangement.

When does child support end in New Jersey?

Under a law that took effect in January 2016, child support ends automatically when a child turns 19, with exceptions, such as when the child is still in high school, is enrolled full time in college or vocational school, or an agreement sets a different date.
