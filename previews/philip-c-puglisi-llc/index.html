Common questions

Questions people ask before they call.

Answered only from what the current site says. General information about New Jersey divorce, not legal advice.

What are the ways to get divorced in New Jersey?

The site describes three paths: a traditional litigated divorce filed in the Superior Court, Chancery Division, Family Part; divorce mediation; and the collaborative divorce process. Mr. Puglisi handles all three and explains them at the initial consultation.

Does filing on fault grounds get you more?

According to the site, no in most circumstances. Most New Jersey divorces are no-fault, based on irreconcilable differences, and fault-based filings are typically more expensive because the filing spouse carries the burden of proof.

Can a divorce be uncontested?

Yes, and the site calls it the preferred route. A matter is uncontested when the parties have reached agreement on all issues arising from the marriage.

How long does the other spouse have to respond to a divorce complaint?

The site explains that once a complaint is served, the other spouse has 35 days to respond with an answer, usually with a counterclaim.

Is the first consultation free?

Yes. The site offers a free half-hour consultation to explain the different avenues and strategies. Call (201) 431-8400.
