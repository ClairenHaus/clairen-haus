Divorce Mediation in Bergen County, NJ

Mediation lets you and your spouse decide the outcome yourselves, at your own pace and in private. Philip Puglisi is accredited by the New Jersey Supreme Court as a Family Law Mediator.

An experienced, accredited mediator

Philip is accredited by the New Jersey Supreme Court as a Family Law Mediator to mediate every aspect of a divorce, as well as post-divorce issues. For more than two decades he has been listed on both the Bergen County and Passaic County rosters of economic mediators, so litigated cases that are not resolving may be sent to him as mediator.

Why choose mediation?

  • It can save considerable time, money and aggravation compared with a case that moves through the court system.
  • You choose when to meet and how to proceed, instead of appearing whenever the court schedules a hearing.
  • You, not a judge, decide what is fair on assets and debts, spousal support, custody, parenting time and child support.
  • It encourages better communication and a more cooperative relationship afterwards, especially as parents.
  • Your finances and disagreements stay private, rather than being aired in a public courtroom.
  • Because you work through the agreement yourselves, you are more committed to it.

What a mediator does, and does not do

A common misconception is that the mediator advises the spouses on what to do or what the law is. That is not permitted. The mediator is neutral, and the role is to facilitate a resolution. A mediator sets an agenda, starts from the areas where you already agree, provides relevant information and examples from experience, clears up misunderstandings, and helps each of you see the other’s perspective without losing sight of your own.

You are free to retain your own attorney during mediation for advice along the way. It is not required, but it is the better way to go. Philip often represents a spouse who is mediating elsewhere and consults with him periodically for advice.

How the process starts and ends

Mediation can begin before, during or after a divorce has been filed, with or without attorneys. Some couples consult Philip as a Family Law Mediator just to learn what is involved before either of them retains counsel. If they decide to proceed, he explains his role, the retainer agreement, the costs and the approximate time the process might take.

At the end, the mediator drafts a “Memorandum of Understanding”, an outline of the issues the spouses have resolved. Each spouse takes it to his or her own attorney for review, and if it is acceptable, one attorney drafts the formal Marital Settlement Agreement.
