Prenuptial & Postnuptial Agreements in New Jersey

Couples marrying later in life, or for a second time, often want to decide in advance how assets and support would be handled. New Jersey has its own statute on these agreements, and it demands full disclosure.

Prenuptial agreements

Couples who are older and have been working for a while, or who are entering a second marriage, have often built up assets they want to keep separate if the marriage ends. One spouse may earn more and want to define the scope of any future alimony obligation. Spouses can agree on almost anything about what happens on separation or divorce, unless it violates public policy. Child support cannot be bargained away, for example, because the right to support belongs to the child, not the parents.

A prenuptial agreement can address:

  • Distribution of property
  • Keeping premarital property separate
  • Whether, and how much, spousal support is paid
  • What happens to the house
  • Whether and how business interests are divided
  • Inheritances and gifts from third parties
  • Property acquired during the marriage
  • Premarital and marital debt

Full disclosure

New Jersey requires absolute full disclosure of both parties’ assets, debts and income. The agreement must be in writing and signed by both, and each party should have separate counsel. Agreements are usually challenged on grounds of coercion or duress, fraud, or a failure to fully disclose assets, liabilities or income.

Postnuptial (mid-marriage) agreements

Spouses are free to enter into an agreement after they marry, known as a postnuptial or mid-marriage agreement. Courts scrutinize these more closely than premarital agreements, because there may be a greater chance that one spouse was coerced or unduly influenced while living together as one household. A postnuptial agreement is usually considered after important changes in the relationship, whether financial or because of marital strife.
