Collections

The firm represents individuals and businesses, large and small, collecting what they are owed, and defends people and businesses facing debt claims.

Collecting a debt

Start with a contract, an invoice, a promissory note or some other writing that shows the debt. Information about the debtor also helps: a current address, a copy of a check or payment they made to you, their Social Security number or place of employment. The more information you have, the better equipped the firm is to help you collect.

After judgment

Once you have a judgment, the next step is collecting it. The firm handles post-judgment work, which can include wage garnishments, wage executions, information subpoenas and foreclosures.

If you have been sued

If you are an individual or a business being pursued for a debt, the firm can defend the claim, including a claim for a debt you may not owe, and can try to negotiate the debt so you pay less than the amount demanded.

Fees. The firm offers flexible fee arrangements in collections. Depending on the case, that may be a contingent fee based on a percentage of the amount collected or the judgment, an hourly fee, or a flat fee.
