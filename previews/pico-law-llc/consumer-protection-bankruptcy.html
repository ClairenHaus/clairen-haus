Consumer protection and bankruptcy

Representation for people harmed by deceptive business practices or credit reporting errors, and guidance through Chapter 7 and Chapter 13 bankruptcy.

Consumer fraud

The firm represents individuals who are victims of consumer fraud by businesses using deceptive, misleading or unconscionable tactics. Under the New Jersey Consumer Fraud Act, fraud can be an untrue statement of fact, the omission or concealment of a material fact about a product, or a violation of one of many specific consumer protection laws. A consumer who succeeds under the Act can recover three times their actual damages plus attorney’s fees.

Cases include home improvement contractor fraud or breach of contract, automobile dealership fraud, unfair and deceptive trade practices, and abusive or improper collection tactics.

Credit reports and credit card companies

If your credit report contains false or incorrect information, or you have a significant problem with a credit card company, federal law may protect you. Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, damages and attorney’s fees can be awarded to the consumer in many cases.

Bankruptcy

Bankruptcy is the legal way for a person or business that owes more than it can pay to reduce or eliminate debts, either by repaying over time or by having debts discharged. Any person, partnership, corporation or sole proprietorship may file, and U.S. citizenship is not required.

  • Chapter 7 is a liquidation bankruptcy that can eliminate most unsecured debt, such as credit cards and medical bills.
  • Chapter 13 is a repayment plan, usually three to five years long depending on income, that can stop foreclosure and sheriff’s sales and prevent repossession and utility shutoffs, while letting many people keep important assets such as retirement savings, homes and cars.

Beyond guiding you through the process, the firm will advise you on the best course of action for your situation.
