Elder law

Planning that keeps up with your life as it changes: protecting assets, caring for the people you love, and keeping the level of control you want.

What elder law covers

  • Asset protection and Medicaid planning
  • Simple and complex Wills
  • Powers of Attorney and health care directives
  • Special needs trusts and inter vivos trusts; revocable vs. irrevocable trusts
  • Elder abuse and undue influence
  • Guardianship and conservatorship
  • Age discrimination

Plans that change as you do

Every client’s needs should be reviewed carefully, and adjusted as they age. Children grow up, problems arise for the loved ones you want to keep caring for, and financial needs can become more challenging over time. Decisions have to be made about whether you will keep managing your own affairs or have help now or in the near future. Transferring or gifting assets may or may not be right for you, so get advice before you act.

Elder law can draw on lifetime transfers, gifts, life insurance and your Last Will and Testament. The goal is to work through all of your assets and intended beneficiaries with you, and to preserve as much of your estate as possible while you keep the control, and the assets, you need for a comfortable life.

Deborah L. Pico is a member of the National Academy of Elder Law Attorneys, Inc. (NAELA). Elder law matters are usually billed hourly; in some instances a flat fee may be charged.
