Estate planning and estate litigation

Estate planning is an important part of your overall life plan. The firm helps you decide what your estate needs, and stands up for your rights as an heir when an estate is contested.

A plan for your assets and your heirs

Working out what your estate needs takes time and experience. Depending on your conversations with the firm, your plan may include a Last Will and Testament, a Power of Attorney, a Living Will or Medical Directive, a revocable or irrevocable inter vivos trust, a special needs trust, and other estate planning tools.

The point is to plan what will happen to your assets and your heirs when you are no longer here, or if you become disabled or incapacitated. Each person’s life is different, and your plan should fit your own situation.

Probate and estate administration

The firm handles probate and estate administration matters: probating a Last Will and Testament, or the estate of someone who died intestate (without a Will). Administration covers transferring assets under the Will as well as non-probate transfers that pass outside the Will, or without one.

Assets in more than one state

Each state has its own probate laws, and a decedent often has assets in more than one state. When that happens an ancillary probate is needed, handled by an attorney licensed in the state where the asset sits. Because Deborah L. Pico is licensed in both New Jersey and New York, the firm can handle these ancillary probates without you having to retain separate local counsel.

Estate litigation

The firm also handles estate litigation, and will defend your rights as an heir when necessary. The goal throughout is for the decedent’s assets to be distributed properly, promptly, and with as little tax consequence as possible.

Fees. Estate matters are usually billed hourly. In some instances a flat fee may be available.
