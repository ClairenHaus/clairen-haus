Frequently asked questions

Answers drawn from the firm’s own practice: where it is, what it handles, how fees work, and how probate and closings begin.

Where is the firm located?

The office is at 530 Main Street, Suite 200, Fort Lee, NJ 07024, in Bergen County. The firm also has an office in lower Manhattan.

Which states does Deborah L. Pico practice in?

She is licensed to practice law in New Jersey and New York, and is admitted to practice before the United States Supreme Court.

How long has the firm been practicing?

Deborah L. Pico opened her first law firm, Myers & Pico, P.C., in 1984, and clients have been coming to the practice for 40 years. Today the practice is The Law Offices of Deborah L. Pico, LLC in Fort Lee.

What kinds of cases does the firm handle?

It is a general practice concentrating on residential and commercial real estate (including co-ops and condos), estate planning, Wills and trusts, probate, estate litigation, elder law, small business and corporate law, and collections. It also handles short sales, foreclosures, consumer protection and bankruptcy matters.

How are fees charged?

It depends on the matter. Real estate matters are usually a flat fee, with some transactions billed hourly. Estate planning, probate and elder law are usually hourly, and a flat fee may be available in some instances. Collections can be handled on a contingent, hourly or flat fee basis.

Can the firm probate an estate that has assets in both New Jersey and New York?

Yes. When a decedent has assets in more than one state, an ancillary probate is needed, handled by an attorney licensed where the asset is located. Because the firm is licensed in both New Jersey and New York, it can handle these without you retaining separate local counsel.

What do I need to start probating an estate?

The original death certificate and the original Last Will and Testament. If you are named Executor in the Will, the Surrogate in the county where the decedent lived can issue Letters Testamentary. If there is no Will, the next of kin can ask the Surrogate for Letters of Administration.

Does the firm handle co-op purchases?

Yes, in New Jersey and New York, for individual buyers and sellers and for co-op and condo boards. Buying a co-op means buying shares in a corporation and receiving a proprietary lease, which works differently from buying a house or condo.

Can I just use a Will or trust form from the internet?

The firm’s view is that a generic, “cookie cutter” document can do a disservice to you and your loved ones. Your documents should reflect your own family, assets and goals, and you should understand the consequences of what you sign.

Is Deborah L. Pico a member of any elder law organization?

Yes. She is a member of the National Academy of Elder Law Attorneys, Inc. (NAELA), as well as the Bergen County Bar Association and the American Bar Association.

Does the firm represent lenders or borrowers in foreclosures?

Both. The firm has represented hard money lenders and traditional banks, represents homeowners facing foreclosure, and represents buyers of foreclosed properties.
