Deborah L. Pico, Esq. has been a New Jersey resident her entire life. She graduated from Montclair State College in 1979 with a Bachelor of Arts in Political Science, then from the Benjamin N. Cardozo School of Law in New York City with her Juris Doctor in 1982.

Licensed in New Jersey and New York, she opened her first law firm, Myers & Pico, P.C., with Lois A. Myers, Esq. in 1984. That firm operated until Ms. Pico opened The Law Offices of Deborah L. Pico, LLC, now located in Fort Lee, NJ, with an office in New York City. She has also taught adult-education classes on the law as an adjunct professor.

The firm describes itself as a general practice highlighting real estate and estate planning, providing legal services for reasonable fees, and standing by its reputation for good service, communication and cost-effective problem solving.

Common questions

Answers drawn from the firm’s own site.

Question-and-answer content, marked up as FAQ schema, is the format search engines and AI assistants quote directly.

Are you licensed in both New Jersey and New York?

Yes. The firm is licensed to practice law in New Jersey and New York, with its office in Fort Lee, NJ and an office in New York City.

Do you handle co-op and condominium closings?

Yes. The firm handles cooperative and condominium purchases and sales in both New Jersey and New York and represents co-op and condo boards. It notes that a co-op is not real estate but shares of stock with a proprietary lease, so it calls for an attorney who understands cooperatives.

What is ancillary probate?

When a decedent held assets in more than one state, a separate ancillary probate is needed in the other state, handled by an attorney licensed there. Because the firm is licensed in New Jersey and New York, it says this situation comes up often.

Does elder law include Medicaid planning?

Yes. The firm lists asset protection and Medicaid planning among its elder law services, alongside wills, powers of attorney, health care directives, special needs trusts, guardianship and conservatorship.

Which business entities can you help set up?

The firm assists in choosing among a Sub-Chapter S corporation, a business partnership, a limited liability company or a C corporation, and prepares shareholder, partner, confidentiality and non-compete agreements.
