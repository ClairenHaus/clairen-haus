Why a general practice

Seeing the whole picture, not just one corner of it

You may come in for one legal problem and discover, in the first consultation, that there are others that need attention. Because the firm has handled most areas of the law at one time or another, it can look at your situation from all sides and point out how a step in one area may affect another, such as how buying a home may change your estate tax picture, or how a divorce may affect your Will.

When a matter calls for it, the firm will co-counsel with experts in the field, with your approval. No problem is too small to review.

Two states, one attorney

When your home is in one state and your assets are in the other

Many clients live in New Jersey and own property in New York, or the reverse. Because the firm is licensed in both, it can resolve issues that cross the river, including ancillary probate for a loved one who left assets in both states, without bringing in separate local counsel.

  • Real estate closings in New Jersey and New York
  • Co-op and condominium purchases, sales and boards
  • Probate before the Surrogate in New Jersey and New York counties
  • Wills and trusts that account for assets in both states

All questions
Which states does Deborah L. Pico practice in?

She is licensed to practice law in New Jersey and New York, and is admitted to practice before the United States Supreme Court.

How are fees charged?

It depends on the matter. Real estate matters are usually a flat fee, with some transactions billed hourly. Estate planning, probate and elder law are usually hourly, and a flat fee may be available in some instances. Collections can be handled on a contingent, hourly or flat fee basis.

Can the firm probate an estate that has assets in both New Jersey and New York?

Yes. When a decedent has assets in more than one state, an ancillary probate is needed, handled by an attorney licensed where the asset is located. Because the firm is licensed in both New Jersey and New York, it can handle these without you retaining separate local counsel.
