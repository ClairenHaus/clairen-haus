Estate probate and administration in New Jersey and New York

When a loved one passes, the estate has to be probated before assets can go to the beneficiaries and debts can be paid. The firm guides executors, administrators and beneficiaries through each step.

How probate begins

Every county in New Jersey and New York has a Surrogate, and an estate must be probated in the county where the decedent lived at the time of death. To begin, you need the original death certificate and the original Last Will and Testament.

  • If there is a Will: you must be named as Executor in it to probate the estate and obtain Letters Testamentary appointing you Executor.
  • If there is no Will: you will need to be the next of kin and ask the Surrogate to issue Letters of Administration appointing you Administrator.

The firm has practiced in most counties in New Jersey and New York and knows the nuances of each county’s Surrogate.

After the appointment

The Executor follows the terms of the Will, pays debts, distributes assets to the beneficiaries and closes the estate. Without a Will, state law sets out who inherits and which debts are paid. Either way, the decedent’s assets have to be gathered, debts identified, and a formal or informal accounting prepared, depending on the estate.

When probate gets complicated

The original Will may be lost and only a copy found. The Executor may not want to serve. The Will may have been signed under duress, or be handwritten. Heirs may have died, leaving the order of inheritance unclear. In situations like these, representation helps make sure the estate is handled properly and your rights are protected, whether you are the Executor, the Administrator or a beneficiary. If a lawsuit is needed to protect your rights, the firm handles probate litigation.

Taxes

Whether inheritance tax, estate tax or federal tax is due depends on many factors: each beneficiary’s relationship to the decedent, the value of the estate, the type of assets, and whether assets carry a named beneficiary. In some estates this is complex, which is why bringing in a probate attorney early matters for any Executor or Administrator.
