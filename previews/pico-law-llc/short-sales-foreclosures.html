Short sales and foreclosures

The firm represents homeowners negotiating short sales, and lenders, borrowers and buyers in foreclosure matters.

Short sales

A short sale happens when the loans against a property are greater than what it can be sold or refinanced for. It is a way for homeowners to avoid foreclosure by paying their lenders less than the full payoff.

Negotiating one is difficult: it can be hard to find the bank officer with authority to accept the discount, and then that officer has to be persuaded to understand your situation. Lenders are not required to discount a payoff, and there is no guarantee a lender will allow a short sale. Lenders tend to be more willing to negotiate when payments are in arrears, and if you have cash assets, a lender may try to reach them. An experienced guide helps you give the lender what it needs to evaluate your financial situation.

Representing lenders

Hard money lenders and traditional banks have been clients of the firm over the years. If your borrower has defaulted, the firm can pursue foreclosure on the mortgage and a lawsuit on the promissory note. Often it makes sense to sue on both at the same time, because some damages cannot be recovered in the foreclosure alone. Where appropriate, the firm will also recommend updates to your lending documents to avoid problems in the future.

Representing borrowers and buyers

If you are being foreclosed upon, the firm uses the legal strategies available to try to keep you in your home. In the right circumstances, a counterclaim can be brought against the lender for violations such as predatory lending, the Consumer Fraud Act, the Truth in Lending Act, the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, the Uniform Commercial Code, breach of contract, or fraud and misrepresentation. The firm also represents prospective purchasers of foreclosed properties.
