Wills, trusts, powers of attorney and living wills

The documents you put in place are the essence of protecting your assets now and in the future. They should be written for you, not copied from a template.

Documents the firm prepares

  • Last Wills and Testaments
  • Living Wills and advance directives
  • Powers of Attorney
  • Inter vivos (living) and testamentary trusts
  • Revocable and irrevocable trusts
  • Special needs trusts and Medicaid trusts
  • Asset and estate planning
  • Litigation relating to Wills and estates

When a trust may be the answer

You may want to protect minor children, or adults, from accessing funds from your estate. You may want to protect your assets from creditors, or plan for the possibility of a divorce. You may decide to transfer a family home or an investment property into a trust during your lifetime. Each of these calls for an individual analysis and advice tailored to you.

Why not a form from the internet?

Many clients arrive with a firm idea of what they need, and it is often off the mark. There is a lot to think about when creating these documents: what options exist, what the consequences are, and how the documents affect your life and your estate. A generic, “cookie cutter” document can do a disservice to you and your loved ones. Doing the documents correctly, in the best way for your situation, helps ensure your assets are protected and distributed according to your wishes.

New Jersey and New York

Because the firm practices in both states, it can take your situation into account if you live in one state and have assets in the other. If a dispute over a Will cannot be resolved amicably, litigation can be brought to enforce or challenge the estate planning documents.
