Business Formation

Help choosing a business structure, forming the entity and putting the founding documents in place, from attorneys who also handle the disputes that poorly drafted documents can cause.

What this includes

  • Choosing an entity type
  • Forming LLCs and corporations
  • Operating agreements and bylaws
  • Agreements between owners
  • Contracts the new business will need

Starting on solid ground

The structure you choose for a business affects who is personally responsible for its debts, how profits are taxed, how decisions are made and what happens when an owner wants out. In Alabama the common choices are a limited liability company, a corporation or a partnership, and each is formed by filing with the state and adopting governing documents.

What the firm helps with

  • Talking through the options for your situation and goals
  • Preparing and filing formation documents
  • Drafting operating agreements, bylaws and agreements between owners, including what happens if an owner leaves, dies or wants to sell
  • Reviewing leases, vendor agreements and other contracts the new business will sign

Because Randy and Fran Quarles also litigate commercial disputes and draft and review contracts, they know where founding documents tend to cause problems later, and they can write with those problems in mind.

Tax consequences of an entity choice are best confirmed with your accountant; the firm can work alongside them.
