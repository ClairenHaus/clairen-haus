Grounds for divorce in Alabama

Alabama recognizes 12 grounds for divorce. Two are “no-fault” grounds, “incompatibility of temperament” and “irretrievable breakdown of the marriage”, which do not require proof that either spouse did anything wrong. The other ten are “fault” grounds, such as adultery and abandonment. Because many fault grounds are difficult to prove, no-fault grounds are typically included in a divorce complaint, with or without fault grounds.

Alternatives to trial

  • Uncontested divorce. The spouses agree on every issue and the court enters a judgment based on their agreement.
  • Mediation. A mediator, usually a lawyer who represents neither side, guides settlement talks. An agreement reached in mediation is then filed as an uncontested divorce.
  • Collaborative divorce. The parties, their attorneys and non-lawyer professionals work out the terms away from court. Fran Quarles is trained in collaborative law.

Alimony

Alimony is court-ordered spousal support. It can be awarded as a lump sum (“alimony in gross”) or as periodic payments, and a court can also award temporary alimony while the case is pending. In setting the amount, courts look at factors including the length of the marriage, the parties’ age and health, their standard of living, their future prospects and their conduct leading to the divorce.

Common questions

I just received a complaint for divorce from my spouse. What do I do now?

Contact a divorce lawyer as soon as possible. You have only 30 days to respond to the complaint with a motion, answer, or answer and counterclaim. If you do not file an answer, your spouse may obtain a default judgment against you.

Will a jury decide my divorce?

No. A divorce is heard only by a judge in a “bench” trial. Before the final trial, a “special master,” usually an attorney who practices divorce law, may hold a hearing on a request for temporary custody or support.

Where do I file?

When both spouses live in Alabama, the complaint is generally filed in the county where the defendant lives or the county where the parties lived when they separated. If the defendant no longer lives in Alabama, it is filed where the plaintiff lives.
