Frequently asked questions

Divorce, custody and support, answered plainly.

Answers to common questions about divorce, child support and child custody in Alabama, from the attorneys at Quarles Law Firm, LLC.

About divorce

What are the grounds for divorce in Alabama?

Alabama has 12 grounds for divorce. Two are “no-fault” grounds, “incompatibility of temperament” and “irretrievable breakdown of the marriage”, and ten are “fault” grounds, such as adultery and abandonment.

Do I need to prove my spouse was at fault?

No. You may ask for and obtain a no-fault divorce on grounds of incompatibility of temperament or irretrievable breakdown of the marriage. These grounds do not require proof that a spouse did anything wrong.

What is a “fault” divorce?

One in which misconduct by the husband or wife is found to have caused the end of the marriage. Fault grounds in Alabama include adultery, abandonment, imprisonment, habitual drunkenness or drug addiction, violence or reasonable apprehension of violence, and separation without support, among others. Many are difficult to prove, so no-fault grounds are typically included in a divorce complaint.

Do I need a lawyer to get divorced?

No, but the legal and procedural requirements must be handled correctly, and completing the process can be very difficult without training in divorce law. You can also lose valuable rights if no one is looking out for your interests.

I don’t want to go to court. Are there alternatives to a trial?

Yes: an uncontested divorce, mediation, or collaborative divorce. In mediation, a mediator (usually a lawyer who represents neither side) guides settlement talks; an agreement is then filed as an uncontested divorce. In collaborative divorce, the parties, their attorneys and non-lawyer professionals in finance and counseling resolve the issues away from court. Fran Quarles is trained in collaborative law.

What is alimony, and how is the amount decided?

Alimony is court-ordered spousal support, paid as a lump sum (“alimony in gross”) or as periodic payments; a court can also award temporary alimony while the case is pending. Factors include the length of the marriage, the parties’ age and health, their standard of living, their future prospects, and their conduct leading to the divorce.

Where do I file for divorce?

When both spouses live in Alabama, generally in the county where the defendant lives or the county where the parties lived when they separated. If the defendant no longer lives in Alabama, in the county where the plaintiff lives. If the parties agree, or the defendant waives any objection, the complaint may be filed in any county.

I just received a divorce complaint. What do I do now?

Contact a divorce lawyer as soon as possible. You have only 30 days to respond with a motion, answer, or answer and counterclaim. If you do not answer, your spouse may obtain a default judgment. With a counterclaim, you ask the court to grant the divorce on your terms, for example on custody or the division of property.

Will a jury decide my divorce?

No. A divorce is heard only by a judge in a “bench” trial. Before the final trial, a special master, usually an attorney who practices divorce law, may hold a hearing on a request for temporary custody or support.

Can my spouse or I stop a divorce once it has been filed?

If one spouse does not want to stay married, the parties ultimately will be divorced. But at any time after filing, the parties could reconcile and withdraw the case by consent, or settle and shorten the process.

Am I stuck with the same court order forever?

Maybe not. Either former spouse can seek to modify custody, child support or spousal support when there is a substantial change of circumstances. Changes the court has not approved, even ones your ex-spouse agrees to, may not be enforceable later.

About child support

How is child support calculated?

Using Alabama’s child support guidelines, which consider the number of minor children, both parents’ gross income (minus support already paid for prior children), health insurance and daycare costs. Support can deviate somewhat from the guidelines in some circumstances. It is generally paid until the child turns 19, but can be extended, for example for a child with a disability or by agreement to cover college costs.

Can child support be changed?

Yes. Until the child turns 19, support can be increased or decreased. The parent seeking the change must file a petition to modify and show a material change in circumstances, such as a job loss or a substantial raise.

My ex-spouse has stopped paying child support. What can I do?

File an action to enforce the support judgment. A parent who does not pay can be held in contempt, which can bring fines, probation and potential jail time. An income-withholding order directing an employer to deduct support from wages is often a simple, relatively inexpensive collection method.

I lost my job and can’t pay my child support. What should I do?

Contact an attorney instead of hiding from the problem. Unpaid support can lead to judgment liens and wage garnishment, it cannot be discharged in bankruptcy, and the longer you wait to seek a modification, the more you will owe.

I live out of state, and my ex-spouse moved to Alabama and stopped paying. Can I enforce my order here?

Yes. Alabama has adopted the Uniform Interstate Family Support Act, which lets an Alabama court enforce a support order from another state. The enforcement action is filed in the county where your ex-spouse lives.

If DHR is enforcing my ex-spouse’s support, do I need my own lawyer?

You have the right to hire a private attorney even if the Department of Human Resources filed the action. DHR does not represent the parent seeking enforcement, and it does not represent you if your ex-spouse responds with a counterclaim or contempt motion against you.

About child custody

How does the court decide where my children will live?

Based on the children’s best interests. Common arrangements are sole custody with visitation, joint custody, and joint custody with a primary physical custodian, in which both parents share decisions but the child lives mainly with one parent.

When can my children decide where they want to live?

In Alabama, the court always makes the custody decision. The judge may hear from your child and consider his or her wishes, but the judge decides.

Can I relocate if I have custody?

Under Alabama law, a custodial parent generally cannot move more than 60 miles away (or out of state) from the other parent without at least 45 days’ advance written notice by certified mail. The other parent has 30 days to object in court; if they do, the judge decides whether the move is in the child’s best interest. Different notice rules apply to an active-duty service member relocating under a non-voluntary government order.

If my ex-spouse doesn’t pay child support, do I still have to allow visitation?

Yes. Support and visitation are separate issues, and you can be held in contempt for denying visitation. File a contempt proceeding to enforce the support order instead.

Do I have to pay child support if my ex-spouse won’t let me see the children?

Yes. Keep paying, and file a contempt proceeding to enforce your visitation rights. Otherwise you could face a contempt action for failing to pay.
