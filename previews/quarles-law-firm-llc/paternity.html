Paternity

Legal help establishing a child’s parentage, so that custody, visitation and support can be decided by a court.

What this includes

  • Establishing paternity
  • Custody and visitation for unmarried parents
  • Child support after paternity is established

Why paternity matters

When parents are not married, a child’s legal father is not always established automatically. Establishing paternity gives a court the basis to decide custody and visitation, order child support, and secure the child’s rights to things like inheritance and benefits through both parents.

How it is generally done

  • Both parents may acknowledge paternity voluntarily, or
  • Either parent may ask the court to determine paternity, which may involve genetic testing.

Once paternity is established, the same custody, visitation and support questions arise as in a divorce, and the firm’s experience with child custody and child support applies.
