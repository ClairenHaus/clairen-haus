About the practice

The information clients need before they call.

First client conference at no cost; flat fees quoted for estate planning work

The firm describes offering individuals, families and small to mid-sized companies a blend of the experience found at large law firms with the individualized attention and cost effectiveness of a small one. Clients call on it for sound advice, fast turnaround, competent representation and reasonable fees.

On estate planning, the firm's stated mission is to understand each client's family dynamics and finances, set clear goals, show recommended strategies with flowcharts and spreadsheets, review the drafted documents together, and keep assisting after signing, including asset transfers and updates as estate, trust, gift and tax laws change.

Preview content is based on publicly available information and is subject to the firm's review before publication. Images, final copy, domain connection, forms, and accessibility checks are completed during the paid build.

Questions clients ask

Answers drawn from the firm's own site.

Short, direct answers are what people and AI assistants look for first. Each one below comes from information the firm already publishes.

How does Reiter Law Firm charge for estate planning?

The firm says most of its clients prefer a flat fee for estate planning rather than an hourly rate, and that it prefers this method too. The first client conference is at no cost; the firm then quotes a flat fee, or in complex matters a reasonable hourly rate with an estimated range of hours, set out in a written retainer agreement.

Where are the firm's offices?

The main office is at 135 Chestnut Ridge Road #200, Montvale, NJ 07645, phone (845) 357-2215. The firm also has offices at 808 Columbus Ave, New York, NY 10025, phone (212) 666-3197, and 9227 East Rusty Spur Place, Scottsdale, AZ 85255, phone (480) 998-7493.

In which states does the firm practice?

The firm serves New York, New Jersey, Arizona and the District of Columbia. Managing partner Arnold E. Reiter is admitted in all four.

Does the firm handle estate administration after a death?

Yes. Estate administration, commonly referred to as probate, is a listed practice area alongside estate planning and estate litigation.
