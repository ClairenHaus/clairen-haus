DWI / DUI Defense

Driving while intoxicated charges, and the driver’s license issues that come with them. Robbie Barker handles these cases in Conroe, Montgomery County and Harris County.

A DWI charge is two cases

An arrest for driving while intoxicated in Texas usually starts two separate matters: the criminal case in court, and an administrative case over your driver’s license. A first DWI is generally a Class B misdemeanor, but prior convictions, a high breath or blood test result, a child passenger or an accident causing injury can raise the charge, in some situations to a felony.

Watch the license deadline

If you refused a breath or blood test, or the result was over the legal limit, the State can move to suspend your license through a process called Administrative License Revocation (ALR). You generally have only 15 days from receiving the notice of suspension to request a hearing. Missing that deadline can mean an automatic suspension. If your license is suspended, an occupational driver’s license may let you keep driving to work and other essential places.

Questions a defense looks at

  • Whether the traffic stop was lawful
  • How field sobriety tests were given and recorded
  • How breath or blood samples were taken, stored and tested
  • What the video from the patrol car and the jail shows
