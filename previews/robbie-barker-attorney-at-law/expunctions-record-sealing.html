Expunctions & Record Sealing

Clearing or sealing a criminal record when Texas law allows it. Robbie Barker handles these cases in Conroe, Montgomery County and Harris County.

Two ways to put a record behind you

Texas law offers two main tools for people with a criminal record. An expunction erases the records of an arrest, so that in most situations you can deny the arrest ever happened. An order of nondisclosure seals a record from the public, including most employers and landlords, though it remains visible to law enforcement and certain agencies.

Who may qualify

Eligibility depends on how the case ended. Expunctions are generally available when a person was acquitted, when charges were dismissed or never filed and certain conditions are met, and in some other limited situations. Nondisclosure is often available after successfully completing deferred adjudication, and for some first-time offenses, after a waiting period. Some offenses can never be sealed. Each case needs to be checked against the current law.

How the process works

Clearing a record means filing a petition in the right court, notifying the agencies that hold records about the case, and in some cases attending a hearing. Once an order is granted, those agencies must remove or seal the records.
