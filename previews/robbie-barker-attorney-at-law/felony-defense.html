Felony Defense

Defense for felony charges at every level, from state jail felonies to first-degree and capital cases. Robbie Barker handles these cases in Conroe, Montgomery County and Harris County.

What makes a charge a felony in Texas

Texas sorts felonies into five levels: capital felonies, first-, second- and third-degree felonies, and state jail felonies. The level depends on the offense and its circumstances, and in some cases on a person’s prior record. A felony conviction can mean time in prison or state jail, and it follows a person long after the sentence ends, affecting jobs, housing, firearm rights and professional licenses.

How a felony case moves

Most felony cases in Texas go to a grand jury, which decides whether to return an indictment. After that come court settings, the exchange of evidence, motions, and in many cases negotiations with the prosecutor. Some cases are dismissed, some are resolved by agreement, and some go to trial before a jury. Decisions made early, including those about bond and what to say to investigators, can matter a great deal.
