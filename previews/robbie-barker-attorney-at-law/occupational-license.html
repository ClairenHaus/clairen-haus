Occupational Driver’s License

A restricted license so you can keep driving to work and other essentials during a suspension. Robbie Barker handles these cases in Conroe, Montgomery County and Harris County.

Keep driving to work during a suspension

A driver’s license suspension can make it hard to keep a job, get to school or take care of a family. Texas law lets many drivers whose licenses are suspended ask a court for an occupational driver’s license: a restricted license that allows driving for essential needs such as work, school and household duties, within limits the court sets.

What the court looks at

A judge considers why the license was suspended and why you need to drive. Courts commonly set conditions such as approved hours and routes, proof of insurance, and in alcohol-related cases an ignition interlock device. Some suspensions require a waiting period before a restricted license can be granted.

When it comes up

  • A suspension after a DWI arrest or conviction
  • A suspension after refusing a breath or blood test
  • Suspensions for other offenses or driving record issues
