Probation Issues

Problems on probation, including motions to revoke and motions to adjudicate. Robbie Barker handles these cases in Conroe, Montgomery County and Harris County.

Probation in Texas

Probation in Texas is called community supervision. It comes with conditions, such as reporting to a probation officer, paying fees, completing classes or community service, and staying out of new trouble. A missed appointment, a failed drug test or a new arrest can put probation at risk.

Motions to revoke and motions to adjudicate

When the State believes a condition has been violated, it can ask the court to act. If you are on regular probation, the prosecutor files a motion to revoke; if you are on deferred adjudication, it files a motion to adjudicate guilt. Either can lead to a hearing before the judge, and the possible outcomes range from continuing probation with new conditions to a jail or prison sentence. These hearings move quickly and follow different rules from a trial, so it pays to act early.

Other probation questions

  • Changing or adding probation conditions
  • Ending probation early when the law allows
  • Warrants issued for alleged violations
