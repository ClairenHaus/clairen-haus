Frequently asked questions

Answers drawn from the firm’s own guidance on buying a home and working with the firm.

Buying a home

When should I involve a real estate attorney in a home purchase?

The firm advises retaining an attorney from the very beginning, and certainly before you enter into a binding contract of purchase. Even if an attorney is not involved before the contract is signed, it is advisable to have your attorney involved in your settlement.

Is a standard real estate form contract safe to sign as written?

There is no such thing as a “typical” form contract. Almost every firm’s contract has its own provisions that affect the rights and obligations of both purchasers and sellers. A one- or two-page form may seem harmless, but it usually is not, so every provision should be read and understood, ideally with an attorney reviewing it or drafting a contract with your interests in mind.

Doesn’t the real estate agent represent me?

By law, absent a written agreement between the purchasers and the real estate agent, the agent owes his or her primary duty to the sellers, who pay the agent’s commission.

Does a home purchase contract have to be in writing?

Yes. A contract to purchase a home must be in writing to be enforceable by a court; oral agreements not evidenced by a written document are not binding. A contract signed by only one owner, when the property is held by more than one, may not be legally binding either.

Title and settlement

What does the attorney check about the title?

Old records are reviewed to make sure there is no cloud on the seller’s title, such as forgeries or hidden liens. Your attorney makes sure the sellers actually hold good title and have the right to transfer it, and reviews covenants, easements, restrictions and any unpaid real estate taxes.

Do I need an owner’s title insurance policy?

In most cases in Virginia, the lender will require the purchasers to pay for a lender’s title insurance policy. Whether to also buy an owner’s policy to cover your own interests is a question to ask your attorney.

How should we take title to the property?

It depends on your situation, and the language must be precise. For example, a husband and wife may take title as “tenants by the entirety with the common law right of survivorship,” so the survivor takes full title if one dies; two unrelated people may take title as “tenants in common,” each owning a share they can convey independently.

What should I bring to settlement?

Purchasers are generally required to bring the balance of the down payment and the required closing costs by certified or cashier’s check or wired funds. Check with the settlement agent ahead of time so there is enough time to put the money in the proper form.

How quickly are funds disbursed after settlement?

In Virginia, the Wet Settlement Act requires, among other things, that the settlement attorney disburse all funds collected at settlement to the proper people within two business days after the funds are collected and deposited in the attorney’s escrow account.

Will I know the attorney’s charges in advance?

Yes. The attorney’s charges for representing the purchasers are estimated in advance.
