Purchasing your home

Not a “fill-in-the-blanks” procedure.

For most people, buying a home is the largest single investment of their lives. Here is how your attorney helps at each stage.

  1. The contract

    Review the contract you are handed, or draft one with your interests in mind, before you are bound by it.

  2. Financing

    Make sure the contractual arrangements with your lender are legal and fair, and that the contract’s terms are followed.

  3. Title

    Examine the title so there is no cloud on it: no forgeries, hidden liens, unpaid taxes or surprise restrictions.

  4. Settlement

    Draft and review the settlement papers, explain the accounts, record the documents and disburse the funds.

Common questions
When should I involve a real estate attorney in a home purchase?

The firm advises retaining an attorney from the very beginning, and certainly before you enter into a binding contract of purchase. Even if an attorney is not involved before the contract is signed, it is advisable to have your attorney involved in your settlement.

Is a standard real estate form contract safe to sign as written?

There is no such thing as a “typical” form contract. Almost every firm’s contract has its own provisions that affect the rights and obligations of both purchasers and sellers. A one- or two-page form may seem harmless, but it usually is not, so every provision should be read and understood, ideally with an attorney reviewing it or drafting a contract with your interests in mind.

Doesn’t the real estate agent represent me?

By law, absent a written agreement between the purchasers and the real estate agent, the agent owes his or her primary duty to the sellers, who pay the agent’s commission.
