Primary focus of the practice

Residential real estate & closings

New home closings require attention to detail. The firm’s experienced staff work to make sure it’s right the first time.

The second most important decision

When you are looking for a new home, the first decision is location, location, location. The second most important decision is who will lead you through the legal process of closing the deal properly. This is not a “fill-in-the-blanks” procedure.

Your purchase of a home will require you to take on major financial and contractual responsibility. Ms. Allen will make sure you understand all the facts before you make a final commitment.

For the best protection, retain an attorney from the very beginning, certainly before entering into a binding contract of purchase. Even if an attorney is not involved before the contract is signed, it is advisable to have your attorney involved in your settlement.

Questions every purchaser should ask

  • Exactly what real and personal property is included in the purchase?
  • How should we take title?
  • What financing and terms of payment can be arranged?
  • Are any liens filed against the property? Are real estate taxes paid to date?
  • Do any easements or covenants restrict use of the property? What local land use regulations affect it?
  • Is there mandatory membership in a homeowners association?
  • What happens if a builder fails to deliver as promised, or when promised?
  • Exactly what are our rights and responsibilities under the proposed contract?

Contract review

Although many real estate agents provide printed “form contracts,” there is no such thing as a typical form contract. Each firm’s contract has its own provisions that affect the rights and obligations of both purchasers and sellers. The purchasers’ attorney reviews the offer and makes the changes that are in the purchasers’ best interests, or drafts a contract with the purchasers’ interests in mind.

Title examination

Home ownership is one of your most valued assets, and making certain the title to your property is clear of any encumbrance is an important task for your attorney. Old records are reviewed to make sure there is no cloud on the seller’s title: no forgeries or hidden liens making the property security for debt. Your attorney makes sure the sellers actually hold good title and have the right to transfer it, and explains any potential problems.

Settlement

Before settlement, the papers are carefully drafted and reviewed: the deed, the note, the deed of trust, the settlement statements, any releases of earlier deeds of trust, the truth in lending statement and the survey. At settlement the attorney explains the accounts among buyers, sellers, lender and agents. Afterward, the attorney records the documents among the land records and disburses the proceeds, within the time Virginia’s Wet Settlement Act requires.

The attorney’s charges for representing the purchasers are estimated in advance.
