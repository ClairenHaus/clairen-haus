Section 1031 exchanges

Exchanges of investment or business real estate, handled as part of the firm’s real estate practice.

What a 1031 exchange is

Section 1031 of the Internal Revenue Code generally allows an owner of real property held for investment or for use in a business to defer tax on the gain from selling it, by exchanging it for other like-kind real property instead of simply selling and buying.

Section 1031 exchanges are part of S. Page Allen’s real estate practice, alongside the residential closings that are its primary focus.

Why the details matter

Exchanges follow strict rules. Under the federal rules, replacement property generally has to be identified within 45 days of the sale and acquired within 180 days, and the sale proceeds generally may not pass through the seller’s own hands along the way. The contracts, the deeds and the timing at settlement all have to line up.

Tax treatment depends on your own circumstances. Talk with your attorney and your tax advisor before you sign a contract to sell property you hope to exchange.

Call the office to talk through the property you plan to sell or buy, the contracts involved, and how the exchange fits with your settlement.
