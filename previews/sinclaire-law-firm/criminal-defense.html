Charged with a crime in South Carolina

Jack Sinclaire represents clients charged in Federal, State, Magistrate or Municipal Courts. Before private practice he spent 17 years in the State prosecutor’s office, rising to Deputy Solicitor, where he prosecuted violent felony offenders and managed the Charleston County Solicitor’s Office.

Federal, State, Magistrate and Municipal Courts

Where a case is heard depends on the charge. In South Carolina, Magistrate and Municipal Courts handle traffic matters and less serious offenses, more serious state charges go to the Court of General Sessions, and federal charges are heard in the United States District Court. Each court has its own procedures and its own pace.

DUI and DUAC

Driving under the influence (DUI) and driving with an unlawful alcohol concentration (DUAC) are separate charges, and either can also affect your driver’s license. More on DUI and DUAC.

Criminal domestic violence

South Carolina grades criminal domestic violence by degree, from third degree up to domestic violence of a high and aggravated nature. A charge can bring bond conditions, such as no contact with the other person, from the very first appearance.

Drug cases

Drug charges range from simple possession to distribution and trafficking, in state or federal court. How evidence was gathered, including any search or traffic stop, is often a central question.

Probation violations and parole hearings

A probation violation is heard by a judge and can put a suspended sentence back on the table. Parole is decided by the parole board after a hearing. In both, preparation and presentation matter.

Expungements

An expungement removes an eligible charge from your public record. More on expungements.
