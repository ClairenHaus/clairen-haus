Deeds & Transfer on Death Deeds

Changing who owns real estate, and passing it on without probate.

From Angela: creating a deed to change the title costs less than taking the property through probate. She will quote her flat fee for a Deed if you ask by email.

Transfer on Death Deeds

Since August 2008, Minnesota law has allowed a Transfer on Death Deed, or TODD. It works like a “mini-will” for just the real estate it describes: ownership passes to the people you name only when you die.

  • The real estate avoids probate.
  • You can revoke a TODD for any reason.
  • You can sell the property without anyone else’s signature.
  • It doesn’t matter if the people you name have a divorce, a bankruptcy or a judgment against them, because they have no interest in the property until after your death.

Life Estate Deeds

A Life Estate Deed also avoids probate and can preserve some of the property’s value for the people who receive it. But it can only be revoked, or the property sold, with the notarized signatures of everyone who receives it and their spouses. And the property’s value is “on the table” if one of them divorces, files bankruptcy or owes money under a judgment.

Other deeds

A deed is also how a spouse’s name gets added to a title, how property changes hands in a sale, and how an estate passes land to heirs. The Minnesota Department of Commerce publishes free standard deed forms, and Angela can prepare or review a deed so it’s done right and recorded properly.

St. Louis County’s free online mapping site lets anyone look up parcels, deed lines and property tax information. You’ll find it on the resources page.
