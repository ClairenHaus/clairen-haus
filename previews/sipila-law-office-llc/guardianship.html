Guardianship & Conservatorship

When a person can no longer take care of themselves or their money, the court can appoint someone to help.

From Angela: a Power of Attorney and a Health Care Directive, signed in advance and properly used, can replace the need for a guardianship hearing. See Powers of Attorney.

What guardianship is

When a person can’t take care of himself or herself, the court can place that person under guardianship. The person is called a “ward,” and loses the legal ability to act on his or her own behalf. The guardian has the legal right and duty to care for the ward (Minn. Stat. 524.5-313). To be appointed, the proposed guardian petitions the court and proves that the ward can’t function safely and needs a guardian.

What conservatorship is

A conservatorship is over the “estate” of the person: all the money and property they own (Minn. Stat. 524.5-418). A petition often asks for a “Guardian and Conservator,” because the person usually needs both and one person can fill both roles.

Who serves

The court appoints the guardian or conservator based on the best interests of the ward or protected person, looking at any current guardian, kinship, and the person’s reasonable preference (Minn. Stat. 524.5-309). Guardians and conservators generally must pass a criminal history and background check (Minn. Stat. 524.5-118).

In Northeastern Minnesota, Lutheran Social Services Guardianship Options provides professional guardian and conservator services for a fee. When there is no free or low-cost family option, Angela recommends LSS, and she has worked for them.

What a guardian does

  • Decides where the ward lives
  • Provides for the ward’s care, comfort and everyday needs: food, clothing, shelter, health care, social and recreational needs
  • Takes reasonable care of the ward’s clothing, furniture, vehicles and other belongings
  • Consents to medical and other professional care
  • Approves or withholds approval of contracts the ward may want to make

If something isn’t right

A ward or family member can contact the county probate court to request a change in guardian or conservator. If the current guardian or conservator won’t agree to resign, the ward must show the court that they haven’t acted in the ward’s best interest, or that the ward no longer needs the services. That can take time and money.

Suspected abuse, neglect or financial exploitation of a vulnerable adult should be reported to the county’s Adult Protection services. A ward is considered a vulnerable adult.

After appointment

Guardians and conservators have ongoing duties to the court after they are appointed, and Angela can walk you through what those are for your situation. For people who want to serve as guardians, the Minnesota courts publish forms and a guardianship manual, and the National Guardianship Association offers training and a registered guardian program.
