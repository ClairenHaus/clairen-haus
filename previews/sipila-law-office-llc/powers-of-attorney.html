Powers of Attorney & Health Care Directives

Choose who can act for you on money and medical decisions, before anyone has to ask a court.

From Angela: these two documents are highly recommended for everyone over age 18. Properly used, a Power of Attorney and a Health Care Directive can replace the need for a court hearing.

Two separate documents

A Power of Attorney names someone to handle your finances. A Health Care Directive names someone to make health care decisions when you can’t make them yourself. They are separate, just as a guardian (over a person) and a conservator (over money and property) are separate roles in court.

Why plan ahead

Without them, a family may have to petition the court for a guardianship or conservatorship, which takes a hearing and costs far more. This matters most for people who can see they will need help, but incapacity can happen to any of us.

“Living Will” is now a Health Care Directive

The term “Living Will” has been replaced by “Health Care Directive.” A simple statement of whether you want to be kept on life support isn’t enough. Doctors feel safer following the decisions of a real person than the words of a document, especially on matters of life and death. A Health Care Directive, also called a Power of Attorney for Health Care, gives one or more people you choose the power to make those decisions for you.

Family Care Agreements

If one family member provides all or most of the care for another adult, any agreement to pay for that care should be in writing. This contract is called a Family Care Agreement. It can be one page: names, the hourly wage, expected hours per day, the tasks to be done, signed and dated.

It can feel awkward for a caregiver to ask an elderly parent for pay, but it settles whether the caregiver will be paid. Some parents assume the care is free and leave their estate equally to all their children; some caregivers assume they’ll inherit everything. Medical Assistance can also “pull back” money paid to a family member as a gift if the family can’t prove it was payment for care.
