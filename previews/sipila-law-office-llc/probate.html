Probate & Estate Settlement

Settling a loved one’s estate in Minnesota, with a written fee estimate up front.

From Angela: “As an attorney, I make my living handling probates, and I do have valuable insight as to how the process works. But I am also a do-it-yourself type of person who would want to avoid the expense of legal fees if I were a client.”

What probate is

Probate is the court process that settles the estate of a person who has died. A personal representative is appointed, gathers what the person owned, pays valid debts and expenses, and then passes what is left to the heirs or to the people named in the Will. In Minnesota, probate is handled in the district court of the county where the person lived.

A written fee estimate

Angela has a written fee estimate for a Probate that lists the time and cost of each task. It is the same estimate for every estate, with a few tasks, like the Final Account, shown as a range because they depend on how complex the estate is. Ask by email and she’ll send it.

Smaller estates may not need probate

Minnesota has an affidavit for collecting a small estate, which Angela’s site describes for estates worth under $75,000. Assets that pass automatically to a living person, through joint ownership, a beneficiary designation or a payable-on-death account, usually don’t count toward that limit. There are also court forms for making a claim against an estate and for opening a safe deposit box after the owner has died.

When there is no Will

Minnesota law decides who inherits. In general a surviving spouse is entitled to everything after debts are paid, but property titled only in the deceased person’s name may still have to go through probate to get the spouse’s name on the title. With no spouse and no children, the estate goes to the person’s parents. With children but no spouse, the children share equally. If no legal relative comes forward, the State of Minnesota receives the estate.

“I believe that people are less likely to steal and argue over property when there is a Will to guide them.”

Doing some of it yourself

The Minnesota courts publish free self-help probate forms. Angela works with them as needed and can review self-help forms for her clients at a fair price, so you can save money without risking mistakes that cost more later.

Planning ahead can keep an estate out of probate altogether. See Wills & Estate Planning and Transfer on Death Deeds.
