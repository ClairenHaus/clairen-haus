FAQ

Chapter 7 & Chapter 13 questions

Can I keep my house and still file Chapter 7?

Most likely, yes. The real answer depends entirely on your situation, but it is possible to keep your home while filing Chapter 7. If your circumstances don't qualify, Chapter 13 may be the best way to save your home.

What is credit counseling, and do I have to do it?

Credit counseling is a mandatory part of any bankruptcy filing, and it must be completed within the 180 days before your case is filed. A credit counselor may propose a plan to repay your debt outside of bankruptcy. You don't have to agree to the plan, but you must complete the course to be eligible for bankruptcy. If you have questions about a credit counseling plan, ask one of our attorneys.

How will bankruptcy affect my credit?

Bankruptcy will certainly give your credit rating an initial shock. A bankruptcy can stay on your credit report for ten years, and interest rates on new credit may be high. But most people considering bankruptcy are already having credit problems, and for them it can be a fresh start. By paying bills on time and budgeting carefully, many people improve their credit rating within a few years.

Are all of my debts discharged in bankruptcy?

Many will be, but not every debt is eligible for discharge. Contact one of our attorneys to find out whether your debts can be discharged.

What are bankruptcy petition preparers?

Bankruptcy petition preparers are non-attorney services some people use to file. They fill out the bankruptcy documents exactly the way you tell them to, and they are not allowed to give you legal advice. If a bankruptcy trustee believes a preparer gave you legal advice, your case can be thrown out. Their low cost can be attractive, but the service is typically not much help to you.

How long does a Chapter 7 case take?

After your first contact with us, it normally takes a few weeks to collect your documents and complete the paperwork. Once the case is filed with the bankruptcy court, it typically takes three to six months to complete. Several factors affect how long a case takes to reach discharge, and an attorney can go over them with you.

How long does a Chapter 13 case take?

Gathering the information takes a few weeks at the start. After that, a Chapter 13 repayment plan runs either three or five years, depending on your income.
