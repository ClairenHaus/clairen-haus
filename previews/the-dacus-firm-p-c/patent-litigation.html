Patent Litigation

Hundreds of patent cases in the Eastern District of Texas, for patent owners and for companies accused of infringement.

The Dacus Firm has represented clients in hundreds of patent cases in the Eastern District of Texas. We represent both patent owners and those accused of infringement, and we have served as both local and lead counsel.

We understand the intricacies of patent litigation, the local rules and the judiciary, and we use that knowledge to position our clients’ cases for resolution, if the client so chooses. But our firm is first and foremost a trial firm. Because of that, we have routinely taken a leading role in patent trials, while being an effective and productive part of what is often a larger team.

Who we represent

  • Patent owners asserting their patents. We have secured individual judgments for tens of millions of dollars for patent owners.
  • Companies accused of infringement. We have successfully defended accused infringers, including verdicts where juries found both “no infringement” and invalidity.

Local knowledge of the Eastern District

Deron Dacus has represented clients in well over 1,000 patent cases in Tyler, Marshall, Texarkana and Lufkin, and has tried dozens of patent cases to verdict. He has extensive knowledge of the local courts, the rules and the district’s patent rules, and is a charter member of the T. John Ward Inn of Court.

Ask about results, not just volume

All too often, litigants only ask prospective lawyers how many cases they have litigated or participated in, without asking whether the lawyer has had success. We believe the latter question is the more important one, and we would be happy to discuss our track record with you and how our firm might assist you.

How patent cases in the Eastern District generally move

In general terms, a patent case in federal court moves from the complaint and answer through local patent-rule disclosures, claim construction (where the court decides what disputed patent terms mean), fact and expert discovery, and dispositive motions before trial. The district’s local patent rules set many of these deadlines, which is one reason local counsel experience matters.
