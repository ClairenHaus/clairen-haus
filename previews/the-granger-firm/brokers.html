Representing real estate brokers

For brokers, representing consumers in real estate transactions can be a challenge. Negotiating agreements of sale, commission disputes and Real Estate Commission matters are among the most frequent issues.

Knowing who you represent

It can be challenging at times for brokers to know whom to represent in a sale and how to enforce their rights depending on the situation. Pennsylvania real estate law allows brokers to represent buyers, sellers, both, or none, but they must know the standards and obligations that come with each role.

The Firm’s attorneys are experienced in residential and commercial broker matters, and David J. Scaggs’ practice includes representing brokers and salespersons in claims of failure to disclose defects or breach of contract, and in disputes over commissions, competitors and broker/agent relationships.

Where broker disputes usually arise

Most broker matters fall into a few groups: claims by buyers or sellers that a defect was not disclosed, disputes over whether a commission was earned under a listing or buyer agreement, disputes between brokerages or between a broker and its agents, and proceedings before the State Real Estate Commission. Errors and omissions insurance often plays a part in how a claim is defended.
