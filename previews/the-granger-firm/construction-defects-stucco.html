Construction defects & stucco problems

The Granger Firm actively handles residential and commercial construction defect and failure-to-disclose cases in the courts of Pennsylvania, and has helped numerous clients with stucco issues, including purchasing, selling, remediation and recovery.

The stucco problem in Southeastern Pennsylvania

If you are from Southeastern Pennsylvania, you have likely heard about problems with stucco siding on residential homes. It is not just the stucco: insufficient weather-resistant barriers and improper flashing details can also let water into the building envelope. This kind of water intrusion can go on for years without the homeowner’s knowledge, only to be discovered after wood sheathing and framing have deteriorated and mold has grown inside the wall cavity.

  • Buying a stucco home: buyers should have a stucco inspection by a qualified inspector. Because you cannot see behind stucco, you will likely need the seller’s permission to probe it.
  • Selling one: Pennsylvania law requires disclosure of known stucco problems, and even a remediated home can be harder to sell without a well-documented repair report.
  • Recovering the loss: homeowners often learn of a stucco problem years later, sometimes after the builder is out of business, which makes recovery difficult.

Builders, warranties and timing

The Pennsylvania Supreme Court has held that the implied warranty of habitability given to buyers of newly constructed homes does not transfer to later purchasers (Conway v. Cutler Group, Inc.). Claims against sellers under the Seller Disclosure Law must be filed within two years of closing. Because the right claim and the right defendant depend on the facts, early advice matters.
