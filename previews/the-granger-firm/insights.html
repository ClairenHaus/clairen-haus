Cases and hot topics in Pennsylvania real estate

Summaries of recent decisions and recurring issues, drawn from the articles The Granger Firm publishes for clients and colleagues.

Firm news

A Firm case at the Pennsylvania Supreme Court

City of Philadelphia v. Galdo, 194 A.3d 1043 (Pa. 2019)

Frank Galdo owns a home in Philadelphia’s Fishtown section. Since 1989 he cleaned up, maintained and used a vacant lot across the street that the City had condemned in the 1970s while rerouting the Market-Frankford Elevated line for the construction of Interstate 95. When the City tried to take back the lot in 2013, Mr. Galdo, represented by David J. Scaggs, claimed ownership through adverse possession.

The trial court held the City immune. The Commonwealth Court vacated that decision, and the Pennsylvania Supreme Court affirmed: although the lot was acquired for a public use, the City had abandoned that use once construction was complete in the late 1970s, and holding the property for resale did not extend it. Back in the trial court, judgment was entered for Mr. Galdo for adverse possession of the entire parcel.

Important recent cases

Decisions worth knowing

Seller disclosure

Disclosure claims must be filed within two years of closing

Hollinger v. Dietrich (Pa. Super. 2026)

The Superior Court held, as a matter of first impression, that the Seller Disclosure Law contains a statute of repose: a claim under that law must be filed within two years of closing or it is lost, whether or not the buyer knew of the problem.

Seller disclosure

Psychological stigmas need not be disclosed

Milliken v. Jacono (Pa. 2014)

The Pennsylvania Supreme Court held that purely psychological stigmas, including those from a murder/suicide in a home, are not “material defects” that must be disclosed under the Seller Disclosure Law. Buyers should investigate any house, and its neighborhood, themselves.

New construction

No implied warranties to later purchasers

Conway v. Cutler Group, Inc. (Pa.)

The Pennsylvania Supreme Court held that the implied warranty of habitability received by buyers of newly constructed homes does not transfer to subsequent purchasers, which makes a claim against the original builder an unlikely remedy for a later owner.

Agreements of sale

A formal agreement of sale is not always required

Trowbridge v. McCaigue, 992 A.2d 199 (Pa. Super. 2010)

A signed document with a brief land description, the price, closing-cost terms and other basics was held binding, even though it said a formal agreement of sale would follow.

Commercial deposits

Return of a deposit when a commercial buyer walks away

Wawa, Inc. v. Insnetvest Corp. (C.P. Phila. 2010)

A dispute over a $150,000 escrow deposit on a $4,000,000 commercial purchase that depended on conditional use approval, decided under the terms of the agreement of sale.

Seller disclosure

Failure to disclose, fraud and sellers without knowledge

Growall v. Maietta, 931 A.2d 667 (Pa. Super. 2007)

A buyer who discovered basement leaks after closing sued under the Seller Disclosure Law and the consumer protection law; the case addresses what a seller must actually know to be liable.

Insurance

Insurer may appoint counsel under a reservation of rights

Eckman v. Erie Insurance Exchange (Pa. Super. 2011)

The Superior Court approved an insurer’s appointment of counsel of its choosing while defending under a reservation of rights.

Brokers

A listing agreement’s length cannot be changed orally

Michael Salove Co. v. Enrico Partners, L.P. (Pa. Super. 2011)

Under the Real Estate Licensing and Registration Act, the duration of a written exclusive listing agreement is a material term that cannot be extended by oral agreement.

Hot topics

Issues clients ask about

Construction

Stucco litigation and leaking homes

Improperly applied stucco, insufficient weather barriers and poor flashing can let water into a home for years before anyone notices. Buyers should get a qualified stucco inspection; sellers must disclose known problems. More on stucco.

Taxes

Transfer taxes

Pennsylvania imposes a 1% realty transfer tax, counties typically another 1%, some municipalities more, and Philadelphia 3% instead of 1%. Parties can agree who pays, but that agreement does not bind the taxing authorities.

Taxes

Transfer tax and assigning an agreement of sale

Buyers often assign an agreement of sale to a single-purpose LLC before closing. A Pennsylvania Department of Revenue bulletin calls into question whether such an assignment can itself trigger transfer tax, so any assignment should be reviewed by counsel first.

Litigation

Partnership disputes in troubled real estate deals

When a real estate venture fails, partners who each brought money, construction knowledge or sales experience often end up litigating who is to blame. These disputes typically center on a few recurring issues.
