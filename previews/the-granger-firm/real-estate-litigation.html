Real estate litigation, from the first review through appeal

Helping clients resolve disputes involving disclosure issues, boundary lines, title, contracts and landlord/tenant matters is the focus of The Granger Firm.

A firm built around real estate disputes

At times, the condition of a home or building, the ownership rights to property, or contractual issues involving the sale or construction of property become the focus of a dispute. Real estate itself is usually unique, and disputes involving real estate tend to have multiple parties with differing goals. Solving them often means resolving complex issues with individual assessments and strategies.

The Granger Firm handles residential and commercial real estate litigation throughout the state and federal courts of Pennsylvania, as well as before regulatory agencies and municipalities, from inception through trial and the appellate process. A significant part of the practice is title and boundary disputes, construction defect cases (including stucco litigation), failed real estate deals, disclosure cases and landlord/tenant disputes, including evictions.

Before anything is filed

Prior to putting any matter into litigation, the attorneys at The Granger Firm review with the client the strengths and weaknesses of the case, whether the cost of litigation makes sense, possible alternative resolutions, and whether mediation would be a reasonable avenue to resolve the case, both before and during litigation.

How real estate cases generally move in Pennsylvania

Most real estate disputes begin in a county Court of Common Pleas. Appeals go to the Superior Court or, for matters involving government bodies such as municipalities, the Commonwealth Court, with the Pennsylvania Supreme Court above both. Some disputes belong in federal court, and many can be resolved through mediation or arbitration. Which path fits a dispute depends on the parties, the property and the agreement involved.
