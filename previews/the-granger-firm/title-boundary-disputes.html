Title, boundary & adverse possession disputes

A significant part of the Firm’s practice is handling title and boundary disputes, including litigation on behalf of major title insurers for their insured owners and lenders.

Adverse possession

Adverse possession is a legal theory that allows a claimant to obtain title to land through actual, continuous, exclusive, visible, notorious, distinct and hostile possession for twenty-one years. In City of Philadelphia v. Galdo, 194 A.3d 1043 (Pa. 2019), David J. Scaggs represented a Fishtown homeowner who had cleaned up, maintained and used a vacant City-owned lot since 1989. The Pennsylvania Supreme Court held that the City had abandoned the lot’s public use and was not immune from the claim, and the trial court later granted the homeowner title to the parcel. Read the case summary.

Common title and boundary questions

Boundary and title problems usually surface at a sale, a refinance, a new survey or a neighbor’s project: a fence or driveway over the line, an easement nobody recorded, a judgment or lien in the chain of title, or a deed with an error. Remedies range from corrective deeds and negotiated agreements to actions to quiet title, ejectment and partition.
