Subdivision, land development & zoning

Subdivision, land development and zoning involve the division, improvement and use of land, which municipalities regulate through their ordinances.

Two decades of land use experience

The Granger Firm has over two decades of experience working with clients on these issues. The Firm has handled land use applications and appeals, negotiated regulatory matters, assisted in obtaining land development permits, obtained variances, special exceptions and conditional uses, and structured land development transactions throughout Pennsylvania, including Chester, Montgomery and Delaware Counties.

How approvals generally work

In most Pennsylvania municipalities, the Municipalities Planning Code divides the work. The zoning hearing board decides appeals, variances (relief from an ordinance’s requirements where a hardship exists) and special exceptions (uses the ordinance allows if stated criteria are met). The township or borough governing body decides conditional uses and approves subdivision and land development plans, usually after planning commission review. Knowing which body decides what, and on what record, shapes the whole application.

Legislative watch: accessory dwelling units

Pennsylvania is considering legislation that would require municipalities to allow at least one accessory dwelling unit (ADU) on a residential property by right, without the owner needing zoning relief. Municipalities would still be able to regulate ADUs, but by-right status would save homeowners the time and expense of a zoning application. Read the proposed legislation.
