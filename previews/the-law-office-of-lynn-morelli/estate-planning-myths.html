10 myths about estate planning

Common beliefs that leave families exposed, and what Massachusetts law actually says.

Myth 1You only have to do a Will once in your life.

Fact:Marriage and divorce automatically terminate a Will, and other life events often make an existing Will inadequate or inapplicable.

Myth 2I don’t need a Will because everything I own will automatically go to my spouse.

Fact:If you die without a Will, everything does not automatically go to your spouse. Your spouse must share a portion of your probate estate with your children, or with your next of kin.

Myth 3A “simple Will” is good enough to pass my estate to the people I want to receive it.

Fact:Many things can be drafted into your Will to make sure only the people you want receive your estate. An attorney who focuses on estate planning knows what questions to ask to tailor your Will to your goals and wishes.

Myth 4I don’t have a large estate, so probating it would be easy.

Fact:There is no such thing as an “easy” probate in Massachusetts. The process is complicated, time consuming and can require multiple court appearances. A properly drafted Will can reduce court appearances and court involvement, directly reducing attorney’s fees and court costs.

Myth 5No matter what I do, my estate will have to go through probate when I die.

Fact:Creating a trust during your lifetime can eliminate probate altogether.

Myth 6If my spouse and I die while our children are young, their grandparents or our relatives will raise them.

Fact:A Will is the only legally valid document in which you can name a guardian to raise your minor children. Without one, your minor children become wards of the state until the court approves a guardian, which could take a long time if no one suitable steps forward, or if too many people want to raise them.

Myth 7We’ve provided for our children with assets and life insurance in case we die while they are minors.

Fact:Neither parents nor guardians of a minor child are automatically the guardians of that child’s property, so they can’t use it for the child without lengthy court proceedings, and then court involvement each time they sell, invest or distribute it. A properly designed estate plan can lessen or eliminate that court involvement.

Myth 8If I’m incapacitated, my spouse and loved ones will pay my bills, monitor my investments and take care of everything.

Fact:They will have to go to court to become your guardian before they can handle your financial affairs, which is time consuming and expensive. A properly drafted power of attorney can eliminate that court involvement.

Myth 9If I’m incapacitated, my spouse and loved ones will make my health care decisions.

Fact:They may disagree or not know what you would want, and relatives, physicians, social workers and others may start costly court proceedings to resolve the conflict. A properly drafted health care proxy is the only legally valid document for naming someone to make health care decisions on your behalf.

Myth 10Death taxes are a thing of the past. They won’t be around when I die.

Fact:Massachusetts has its own estate tax, separate from the federal estate tax, and its exemption is lower than the federal one. With proper estate planning, this tax burden can be eased.

The bottom line: if you don’t have an estate plan, the government has one for you, and it is guaranteed that you’re not going to like it. Proper estate planning is not an option. It’s a necessity.
