Probate Administration

Guidance through the Massachusetts probate process, from the first filing to final settlement of the estate.

What probate does

When a person dies, property that doesn’t pass directly to others (such as jointly owned property and life insurance) must go through probate, supervised by the probate court where the person lived. Probate exists to:

  • Collect the decedent’s probate assets
  • Protect and preserve the property of the estate
  • Pay all debts and taxes
  • Determine who is entitled to the assets, and distribute them
  • Appoint guardians for minor children and their property, when needed

How long does probate take?

Final settlement of an estate can take up to two and a half years, and assets may be frozen for that time even when the family needs them. Disputes over property, guardianship, missing property or creditor claims can make it take longer.

How Lynn helps

Probating an estate in Massachusetts is complicated and time consuming, and can require several court appearances. Lynn handles the process from start to finish: preparing and filing the documents the probate court requires, preserving the estate’s assets throughout the proceedings, appearing in court and preparing the estate’s tax returns.

Can probate be avoided?

Yes. An estate plan that includes a living trust is designed to avoid the probate court. And a properly drafted Will can reduce court appearances and overall court involvement, lowering fees and court costs.
