Trusts

Keep control of your assets during your lifetime and spare your family the expense and delay of probate.

What is a trust?

A trust is a legal document in which a trustee holds title to property for the benefit of another person, the beneficiary. The trust obliges the trustee to manage its assets and income for the beneficiaries’ benefit. Revocable trusts (also called living trusts) can be changed or revoked by the person who creates them; irrevocable trusts cannot be changed.

Avoiding probate with a living trust

Settling an estate through the Probate Court can take anywhere from nine months to two and a half years, and brings court costs and legal fees. To avoid probate entirely, you can’t own assets in your name alone at death.

Some people try to get there by putting assets in joint names with children or relatives. That can backfire: a joint owner’s mortgage, business debts, creditors, divorce or death can put the property at risk.

The better approach is a funded living trust, also called a revocable or family trust, in which you serve as your own trustee. You keep control of your assets during your lifetime; the only change is that your accounts and property are titled in the trust’s name. At your death, the assets pass directly to the people you named, without Probate Court involvement, and the trust can hold assets for young beneficiaries until they reach a more mature age.

Trusts for other goals

  • Tax planning. For married couples with substantial assets, a living trust can be tailored to capture allowable estate tax benefits (often called credit shelter, QTIP or A-B trusts).
  • Special needs trusts for a family member with a disability.
  • Minor children. A trust can give guardians the funds to care for minor children and direct distributions to children beyond age eighteen.

Is a trust as formal as a Will to sign?

No. Under Massachusetts law, a trust is valid if signed by the creator and the trustee. A Will’s witness formalities don’t apply, which makes a trust easier to update over time.
