Wills

The most basic estate plan, and the right first step when no other planning has been done.

What a Will does

A Will lets you state clearly who you would like to receive your assets when you die. It is also the document in which you name a guardian for minor children and the person who will administer your Will through the probate court, known as the executor or executrix.

Writing a Will is usually not complicated, and once it’s done you can rest a little easier, knowing your wishes will be followed.

What happens if you die without a Will?

If you don’t make a Will, Massachusetts law decides what happens to your probate property. It is split between your spouse and children or, if you have no children, between your spouse and other relatives according to a set legal formula. If you have no spouse, children or other relatives to inherit, it goes to the state.

The limits of a Will

A Will must be allowed by the probate court before it can be enforced, which takes time and brings legal fees and costs. Probate can take a year or more, and during that time assets generally can’t be used or distributed without court or executor approval. Probate is also public: any “interested party” can see what you owned and what you owed.

If you want to avoid probate, minimize estate tax, or plan for minor children or children with special needs, a trust may be the better plan for you. Lynn will help you decide.

Can I change my Will?

Yes. Changing a Will requires signing an amendment, called a codicil, with the same witness and notary requirements as the original Will.
