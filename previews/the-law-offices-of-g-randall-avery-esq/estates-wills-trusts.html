Area of practice

Estates, wills & trusts

Planning ahead protects the people you care about. The firm prepares wills and trusts and helps families through the administration of estates.

Wills

A will states who receives your property, names the person who will manage your estate, and can name a guardian for minor children. Without one, Connecticut law decides how your property passes.

Trusts

A trust holds property for the benefit of others under instructions you set. Trusts are used for many reasons: to provide for children or grandchildren over time, to manage property for someone who needs help, or to make the transfer of assets more private and orderly.

Estates

When someone dies, their estate is usually settled through the Probate Court. The executor or administrator gathers assets, pays debts and taxes, and distributes what remains to the heirs or beneficiaries. The firm can guide executors and families through each step.

Plans that fit your family

Estate planning works best when your attorney understands the whole picture, including real estate and any family business. To begin, call (203) 202-2070 for a free consultation.
