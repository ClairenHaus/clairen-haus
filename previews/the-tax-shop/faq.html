FAQ

Questions we hear often

Straight answers about fees, e-filing, IRS letters and working with The Tax Shop.

Is the first meeting free?

Yes. Call us for a no-charge initial conference. We'll discuss your tax and financial situation and how we can help. If your affairs are simple, it may be a short meeting.

Do you charge extra to e-file my return?

No. Every return we prepare is filed electronically for free, and a Georgia return that goes with it is e-filed free as well.

Can your fee come out of my refund?

Yes. We can take our fees from your refund, so returns with a refund need no cash up front.

Can you prepare returns for other states?

Yes. We prepare returns for any state.

I got a letter from the IRS. What should I do?

Call us first. If any government agency contacts you about your taxes, we will take care of it so you can get on with your business. As an Enrolled Agent, Dennis Futch is authorized to represent you before the IRS.

What is an Enrolled Agent?

A tax professional licensed by the federal government who is authorized to represent taxpayers before the IRS. Enrolled Agents must prove their competence in taxation to the IRS and keep up with 72 hours of continuing education.

Can you help me get an ITIN?

Yes. The Tax Shop is an IRS-authorized ITIN (Form W-7) Acceptance Agent.

Do you work with businesses?

Yes. We serve individuals, corporations, limited liability companies, partnerships, estates, trusts and fiduciaries, with tax returns, bookkeeping, payroll, financial statements and business advice.

Do you sell investments?

No. We review your investment ideas with you and your advisor and explain the tax consequences, but we do not sell investments.

How long should I keep my tax records?

As a general rule, keep most records for six years after you file, and keep a copy of each year's return permanently. We can help you decide what to keep.

How do I check on a return you e-filed?

Clients can check the status of an electronically filed return online at taxinfonet.com, or just call us.
