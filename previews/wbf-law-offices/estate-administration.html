Help for the person left in charge of an estate or trust.

Guidance for executors and trustees, from the first inventory to the final distribution and returns.

What estate administration involves

When someone dies, the person named as executor of the will, or as successor trustee of a trust, becomes responsible for gathering the assets, paying valid debts and expenses, filing the required tax returns, and distributing what remains to the beneficiaries. The work can take months, and it carries real legal duties.

The usual steps

  • Locating the documents: the will, any trust, beneficiary designations and recent tax returns.
  • Probate, if it is needed: assets in the deceased person’s own name usually pass through the circuit court in the county where they lived. Assets held in a trust or passing by beneficiary designation generally do not.
  • Inventory and valuation of what the person owned on the date of death.
  • Notice to creditors and payment of valid claims and expenses.
  • Tax returns: the final individual income tax return, income tax returns for the estate or trust, and federal or Illinois estate tax returns when the estate is large enough to require them.
  • Accounting and distribution to the beneficiaries.

Why the tax side matters here too

Decisions made during administration, such as how assets are valued, when they are sold and whether certain elections are made, can affect the taxes the estate and its beneficiaries pay. Estate administration and estate tax analysis are both part of Mr. Fox’s practice.

This page explains the topic in general terms. It is not legal advice, and the right approach depends on your circumstances and on the law at the time you plan.
