What an estate plan does

An estate plan is the set of documents that says who receives your property, who is in charge of carrying out your wishes, and who can act for you if you can’t act for yourself. A good plan also looks at how your assets are owned and who is named as beneficiary on accounts and policies, because those designations pass outside a will.

Documents a plan often includes

  • A will, naming an executor and, for young children, a guardian.
  • A revocable living trust, which can hold assets during your life and pass them to your beneficiaries without a court-supervised probate.
  • A durable power of attorney for property, so someone you trust can handle financial matters if you are unable to.
  • A power of attorney for health care, naming the person who makes medical decisions when you cannot.
  • Beneficiary designations for retirement accounts and life insurance that match the rest of the plan.

Where tax planning comes in

For some families, the plan also has to account for federal estate and gift tax, Illinois estate tax, and income tax on what beneficiaries inherit. That is where tools such as lifetime gifts, irrevocable trusts, life insurance trusts and GRATs come in. Mr. Fox’s practice covers each of these, together with the estate planning itself.

Keeping the plan current

Plans are written for a moment in time. Marriages, births, deaths, moves, the sale of a business and changes in the tax law can all mean a plan needs a fresh look.

This page explains the topic in general terms. It is not legal advice, and the right approach depends on your circumstances and on the law at the time you plan.
