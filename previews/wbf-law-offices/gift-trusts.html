Gift trusts where you keep paying the income tax, on purpose.

Irrevocable trusts that hold gifted assets outside your estate while you pay the trust’s income tax.

What a “grantor” type gift trust is

A grantor trust is a trust whose income is taxed to the person who created it (the grantor) rather than to the trust or its beneficiaries. A “grantor” type gift trust is an irrevocable trust set up so that the gifts you make to it are generally treated as completed for gift and estate tax purposes, while the trust remains a grantor trust for income tax purposes.

Why that combination is used

  • The assets are out of your estate. Property given to the trust, and its future growth, is generally not part of your taxable estate.
  • You pay the income tax. Because the trust’s income is taxed to you, the trust can grow without paying its own income tax. Paying that tax is not treated as an additional gift.
  • Flexibility. Because the trust is ignored for income tax purposes, some transactions between you and the trust, such as a sale of assets to it, generally do not trigger income tax.

Things to weigh

The trust is irrevocable, so the gifts can’t simply be taken back. Paying the trust’s income tax each year has to fit your own cash flow. And assets given away during life generally do not receive the step-up in income tax basis that inherited assets can. These trade-offs are part of the planning conversation.

This page explains the topic in general terms. It is not legal advice, and the right approach depends on your circumstances and on the law at the time you plan.
