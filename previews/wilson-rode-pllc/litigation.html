Civil Litigation

Representation that is both economical and responsive to each client's individual needs. Patrick Rode heads the firm's litigation practice.

Civil litigation is the process of resolving a non-criminal dispute, such as a broken contract or a disagreement between business owners, through negotiation, arbitration or the courts.

How we approach a dispute

Litigation, like any other business activity, requires careful planning. In undertaking a matter, we give clients a complete and honest assessment of their case so they can make an informed decision on how to proceed. We identify their objectives early and work closely with them to meet those goals, pursuing strategies narrowly focused on the best possible result in an expeditious and cost-effective manner.

Matters we handle

We regularly represent individuals, corporations, partnerships, limited liability companies and non-profit entities in:

  • Contract disputes
  • Unfair competition litigation
  • Restrictive covenant enforcement
  • Uniform Commercial Code matters
  • Shareholder actions
  • Disputes arising from mergers and acquisitions
  • Real estate and commercial lease disputes
  • Tax and probate matters
  • General commercial matters of all sorts

Resolving disputes early, trying them when needed

Court battles can be time-consuming, emotionally stressful, expensive and disruptive, and judicious negotiation and counseling can often prevent a lawsuit from happening. We make all appropriate efforts to resolve disputes at the earliest possible stage, with the least possible expense and risk, and counsel clients on strategies that help them avoid litigation altogether.

At the same time, if a case must be tried, the firm has the trial skills and experience necessary to pursue it. We are available to try cases in state and federal courts throughout Michigan, and in arbitration and select federal courts throughout the country.

Fees, discussed in advance

In each case we discuss billing arrangements in advance (hourly, fixed fee, contingent fee, or a combination) to see what is best suited to the case and the client.
