Living Trusts

A living trust is a contract for the private administration of your estate. Unlike a will, it avoids probate and conservatorship, but only for assets titled in the name of the trust.

Why a trust instead of a will

A will guarantees probate: a long, expensive and public court process. A will also usually does not control all of your property and does no disability planning. A properly drafted revocable living trust avoids probate and conservatorship, plans for disability, gives you better control of your assets, and keeps your affairs private.

  • Avoids probate and court-supervised conservatorship
  • Plans for your own possible disability, with instructions for your disability trustee
  • Lets you decide who receives what, and when and how they receive it
  • Can include tax planning without going through probate
  • Stays private, unlike a probated will

A trust only works if it is funded

The firm is direct about this: unless you transfer your assets to your trust, by changing title or beneficiary designations, your estate plan will not work. An unfunded trust usually ends up in probate anyway. That is why Mike recommends that the firm handle the transfer of title to your real property and guides you through the right process for each other asset.

Keeping the trust current

Your life, the law and planning techniques all change. The firm's annual maintenance programs exist so your trust keeps doing what you designed it to do. See how the process works.
