Estate Planning

Wills, trusts and the documents that protect your family, prepared for your situation rather than copied from a form.

What the firm prepares

  • Simple and trust wills
  • Prenuptial and postnuptial agreements
  • Financial (durable) and healthcare powers of attorney (living wills)
  • Home visits
  • Deed transfers

After the free initial consultation, Yardley Legal Services will recommend which estate planning documents you need, and the legal fee.

Why a form from the internet is a risk

Many people try to save money by buying boilerplate estate planning documents online. Every estate plan should be tailored to the specific needs of the family. For example, a standard trust should not be used for a beneficiary who is disabled or receives public benefits. If the documents are not prepared correctly, the beneficiary could lose those benefits, or the inheritance may have to be paid to the government.

Two things most families don’t know

Most married couples are not aware that if one spouse dies without a will, the surviving spouse does not automatically inherit the whole estate: the children receive a statutory share of the deceased spouse’s estate.

Retirement accounts and other payable-on-death accounts are not controlled by a will. Their beneficiary designations must be coordinated with the rest of the plan to get the distribution you want.

Home visits

Home visits are available for estate planning. If you are disabled, an appointment can be scheduled at your residence.
