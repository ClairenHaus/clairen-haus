What is actually wrong
Critical

40.6 MB page weight, likely from auto-playing drone footage

Your homepage weighs 40.6 megabytes. That is over 20 times the size of a properly built site. The most likely cause is uncompressed video content loading automatically on every page visit. Every visitor, whether they are on WiFi or cellular data, is forced to download over 40 MB before they can interact with your site. On a typical mobile connection, that takes 15-20 seconds. Many visitors on limited data plans will see their browser stall entirely.

Critical

23.3 seconds to become interactive

Time to Interactive measures when a visitor can actually use your site. At 23.3 seconds, your phone number, "Get a Quote" button, and contact form are visible but do not respond to taps for nearly half a minute. The industry benchmark is under 3.8 seconds. You are six times over.

Critical

Cumulative Layout Shift: 0.154 (content jumping on screen)

As your page loads, elements shift and jump around the screen. A CLS score above 0.1 is rated poor by Google. Yours is 0.154. This means a visitor trying to tap a button may accidentally tap something else because the layout shifted at the moment they reached for it. It is also a Google ranking factor. Sites with high CLS get penalized in search results.

Important

Links are not crawlable

Some links on your site cannot be followed by Google's crawler. This means pages that should be indexed and showing up in search results may be invisible to Google entirely. For a company with 497 reviews and FORTIFIED Roof certification, every page should be discoverable.

Important

Best Practices score: 73

Your Best Practices score is the lowest in this category across our analysis. This reflects issues with how the site handles browser APIs, security headers, and modern web standards. It signals to both Google and visitors that the site has not been maintained to current standards.

Important

Contrast ratio failures

Text on several sections of your site does not meet minimum contrast requirements. This makes content difficult to read for visitors with visual impairments and on screens in direct sunlight, which is common for homeowners inspecting roof damage from their driveway.
