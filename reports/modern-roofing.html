What is dragging your score down
Critical

Google scores your mobile performance at 40 out of 100

Google uses this score as a ranking signal. When a homeowner searches "roofing company near me" or "roof replacement Marietta," Google evaluates which sites to place first. Sites scoring in the "Poor" range (below 50) are at a disadvantage. For a company expanding into South Carolina and North Carolina, every new market will evaluate your site against local competitors who may score 80+. Your SEO content score is a perfect 100. Your site performance is what is holding back your placement.

Critical

8.1 MB page weight, almost entirely unoptimized images

Your homepage weighs 8.1 megabytes. Google's top audit flag for your site is "Improve image delivery." Your project photos are high quality, but they are being served at full resolution to every device. Properly compressing, resizing, and lazy-loading these images would cut page weight by 70-80% without any visible quality loss, and is the single fastest path to a higher score.

Critical

758ms of main thread blocking

Heavy JavaScript blocks the browser's main thread for over three-quarters of a second at a time. Google measures this as Total Blocking Time and factors it directly into the performance score. At 758ms, this is the second-largest contributor to your low score after image weight.

Important

Unused CSS and JavaScript

The site loads stylesheets and scripts that are not used on the homepage. Google flags this as wasted bytes that increase page weight and delay rendering. Removing or deferring this unused code would improve the score immediately.

Important

No main landmark for accessibility

The site is missing a main content landmark, which contributes to the accessibility score of 85. Google evaluates accessibility as part of its overall site quality assessment. This is a straightforward HTML fix.

Important

Browser console errors

Google flagged JavaScript errors logged to the browser console. These indicate code that is failing silently on the page, which can affect functionality and contributes to a lower best practices assessment.
